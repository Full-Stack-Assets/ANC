# Fragments Batch F05 — Surname Fragments H–L (Generations 7–12)

## Overview

This batch covers 40 assorted direct ancestors (generations 7–12) drawn from small
surname fragments: Hunt/Hunter/Hutton/Hibbard, Ingersoll, Innesse/Irving, Johansdotter/
Johnson/Jönsson, Jorden/Jordon, Jackson/Jacobsson/Jenkins, Josias Bull, Juxon/Biggs,
Kennedy, Kelly, Kettle/Kendall, Kimball, Kristoffersson, Lamberd/Lambert. Most entries
are unsourced tree fragments (a handful carry an "Ancestry Family Trees" or "Geneanet"
citation, which is compiled/secondary, not primary). Per triage scope, all 40 got a
quick internal-consistency read; the 8 most findable/notable were checked against
external sources (WikiTree, FamilySearch, Geni, Wikipedia, local/family histories).

**Overall read:** the well-documented colonial New England lines (Ingersoll of Salem/
Gloucester, Hunt of Westchester, Kimball of Rattlesden) hold up well against outside
sources. Several entries show classic tree-compilation problems: names strung together
from multiple conflicting sources (e.g., "Mary Flowers Kettle Kendall Kedell," "Sarah
Anne Juxon Marshe Biggs"), an internally-hedged date/place ("1729?", "Worcestershire,
England?"), a titled Scottish marriage that doesn't match the documented holder of that
title, and one likely genealogical anachronism (a "Quaker" ancestor born in 1564,
decades before Quakerism existed).

## Researched entries (8)

### Joshua Hunt Sr. (I182381537788), 1680–1762 — VERIFIED

WikiTree's Joshua Hunt (Hunt-17003) records birth "7 Oct 1680 ... West Farms,
Westchester, Colony of New York" and death "26 Mar 1762 ... White Plains, Westchester
County" — an exact match to the tree's dates and places. His will (dated 24 Jul 1760,
proved 25 Mar 1762) names wife Mercy and sons including **Caleb** — matching the tree's
child "Caleb Hunt" and spouse "Mercy Oakley - Fowler." One WikiTree source gives his
parents as John Hunt and Grace Fowler, and his wife as Mercy Oakley, daughter of Miles
Oakley and Mercy Gardiner (consistent with the tree's compound surname "Oakley -
Fowler," which appears to conflate the wife's maiden name with a family-history
note about the Fowler line). This is one of the strongest matches in the batch.

### Caleb Hunt (I182381537754), 1710–1769 — VERIFIED (derivative)

Confirmed as a documented son of Joshua Hunt Sr. per the same WikiTree/will record
above. Independent Caleb-specific vital records were not separately pulled in this
triage pass.

### SAMUEL LUNT INGERSOLL (I182620337704), 1654–1733 — VERIFIED

Multiple compiled genealogies (Pane-Joyce/Clark University "Ingersoll" report,
Angelfire Ingersoll genealogy, MyHeritage) agree Samuel Ingersoll was born c.1654 in
Gloucester, Essex Co., MA, was a shipwright, and married **Judith Maddiver**, daughter
of Joel Maddiver and Rebecca — matching the tree's spouse "JUDITH MADIVER" (a common
period spelling variant of Maddiver/Madiver). Sources split on death: some give Dec
1732, others 1733 in Gloucester — the tree itself carries both variants as separate
unsourced events, which is consistent with the real record's ambiguity rather than an
error unique to this tree.

### George Ingersoll Lt (I182620337728), 1618–1694 — VERIFIED, with clarification

WikiTree (Ingersoll-60), FamilySearch, and Geni agree George Ingersoll was born 1618,
came to New England as a child with his father Richard Ingersoll during the Great
Migration, received a Salem land grant in 1638, moved to Gloucester by 1646 (selectman
1652, kept an ordinary), returned to Salem c.1675, then to Falmouth/Stroudwater by
1680, and wrote his will c.22 Jun 1694 — matching the tree's dates and general outline
closely.

**On the "Mayflower" note:** the tree's parent-note for Richard Ingersoll says
"arrived 1629 Mayflower." This reads at first as a claim to the famous 1620 Pilgrim
ship, which would be wrong — but period correspondence (a 1629 Massachusetts Bay
Company letter from Matthew Craddock to Governor Endecott) does list "Richard
Indersall" among emigrants sent in 1629, and the 1629 Higginson Fleet to Salem
genuinely included a **second, separate ship also named Mayflower** (distinct from the
1620 Pilgrim vessel). So the tree's note is not wrong, but it is easily misread as a
claim of Pilgrim/Mayflower Compact descent — worth a clarifying annotation rather than
a correction.

### George Holland Jenkins (I182195863385), 1785–1836 — VERIFIED, with a date correction

A Jenkins-family genealogy blog ("Doug's Genealogy: Nicholas Jenkins and the Island
Census of 1798") and a WikiTree profile for his father, Nicolaus (Henckell) Jenkins
(1755 Treysa, Hessen, Germany – 1823), confirm the family: Nicolaus was a Hessian
soldier (Knyphausen regiment) who arrived on PEI in 1779, settled at Vernon River, and
whose son anglicized the surname Henckell to Jenkins — matching the tree's oddly
compound parent name "Nicolaus Nicholas Nicolai Henckell Jenkins."

**Correction:** the same source gives George Holland Jenkins's birth as **c.1795**
("born ca. 1795 in PEI; died 30 Aug 1836, age 41 years"), not 1785 as in the tree — the
stated age-at-death (41) is only consistent with a birth around 1795. The death date
also differs slightly (30 Aug vs. the tree's 23 Aug 1836); both are close enough that
this is likely a transcription variance rather than a different event, but the ~10-year
birth-year discrepancy should be corrected.

### Peter Josias Bull (I182567600278), 1619–1680 — VERIFIED, with a parent/label correction

Compiled genealogies (Ancestry, WikiTree's Bull genealogy pages) consistently give
Peter Josias Bull's birth as Wolverhampton, Staffordshire, 1619 (one variant: 1615),
death 8 Aug 1680 in Wolverhampton — matching the tree — and his wife as **Ellenor/
Eleanor Simson**, matching "Eleanor Simson" in the tree.

**Correction:** his parents are consistently given as **William Bull and Elizabeth
Worthington**, not "Sir WILLIAM Knight Bull (Quaker)" and "Elisabeth BULL" as the tree
states. WikiTree's William Bull entry for this family gives dates of 1564–1635 for the
father — decades before the Quaker movement existed (founded by George Fox in the early
1650s), so the "(Quaker)" descriptor is very likely a later, anachronistic embellishment
rather than a documented fact. Recommend dropping "Sir," "Knight," and "(Quaker)" absent
a specific citation, and correcting the mother's name to Elizabeth Worthington.

### Jeane Innesse (I182381537276), 1627–1683 — SUSPECT

The tree names her spouse as "David Lindsay, 9th Earl of Crawford." Wikipedia's article
on David Lindsay, 9th Earl of Crawford documents two wives — Janet Gray and Katherine
Campbell — with no mention of a Jean/Jeane Innes(se), and that Earl's life dates place
him in the 16th century, well before 1627–1683. A separate, later "David Lindsay"
(1636–1698, Edzell Castle, Angus) is associated with a wife named Jean Innes in some
secondary genealogy sites, but the Crawford earldom's numbering is historically
contested (multiple lines disputed the title), so it is unclear which "9th Earl"
(if any) actually married a Jean/Jeane Innes(se). This looks like a case of a common
secondary-source genealogy conflating two different Lindsay generations/title-holders.
Flagged as suspect pending a documented peerage source (e.g., a proper Scots Peerage
citation).

### Sarah Anne Juxon Marshe Biggs (I182730910290), 1581–1646 — SUSPECT (likely conflated identity)

The tree gives her as wife of "Richard William Biggs III," married 1615 in
Southampton, dying 1646 in Jamestown. Period Jamestown sources (WikiTree's Biggs-87,
the 1624/25 Virginia Muster, and multiple Biggs-family genealogies) show **two separate
women** associated with a Richard Biggs of early Virginia: an "Anne Juxon," and a wife
named "Sarah" (age 35 in the 1624 muster, having immigrated in May 1618 on the
*Marygold* while Richard arrived in 1610 on the *Swann*). The tree's compound name
"Sarah Anne Juxon Marshe" strings together what appear to be at least two distinct
identities (Sarah + Anne Juxon, plus an unexplained "Marshe") into a single person —
a classic symptom of merged/conflated Ancestry-tree records. It's also date-inconsistent
internally: a 1581 birth year would make her 43 at the 1624 muster, not the 35 recorded
there. Flagged as suspect; the "John The Immigrant Biggs / Richard Biggs / Thomas W
Biggs Sr / William Biggs" children may be real, but the mother's identity as given
needs re-verification against primary muster/parish records rather than the Ancestry
Family Trees citation currently attached.

### Abigail Ursula Kimball (I182541956088), 1617–1658 — VERIFIED

FamilySearch, WikiTree (Kimball-184), and Geni agree: Abigail Kimball was born
5 Nov 1617 in Rattlesden, Suffolk, England, daughter of Richard Kimball (the
Rattlesden/Ipswich emigrant — part of the well-documented 1634 *Elizabeth* passenger
list), married John Severance c.1633–1636, and died 17 Jun 1658 in Salisbury,
Massachusetts (Essex Co., not the "Norfolk" county given in one secondary source, which
appears to be that source's own error) — matching the tree's dates, spouse name, and
place closely. This is one of the best-attested entries in the batch, consistent with
its "Ancestry Family Trees" citation.

## Corrections to the tree

- **George Holland Jenkins birth year**: tree says 1785 → likely **c.1795**, per
  age-at-death (41) recorded in Jenkins-family genealogy sources. (Death date 23 Aug
  vs. sourced 30 Aug 1836 — minor, likely transcription variance, not corrected here.)
- **Peter Josias Bull's parents**: tree says "Sir WILLIAM Knight Bull (Quaker)" +
  "Elisabeth BULL" → compiled sources consistently give **William Bull** (no title) and
  **Elizabeth Worthington**; the "Quaker" label is very likely anachronistic (his father's
  documented dates, 1564–1635, predate the Quaker movement by decades).
- **Sarah Anne Juxon Marshe Biggs**: the compound name likely conflates at least two
  distinct women (a "Sarah," age 35 in the 1624 Virginia muster, and a separate "Anne
  Juxon") associated with a Richard Biggs of early Virginia. Recommend flagging this
  identity as unresolved rather than treating it as one documented person.
- **Jeane Innesse's spouse title**: "David Lindsay, 9th Earl of Crawford" does not match
  the Wikipedia-documented holder of that title (16th century, different wives). If a
  real David Lindsay–Jean Innes marriage exists, it likely belongs to a different,
  later Lindsay of Edzell, and the "9th Earl of Crawford" label should be checked
  against a peerage reference rather than repeated as-is.
- **Thomas Holt Jackson's children list**: "Elizabeth Jackson" appears twice among his
  eight listed children — almost certainly a duplicate/merge error in the source tree,
  not two distinct daughters with the identical name.

## New findings (facts not in the tree)

- Samuel Lunt Ingersoll's occupation: **shipwright** (per compiled Ingersoll
  genealogies), not noted in the tree.
- Judith Maddiver's parentage: daughter of **Joel Maddiver and Rebecca** (per compiled
  sources) — the tree gives no parents for her.
- Joshua Hunt Sr.'s parents: **John Hunt and Grace Fowler** (per WikiTree) — the tree's
  fragment for Joshua Hunt Sr. lists no parents at all.
- Joshua Hunt Sr.'s will (dated 24 Jul 1760, proved 25 Mar 1762) additionally names
  sons Joshua, Israel, and Enos/Enoch, and daughters Miriam (Oakley) and Mary — full
  sibling set for Caleb Hunt not reflected in the tree fragment.
- George Holland Jenkins's father Nicolaus Henckell Jenkins was a **Hessian soldier**
  in the Knyphausen regiment who first arrived on Prince Edward Island in 1779 (during
  the American Revolutionary War) before later resettling there permanently — background
  not present in the tree fragment.
- The Richard Ingersoll 1629 emigration is corroborated by a **primary period letter**:
  Matthew Craddock (Massachusetts Bay Company) to Governor John Endecott, dated
  28 May/3 Jun 1629, naming "Richard Indersall" among Bedfordshire men hired by the
  Company — a genuinely strong primary citation available if this line is written up
  more formally.

## Open questions

- Which primary source (parish register, will, muster) actually documents "Sarah Anne
  Juxon Marshe" as a single individual, versus the tree having merged two or more
  Biggs-family women? Needs a look at the original Jamestown muster and any surviving
  Biggs will.
- Is there a genuine David Lindsay–Jean Innes marriage on record, and if so, which
  Lindsay generation/title (not the documented 9th Earl of Crawford) does it belong to?
  A Scots Peerage or Balfour Paul citation would resolve this.
- Robert Kennedy (1704–1804, Quinebaug CT → Norwich CT) is recorded with a 100-year
  lifespan — plausible but unusually long for the period; worth checking a probate or
  gravestone record to confirm both endpoint dates belong to the same person.
- Several Swedish-farm entries (Malin Johansdotter, Cajsa Johansdotter, Per Jonsson,
  Germund Jonsson Hurtig, Johannes Jönsson, Jakob Kristoffersson) were only checked for
  internal place-name plausibility, not verified against Swedish church books (this
  would require ArkivDigital/Riksarkivet access, out of scope for this triage pass).
- No independent check was made of "Mary Flowers Kettle Kendall Kedell" — worth
  resolving which of Flowers/Kettle/Kendall/Kedell is her actual birth surname, since
  her husband's presumed real surname (per child "Eleazer S Whitney") is likely
  **Whitney**, not "Whetny" as recorded for the spouse event.
