# The Cheesman Line (Line 6) — Generations 8–12

## Overview

This is a colonial Long Island → West New Jersey lineage: Ephraim (Efraime) Cheeseman of Kingston‑upon‑Thames, Surrey, England (gen. 12, b. 1610) is the deepest generation in the tree; his son **Thomas Cheesman (gen. 11, b. 1640, d. 1713)** is the immigrant ancestor who crossed to Long Island, New York. The line then runs Thomas → Richard Cheesman Sr. (gen. 10) → Peter C. Cheesman (gen. 9), who relocated the family from Hempstead, Long Island to a plantation in Gloucester County, New Jersey, → Mary Ann Cheesman (gen. 8, b. 1746), who married into the Jackson family and reportedly ended her life in Ohio. Solid, source‑citing compiled genealogy (WikiTree, drawing on the "Long Island Surnames" database and colonial NJ probate abstracts) covers Ephraim through Peter reliably; the documented paper trail effectively **ends at Peter Cheesman (gen. 9)** — Mary Ann's own vital events and marriage are unsourced in every source checked, including the tree.

**Where the well-documented chain ends:** Ephraim Cheeseman → Thomas Cheesman → Richard Cheesman Sr. → Peter Cheesman is corroborated across independent compiled genealogies with underlying citations (parish-register marriage index, town land records, probate). The link from Peter Cheesman down to Mary Ann Cheesman, and everything about Mary Ann herself, is **not documented** — it rests on Ancestry Family Trees only, both in this tree and in the wider online record.

## Verified facts

- Ephraim (Efraime) Cheeseman married Ann Rason on 18 Jul 1639 in Kingston-upon-Thames, Surrey, England — FamilySearch "England Marriages, 1538–1973" index citing Kingston-upon-Thames parish register, FHL microfilm 991,683. [WikiTree Cheeseman-215]
- Ephraim Cheeseman died about 27 Jan 1657 in Kingston, Surrey, England (WikiTree marks this date "uncertain"). [WikiTree Cheeseman-215]
- Thomas Cheesman (b. c.1640, Kingston-upon-Thames) was established in Hempstead, Long Island, NY by 1680, named in town records as son-in-law of Richard Valentine and occupying Valentine's farm — the emigration point of this line from England to America. [WikiTree Cheesman-22 ancestor chart; Valentine genealogy discussion]
- Richard Cheesman Sr. was born 9 Nov 1674 at Hempstead, Queens Co. (now Nassau Co.), Long Island, NY, son of Thomas Cheesman and Mary Margaret Valentine. [WikiTree Cheesman-22, citing Long Island Surnames]
- Richard Cheesman married Hannah **Stringham** (not Halstead — see Corrections) on 10 Jan 1694 at St. George's Church, Hempstead, L.I. [WikiTree Cheesman-22, citing Long Island Surnames]
- Richard Cheesman purchased plantation land in Gloucester County, NJ in 1720 (£145) and an additional 93 acres in 1726 from Lancelot Brown — the move that brought the family from Long Island to New Jersey. [genealogieonline.nl "From Castles to America"; WikiTree]
- Richard Cheesman died 2 Sep 1744 at his plantation, Waterford Twp., Gloucester Co., NJ; his will (dated 19 Mar 1743/4, proved 7 Sep 1744) names wife Hannah and sons Thomas, Benjamin, Richard, William, and Peter. [Calendar of Wills, Administrations, Etc. 1730–1750, Documents Relating to the Colonial History of the State of New Jersey, vol. 30, p. 92, cited on WikiTree Cheesman-22]
- Peter Cheesman was born 16 Jun 1704 at Hempstead, Queens Co., Long Island, NY and died 14 Jun 1788, age 83, at Upton, Burlington Co., NJ (both dates match the tree exactly). [WikiTree Cheesman-112, citing Long Island Surnames]
- Peter Cheesman was a miller and built a stone gristmill near Turnersville (Washington Twp.), Gloucester Co., NJ before 1800; he bought 350 acres from Henry Roe on 22 Aug 1753 and sold the combined 369½-acre tract to John Kay in 1771. [History of the Counties of Gloucester, Salem and Cumberland, NJ (Cushing & Sheppard, 1883), transcribed at genealogytrails.com; West Jersey History Project, Farr's "Watermills of Camden County"]
- Peter Cheesman married more than once: Ann Hanna Ellis (1732, Upton, Burlington Co., NJ), Martha (Taber) Hedger (date not fixed on WikiTree), and Christian (Fisher) Cheesman (5 Feb 1760, Gloucester Co., NJ). The tree's stated marriage to "Martha Taber Hedger" between 1746–1748 is consistent with one of these documented unions but is not itself independently sourced. [WikiTree Cheesman-112]

## Corrections to the tree

- **Richard Cheesman Sr.'s wife: "Hannah Halstead" → Hannah Stringham.** The tree's event line reads "spouse: Hannah Halstead," but the sourced WikiTree biography (drawing on the Long Island Surnames database and Richard's 1744 will, which names his widow "Hannah") gives her surname as **Stringham**, daughter of Peter Stringham and Anne Barker of Hempstead/Jamaica, NY. Notably, WikiTree's own Family/Ancestors panel for Richard still carries a leftover, uncited "Husband of Hannah Halstead — married 10 Jan 1694" entry alongside the sourced "Husband of Hannah (Stringham) Cheesman" entry — i.e., this tree's "Halstead" almost certainly comes from that same uncorrected duplicate spouse record propagating through Ancestry-sourced trees, not from an independent finding.
- **Duplicate person records for Richard Cheesman.** IDs `I182625986316` ("Richard Cheesman Sr") and `I182195863536` ("Richard Cheesman") are the same individual, unmerged: identical birth (9 Nov 1674) and death (2 Sep 1744) dates, identical parents (Thomas Cheesman + Mary Margaret Valentine), and the same single listed child (Peter C Cheesman/Peter C Cheeseman). These two nodes should be merged.
- **Peter Cheesman's birthplace.** The tree gives Peter's birth as "16 Jun 1704 @ Old Upton, Gloucester, New Jersey," but every external source (WikiTree, Long Island Surnames) places his birth at Hempstead, Long Island, NY — consistent with the fact that his father Richard did not buy the NJ plantation until 1720, sixteen years after Peter's birth. The tree appears to have conflated Peter's later New Jersey residence/death place with his birthplace.
- **"Long Island City, Queens" as a birthplace for Richard Cheesman (1674).** This is very likely a geocoding artifact: Long Island City is a 19th-century-founded neighborhood name; the historically correct place for an 1674 birth is Hempstead (then Queens County, now Nassau County). The tree's alternate event line "birth: 1676 @ Gloucs, New Jersey" is even further off and should be discarded — the family was still on Long Island at that date.
- **Ephraim Cheeseman's parent "Mary Lilley" is unsupported.** No external source corroborates a parent named Mary Lilley for Ephraim Cheeseman. The sourced WikiTree profile instead lists his father as Thomas Cheeseman (abt. 1584–abt. 1614) of Kingston-upon-Thames, with the mother explicitly marked "unknown." This tree entry should be flagged as unverified lore, possibly a mismatched record from an unrelated Lilley/Cheeseman family.

## New findings

- Richard Cheesman's full sibling set for the generation below him (Peter's siblings) per WikiTree: Naomi, Anne, Thomas, Hannah, Benjamin, Margaret, Richard Jr., William, Elizabeth, Deborah, and Isabella — twelve children total, only a handful of whom appear in the tree's abbreviated children list for Richard/Peter's generation.
- Peter Cheesman's grist/sawmill near Turnersville, Gloucester Co., NJ passed through a documented chain of later owners (John Kay, Simeon Eastlack, Joshua Borton, Jacob Borden) into the early 19th century, and was reacquired by Peter's grandson, Major Peter Cheesman (1771–1856), in 1814 — useful context for the family's continued presence in Gloucester/Camden County NJ.
- The immigrant generation's marriage record (Ephraim Cheeseman × Ann Rason, 1639) is tied to a specific microfilm (FHL 991,683) and parish register, giving this the strongest documentary footing of any event in this line.
- Peter Cheesman's serial marriages (Ann Ellis 1732; Martha [Taber] Hedger; Christian Fisher 1760) mean Mary Ann Cheesman (b. 1746) was very likely a child of the middle marriage (to Martha), which chronologically fits between the 1732 and 1760 unions — this is a plausible reading not stated outright in any source, offered as inference only.

## Open questions

- Which "Martha Hedger" is correct: WikiTree carries what look like two overlapping/duplicated entries for Peter's wife of that name (one "married, date unknown," one "married 30 Jan 1760") — neither date matches the tree's "Bet 1746 and 1748." This needs resolution before Mary Ann's parentage date can be considered settled.
- Peter Cheesman's death location is given as Burlington Co. (WikiTree) vs. Gloucester Co. (this tree) — the Upton/Turnersville area straddled shifting Gloucester/Burlington/Camden county lines historically, so this may simply reflect different eras of county naming rather than a real conflict, but it hasn't been resolved against a primary deed or probate record.
- No record was found anywhere (tree or web) for Mary Ann Cheesman's death in 1840 in Washington Twp., Montgomery Co., Ohio, or for her 1768 marriage to Thomas Holt Jackson. This would need an Ohio county probate/cemetery/marriage-index search, which was out of scope for this Cheesman-focused pass (see the separate Jackson-line dossier if one exists).
- Ephraim Cheeseman's own ancestry beyond his father (Thomas Cheeseman, abt. 1584–1614) is unsourced even on WikiTree, which explicitly marks the next generation back as unknown ("[Cheeseman great-grandmother?]" placeholders). The tree's "Mary Lilley" claim cannot be traced to any of these compiled sources and should be treated as unverified until a source turns up.
- Exact circumstances/date of Thomas Cheesman's emigration from Kingston-upon-Thames to Long Island are not pinned down by any source found; he appears in Hempstead records only from 1680 onward.
