# Fragments Batch 03 — 40 Direct Ancestors, Generations 7–12

## Overview

This is a triage pass over 40 disconnected tree fragments (mostly single individuals or spouse-pairs, generations 7–12, roughly 1566–1795) spanning colonial Massachusetts/New Jersey/Pennsylvania, England, Scotland, Ireland, Germany, and Sweden. Most entries are thin, unsourced-but-internally-coherent stubs; ten threads were selected for targeted web verification based on notability, unusual names, existing source citations, or internal red flags. Two clusters checked out well against published/compiled genealogies (the Nantucket Coffin-adjacent Gard/Gardner pair, and the Northumberland Fairlam/Farlam one-name study, which also cross-validates against this repo's own `fragments-02.md`), one couple (Fitzgerald/Davison/Butler) surfaced a serious internal date contradiction that also connects to an existing dossier in this repo, and a handful of smaller data-quality issues (a garbled place name, a mismatched surname, a stray leading digit) were caught by inspection alone.

## Verified facts

- **Ruth Gardner** (b. 26 Jan 1676, Nantucket; d. 4 Oct 1748, Nantucket) was the daughter of John Gardner and Priscilla Grafton, and married **James Coffin Jr.** (1664–1741) as his second wife on 19 Mar 1692 (one secondary source gives 19 May 1692 for the same event — see Open questions). This exactly matches the tree's birth date, spouse, and place, and is independently confirmed both by open web sources and by this repo's own existing `line-coffin.md` dossier ("James Coffin Jr. ... married (2) Ruth Gardner, daughter of John and Priscilla Gardner, in 1692 — matches the tree's spouse listing"). — [Empty Branches on the Family Tree: James Coffin and Mary Severance, Nantucket, MA](https://emptybranchesonthefamilytree.com/2018/08/james-coffin-and-mary-severance-nantucket-ma/); [Coffin family PDF (billputman.com)](http://www.billputman.com/the-genealogy/canaday-related-families/Coffin1.pdf); this repo's `docs/research/ancestors/line-coffin.md`
- **A wife named "Christian Gard/Guard" for Peter Coffin (Sr.), 1671–1749, of Nantucket** is attested in multiple secondary sources, consistent with the tree's pairing of Christian Gard with "Peter Coffin Sr." and their son Bartlett Coffin. This repo's own `line-coffin.md` independently documents the same marriage ("Peter Coffin (Sr.) ... married first Christian (Gard/Guard) Conde"). However, her identity is genuinely contested in the secondary literature — see Open questions. — [Geni: Peter Coffin (1671–1749)](https://www.geni.com/people/Peter-Coffin/6000000003490605887); this repo's `docs/research/ancestors/line-coffin.md`
- **The Fairlam/Farlam family of Bishopside and Allendale, Northumberland** is a genuine, well-documented local family, the subject of a dedicated one-name study. The place names given for Nicholas Farlam's birth ("Bishopside, Eastalandaile (Allendale)") and death ("Allendale, Northumberland") match this family's known seat exactly, including a surviving 1650 title deed described as having been kept "in a kitchen drawer at Bishopside" for over 300 years. — [Fairlambs of Allendale](http://www.fairlambs.org/documents.htm); [Fairlambs USA — notes on family history 1678–2003](http://www.us.fairlambs.org/generations.htm)

## Corrections to the tree

- **Charles FitzGerald's own event dates are internally contradictory and should be reconciled before further use.** The tree gives his birth as 1780 (Limerick), a baptism event dated 14 Sep 1824 (44 years after that birth — implausible as a first baptism, and far too late to be a christening record for him personally), and his parents' (Garrett Fitzgerald × Elizabeth Davison) marriage as 20 Nov 1803, Brighton, Sussex — 23 years *after* Charles's own 1780 birth year. As recorded, a child cannot be born 23 years before his parents' marriage. Likely explanations, in rough order of probability: (a) Charles's birth year is mistranscribed (a birth in the late 1820s, after the 1803 marriage, would resolve both the parent-marriage gap and make the 1824 "baptism" event closer to plausible, though still off); (b) the 1824 baptism event actually belongs to a different Fitzgerald relative and was merged onto this record; (c) Garrett Fitzgerald and Elizabeth Davison are not in fact Charles's biological parents, and the parent-link itself is an error carried over from a compiled tree. This same Fitzgerald/Davison/Butler cluster surfaces independently in this repo's `docs/research/ancestors/gen06-patrick-donovan-bridget-crowly.md` dossier, which flagged that Charles FitzGerald and Bridget Butler's daughter, Bridget **Crowly** (b. abt 1821), carries a third surname that matches neither parent — consistent with a pre-existing marriage/relationship on Bridget Butler's side, and independently suggesting a birth-year problem in this branch predates this current dossier. **Recommendation: treat every date on this Fitzgerald/Davison/Butler cluster as unverified pending a look at the underlying "Ireland, Select Births and Baptisms" and "England, Select Marriages" index images the tree cites**, since the index summaries alone do not resolve which date(s) are wrong.
- **Dorryty Grewe's death place is garbled: "Allendale, Northumberland, Pennsylvania, United States" → almost certainly "Allendale, Northumberland, England."** No place named Allendale exists in Northumberland County, Pennsylvania, and mixing an English county name directly with a US state in one place-string is a classic data-merge artifact (the underlying source likely just said "Allendale, Northumberland" and an autocomplete/place-normalization step wrongly appended a US state). Her birthplace (also Allendale, Northumberland, England) and her husband Nicholas Farlam's identical death place ("Allendale, Northumberland, England") both support the English reading.
- **Isobel Grant's surname does not match her recorded parents or spouse.** Her parents are given as Alexr. Murray and Janet Findlay, and her own spouse is given as "John Murray" — yet she herself is recorded under the surname "Grant." A woman's birth surname should match her father's (Murray), not a third name. This is either (a) a genuine married-name-as-primary-name labeling convention (i.e., she was previously married to a Mr. Grant before or after marrying John Murray) or, more likely, (b) a data-entry conflation of two similarly-named people in the source tree. Recommend re-checking the underlying Dumfriesshire/Canonbie parish source before treating "Isobel Grant" as a settled identity.
- **"8 Juliana Grossmann"'s name field carries a stray leading "8"** — almost certainly a footnote/citation marker from the original source that wasn't stripped during import, not part of her actual name. Correct name is simply "Juliana Grossmann."
- **Konrad Grossmann's wife is recorded as "Christina (Grossmann)"** — carrying his own surname in parentheses is the standard placeholder genealogy software uses for an unknown maiden name (auto-filled to the husband's surname), not evidence that Christina was actually born a Grossmann or that this was a same-surname marriage. Treat her maiden name as unknown, not "Grossmann."

## New findings

- **Nicholas Farlam 0 (this batch, 1610–1667) fits precisely into a chain already partly documented in this repo's `fragments-02.md`.** That dossier independently triaged "Nicholas Fairlam" (1580–1635, "plausible," noted as generationally consistent with a grandson) and "*Nichollas Farlam" (1635–1729, Allendale, Northumberland, "plausible") as separate fragments from the same surname-place cluster. The current batch's Nicholas Farlam 0 (parents: Nicholas Fairlam and Agnes Armstronge; child: *Nichollas Farlam) sits exactly in between them by name, generation, and place — i.e., these three fragments across two separate triage batches almost certainly represent three consecutive generations of the same Bishopside/Allendale Fairlam/Farlam family (grandfather 1580–1635 → father, this record, 1610–1667 → son 1635–1729), which is a stronger internal-consistency signal than any single fragment alone.
- **Ruth Gardner's parents — John Gardner and Priscilla Grafton — are not recorded in this tree fragment at all** (the tree lists "parents": [] for her), but are well documented in secondary sources and match the tree's own birth date for her exactly (26 Jan 1676). This is a fact the tree is currently missing outright, not just under-sourcing.

## Open questions

- **Christian Gard's identity is unsettled in the secondary literature.** Multiple sources list a wife of Peter Coffin Sr. under variant spellings/identities — "Christian Gard," "Christian Guard," and "Christian Condy/Conde" — sometimes treating these as the same woman under spelling variants and sometimes as (implicitly) different women. The tree's parentage for her (William Gard × Mary Mariah Jameson) was not independently corroborated this pass. A primary Nantucket vital or probate record, or the published Coffin family genealogy (Allen Coffin, *The Coffin Family*, 1962 — already flagged as a needed primary source in `line-coffin.md`), would be needed to resolve this.
- **Ursula Geuder\Gruders** — is "Geuder" here genuinely connected to the historically real Nuremberg patrician Geuder (von Heroldsberg) family, or is this a case of a distinguished surname being attached to an unrelated ordinary German line (a known pattern in older compiled German genealogies)? Not resolved this pass.
- **Britta Gabrielsdotter, Charles R. Gilligan/Bridget Ganning, Richard Gray, Elizabeth Greenhope, Ann Grey, and Anna Maria Glauner** — all fit plausible, era-appropriate migration/settlement patterns (Swedish patronymic naming; Irish-to-Black-Country industrial migration; Essex Quaker-to-West-Jersey migration; Devon Quaker-to-Philadelphia migration; Haddonfield NJ Quaker settlement; Württemberg Lutheran pastor-adjacent family respectively), but no source specific to the named individual was located in open web search this pass. Login-gated collections (Ancestry, FamilySearch full record search, Find a Grave direct search, Sweden church-book databases like ArkivDigital) would likely be needed to move any of these from "plausible" to "verified."
- **Mary Griggs (b. 1580, England, m. Thomas Merrick)** has too little place/parish data to distinguish her from any of the many similarly-named women in English parish records of this era; this fragment is likely unresearchable further without an additional clue (a specific parish, or Thomas Merrick's own documented origin).
