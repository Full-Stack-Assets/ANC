## Overview

Jeremiah Regan (also spelled Ragan/Reagan) was born about 1838 in Ireland and
Mary Donovan was born 1839/1841 in Ireland, likely in or near Rosscarbery
("Ross"), County Cork, where she was baptized 14 Oct 1841. The couple married
25 Jul 1857 in Lawrence, Massachusetts, then part of the great wave of Irish
Famine-era immigration into that mill city. They eventually settled in
Peterborough, Hillsborough County, New Hampshire — a smaller Contoocook River
mill town that also drew Irish factory labor — where their son Dennis Francis
Regan was born in 1872. Jeremiah died about 1890, reportedly in New Hampshire.
This deep dive corroborates the Irish-Catholic mill-town setting of the
family on both sides of the Atlantic but found no new primary vital or
census record for the couple themselves on the open web; the login-gated
Ancestry/FamilySearch/Find a Grave indexes almost certainly hold the
underlying records the tree already cites.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Likely settlement town: Peterborough, Hillsborough Co., NH.** The tree
  itself only says "New Hampshire" for Jeremiah's death; cross-referencing
  this repo's gen-4 dossier for son Dennis (born there in 1872) strongly
  narrows the family's NH home to Peterborough, a Contoocook River mill town
  (cotton, woolen, paper mills; boot/shoe factory) that — like Lawrence, MA —
  drew heavy Irish immigrant labor from the 1850s onward.
- **Possible kinsman — Civil War soldier John Reagan of Peterborough:**
  Albert Smith's *History of the Town of Peterborough* (1876, Archive.org
  `historyoftownofp00smit`) Civil War roster lists "John Reagan, age 23, 2d
  Reg[iment, NH Volunteer Infantry], Co. G, 3 yrs. Wounded at first Bull Run
  battle, and subsequently taken prisoner at Glendale, Va.," enlisting circa
  May 1861 — meaning a John Reagan born about 1838, the same birth year given
  for Jeremiah, was living in Peterborough at exactly the right generation.
  [documented mention; possible brother of Jeremiah — kinship unproven]
- **Possible widow reference — "Mary Ragan" of Peterborough:** *Historical
  Sketches of Peterborough, New Hampshire* (1938, Archive.org
  `historicalsketch00unse_13`) records that a small farm lot ("No. 28" on the
  mountain road) was "owned or occupied by Samuel Morrison, Samuel Miller
  Jr., Joseph Mace, Betsy Farwell and **Mary Ragan**," with only a cellar
  hole remaining. This could plausibly be Mary (Donovan) Regan as a widow
  after Jeremiah's ~1890 death, but the identification is unproven — no
  dates are given in the source, and "Mary Ragan"/"Mary Regan" is not a rare
  name.
- **Irish immigration context for the 1857 marriage:** Lawrence, MA,
  incorporated in 1847, saw large-scale Famine-era Irish immigration almost
  immediately; by the 1850s a substantial share of its population was
  Irish-born. This supports the plausibility of two Irish immigrants — one
  of whom (Jeremiah) had evidently passed through England en route — meeting
  and marrying there in 1857 rather than in Ireland.

## Open questions

1. **Exact death date/place for Jeremiah** (currently only "Abt 1890, New
   Hampshire" and unsourced in the tree) — check NH death records/town
   reports for Peterborough, 1888–1892, and Find a Grave for a Peterborough
   or nearby Catholic cemetery (e.g., St. Peter's, Peterborough).
2. **Mary Donovan's death date/place** — not given in the tree at all; is she
   the "Mary Ragan" of the 1938 Peterborough sketch (lot 28, mountain road)?
   Peterborough town/probate/land records would help confirm or rule this
   out.
3. **1860, 1870, 1880 census households** for Jeremiah and Mary in
   Peterborough (or wherever they lived 1857–1872) — would establish other
   children besides John Joseph and Dennis Francis, exact Irish birthplaces,
   and Jeremiah's occupation (very likely mill labor, given the town).
4. **Kinship of Civil War soldier John Reagan (2nd NH Vol. Inf., Co. G) to
   Jeremiah** — NH adjutant-general rosters, Civil War pension file (widow's
   pension applications routinely name parents/siblings), and period
   Peterborough vital records could confirm or refute a brother
   relationship.
5. **Daniel Reagan's occupation/regiment at Woolwich in 1851** — was he
   British Army (Royal Artillery, garrisoned at Woolwich) or a civilian
   Arsenal/dockyard worker? This would explain why an Irish family was living
   in Kent, England, in 1851 rather than Ireland or America, and is worth
   checking against the actual 1851 England census household (already cited
   by the tree but not independently re-viewed here).
6. **"Julia Gillian Reagan" as Jeremiah's mother's name** — worth verifying
   against the underlying Ancestry Family Tree/source once accessible; the
   double given-name is unusual and could be a transcription of a
   maiden-name field.
7. **Marriage record itself** (Massachusetts, Marriage Records, 1840-1915,
   Lawrence, 25 Jul 1857) — not independently re-viewed; would give ages,
   parents' names as recorded in 1857, and possibly occupations/residences
   at time of marriage.
