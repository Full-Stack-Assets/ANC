# Fragments Batch 01 — 40 Direct Ancestors, Generations 7–12

## Overview

This is a triage pass over 40 disconnected tree fragments (mostly single individuals or small couple-pairs, generations 7–12, roughly 1573–1780) spanning England, colonial New England/New York, Scotland, Ireland, Germany, and Sweden. Most entries are thin, single-source-or-unsourced stubs with internally coherent dates; nine were selected for targeted verification based on notability, unusual names, or existing source citations. Two solid, well-documented colonial lines emerged (the Bull family of Hamptonburgh, Orange Co., NY, and Bartlett Coffin of Nantucket — the latter already covered in more depth in `line-coffin.md`), one likely embellishment was caught (invented "Lady"/"Lord" titles on the Brigham-Crosby couple), and one flat factual conflict surfaced (Richard Biggs III's death year/place, and a probable case of multiple spurious "wives" merged from other trees).

## Verified facts

- **William Bull** (1689–1755) was baptized 2 Sept 1689 at St Peter's Collegiate Church, Wolverhampton, Staffordshire, son of John Bull; trained as a stonemason (reportedly in Dublin, Ireland); immigrated to New York in 1715; married Sarah Wells 24 Aug 1718 at Hamptonburgh, Orange Co., NY; named his homestead "Hamptonburgh" after his English birthplace; died 17 Feb 1755 at Hamptonburgh. He is the documented progenitor of the Bull family of Orange County, NY, whose stone-masonry trade and stone houses (including the surviving "Bull Stone House," now on the National Register of Historic Places) are well recorded. — [Find a Grave #36891099](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/36891099/william-bull); [Bull Stone House: William Bull & Sarah Wells](https://www.bullstonehouse.org/william-bull--sarah-wells.html); [Wikipedia: Bull Stone House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Stone_House); [WikiTree Bull-592](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Bull-592)
- **John Hunter Bull** (tree: b. 4 May 1645, Wolverhampton) matches the compiled-genealogy record of William Bull's father, "John Bull," christened 4 May 1645 in the Collegiate Church of St Peter, Wolverhampton — confirming the parent-child link and birth date/place in the tree. — same sources as above
- **Everard Bolton Jr** and **Mary Naylor**: a Quaker meeting record shows "Everard Boulton of Cheltenham... and Mary Naylor, daughter of Robert Naylor," publishing their marriage intention at Darby Monthly Meeting in April/May 1707 — closely matching the tree's marriage date of 2 Apr 1707. Everard's will was proven 18 May 1716 with Mary named executor, consistent with the tree's death date of 23 Dec 1715. — [WikiTree Bolton-414](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Bolton-414); [WikiTree Naylor-947](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Naylor-947); [Immigration of the Irish Quakers into Pennsylvania (rootsweb)](https://freepages.rootsweb.com/~billie0w/books/quakers/appendix.htm)
- **Bartlett Coffin of Nantucket** (b. ca. 1699, d. 1762) matches WikiTree/Geni records closely: son of Peter Coffin and his first wife Christian (Gard/Guard) Conde; married Judith Bunker; the tree's parent names ("Peter Coffin Sr," "Christian Gard") and spouse ("Judith Mary Bunker") both check out. This individual and his broader family line were already researched in more depth in this repository's `line-coffin.md`, which independently corroborates these facts from primary-adjacent sources (islandregister.com PEI Coffin narrative, FamilySearch, Geni). — [WikiTree Coffin-278](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Coffin-278); [Geni: Peter Coffin (1671–1749)](https://www.geni.com/people/Peter-Coffin/6000000003490605887); `docs/research/ancestors/line-coffin.md`

Dec 1638 aboard the *John of London* as a widow. — [WikiTree Brigham-88](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Brigham-88); [Find a Grave #38453033](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/38453033)

## Corrections to the tree

- **Mary Biggs's death year: 1696 → likely 1724.** WikiTree's compiled record for "Mary (Biggs) Hawkins" gives her death as 1724 at about age 79 in Brookhaven, NY — 28 years later than the tree's 1696. This is a secondary/compiled source, not a primary record, so treat as **inferred**, not fully documented, but it is a large enough gap (and specific enough, with an age given) to flag as likely wrong in the tree. Evidence: [WikiTree Biggs-1305](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Biggs-1305).
- **Richard William Biggs III: death "1 Sept 1626 at Jamestown, Virginia" is likely wrong on both count and place.** Multiple compiled sources (WikiTree, FamilySearch) instead place this Richard Biggs's death "probably about March 1626" (not September) at **West and Shirley Hundred, Charles City, Virginia** (not Jamestown), and give his birth as ca. **1584**, not 1590. He served in the House of Burgesses 1625–26 and left a will naming wife "Sara" (unnamed surname) and son Richard. The tree's list of **four** spouses (Dorothy Scarlett, Sarah Anne Juxon Marshe Biggs, Sarah Browne, Agnes Henley) — with marriage dates spanning 1605–1619, all before his documented ca.-1618 marriage to "Sarah Unknown" in Virginia — looks like a classic merged-tree artifact where several unrelated Biggs/Henley lineage guesses have been stacked onto one profile. Treat all four listed wives as **unconfirmed/legend** pending a primary source (parish register or Virginia land/probate record) for any of them. Evidence: [WikiTree Biggs-87](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Biggs-87); [FamilySearch L6JK-22J](https://ancestors.familysearch.org/en/L6JK-22J/richard-biggs-1590-1626); [McKenzies of Early Maryland](https://mckenziesofearlymaryland.com/getperson.php?personID=I31852&tree=McKenzie1).
- **"Lady Constance Brigham" / "Lord Robert Crosby of Holme Hall" — titles unsupported.** No source found gives either person a peerage or knighthood title; the compiled WikiTree/Find a Grave record simply describes Constance as a widow (maiden name Brigham) who remarried into the Crosby family and calls her husband "Robert Crosby," with no "Lord" styling. This reads as an inflation commonly seen in older family-tree compilations reaching for gentry status. Recommend dropping "Lady"/"Lord" from both names unless a primary source surfaces. Evidence: [WikiTree Brigham-88](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Brigham-88).
- **Sir WILLIAM Knight Bull "(Quaker)" — the Quaker label is anachronistic.** He died in 1623; the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers) was not founded until the 1650s (George Fox began preaching in 1647, the movement organized from 1652). Either the death date, the "Quaker" tag, or the identity of this person is wrong; this looks like a label bled over from a later, unrelated Bull-family Quaker branch (several other Bulls in this same tree fragment, e.g. William Bull of Orange Co. NY, moved in Quaker circles). Not independently researched beyond noting the internal contradiction.
- **Mary Brooks (I182541959520) and Mary Brooks (I182541966376) are duplicate entries** — identical name, sex, generation, birth/death years, birthplace, deathplace, spouse, and single child ("Priscilla Colburn"). This is a data-quality issue (the same person entered twice under two IDs) rather than two real people; recommend merging in the source tree.
- **Bridget Butler's birthplace is internally inconsistent** — one event says birth "abt 1805 @ Limerick, St John's, Limerick city," another says birth "@ Dublin, Ireland." Both are marked [sourced] in the tree, meaning two different underlying source records disagree; this needs to be resolved against the actual cited records (Ireland Catholic Parish Registers vs. the NSW Gaol description book) rather than trusted as-is.

## New findings

- William Bull's stonemasonry trade (learned in Dublin) and his 1715 immigration are not captured anywhere in the tree's event list, which has no immigration or occupation event for him at all — only birth, death, and burial.
- The Bull Stone House built by William Bull's descendants at Hamptonburgh, NY survives and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, giving this line an unusually strong physical/documentary record for a generation-9 colonial ancestor.
- Everard Bolton Jr.'s father, the immigrant Everard Bolton Sr. (b. ca. 1655–1660, d. 1727), arrived in Pennsylvania in 1682 from Ross Monthly Meeting, Herefordshire — the same English origin place the tree gives for Everard Jr. (Ross, Herefordshire, b. 1680) — supporting that Everard Jr. was born in England just before the family's 1682 emigration, though the tree currently lists no parents for him.
- Thönges (Thoenges) Christ of Rengsdorf, Neuwied, had a documented emigrating son, Lorentz/Lawrence Christ (baptized 29 Jan 1707, naturalized in New York 8 Nov 1735), fitting the tree's note that this family "may have sailed to New York" — though the connection between the tree's Thoenges (Antonius) Christ and the "Thönges Christ" of this WikiTree profile is not confirmed as the same individual (see Open Questions).

## Open questions

- Which of Richard William Biggs III's four listed "wives" (if any) is real? A primary Hampshire parish register or Virginia land/probate search is needed; current secondary sources only document one wife ("Sarah Unknown," ca. 1618, Virginia).
- Is the tree's "Thoenges (Antonius) Christ\Crist" (b. 1678) the same person as WikiTree's "Thönges Christ" (b. abt. 1690)? A 12-year birth-year gap is significant; this needs a Rengsdorf parish register check rather than reliance on two disagreeing secondary compilations.
- Did Mary Biggs (Hawkins) die in 1696 (per the tree) or 1724 (per WikiTree)? No primary Setauket/Brookhaven town or church record was checked directly.
- No independent record was located for Bridget Butler's convict transportation to Australia despite the tree's unusually rich source list (NSW Gaol Description Books, Ireland Calendar of Wills, etc.) — these sources were cited in the tree but not re-verified here; a follow-up pass should pull the actual NSW State Archives or Ireland civil registration index entries rather than relying on general web search.
- Isabella Boyce (1739–1823, Kelton, Kirkcudbrightshire → Bay Fortune, PEI) could not be confirmed or refuted online in the time available; the migration pattern (southwest Scotland → PEI in the late 18th century) is common and plausible but unverified.
- No independent verification was attempted for the ~27 "plausible" entries in the triage table; they are internally coherent but rest entirely on the tree's own (mostly unsourced) data.
