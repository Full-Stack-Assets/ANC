# Fragments Batch 10 — 40 Ancestors, Generations 7-12 (Triage)

## Overview

This batch covers 40 direct ancestors scattered across surname fragments S–T (Simmons/Simpson through Twadel), generations 7–12, drawn from English, Scottish, Swedish, German, and colonial American/Irish lines. Per instructions this was a **triage pass**: every person got a quick plausibility read from the tree data, and the ~10 most findable/notable were checked against outside sources (WikiTree, Geni, Access Genealogy, dedicated local-history sites, rootsweb/WorldConnect). All individuals in this batch are pre-1940 with no living-persons concerns.

Two names turned out to be genuinely notable and well-documented outside the tree: **Charlotte (Taylor) Hierlihy**, the semi-legendary "Mother of Tabusintac" (New Brunswick), and **William Stockdale**, a Quaker minister/pamphleteer — though the latter raises a serious identity question (see corrections). The Taber and Tringham/Stringham lines connect to solidly documented 17th-century Massachusetts and Long Island colonial families with published genealogies.

## Verified facts

- **Thomas Taber** (1646–1730) of Dartmouth, MA was baptized 8 Feb 1646 at Yarmouth, Plymouth Colony, son of Philip Tabor and Lydia Masters; died 11 Nov 1730 in Dartmouth. He served as town surveyor (1673), town clerk and constable (1679), was made freeman (1684), served as selectman (1685, 1692, 1694, 1699, 1701, 1702, 1711), was twice a representative to the Massachusetts General Court (incl. 1693), and held rank of captain (1689). — [WikiTree Taber-73](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Taber-73), [Access Genealogy: Taber family of Dartmouth](https://accessgenealogy.com/massachusetts/taber.htm)
- Thomas Taber married (1) Esther Cook, who died 1671, and (2) in June 1672, **Mary Thompson/Tomson** (b. 1650, Plymouth), daughter of John and Mary (Cook) Thompson and granddaughter of *Mayflower* passenger Francis Cook of Plymouth; she died 3 May 1734 in Dartmouth. — [Access Genealogy](https://accessgenealogy.com/massachusetts/taber.htm)
- **Jonathan Taber** (1685–1723), son of Thomas Taber and Mary Tomson, is documented in the same Dartmouth-family compilations. — [WikiTree Taber-73](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Taber-73)
- **Peter Stringham** (tree's "Peter Stringham II," b. c.1650–1659, Hereford) sailed for America in May 1677 among ~230 Quakers on the ship *Kent*, settling first at Jamaica and then Hempstead, Long Island; his will was dated 6 Aug 1726 and proved 13 Feb 1727 at Hempstead. — [WikiTree Stringham-17](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Stringham-17), [Sackett-tree database](https://sackett-tree.org/getperson.php?personID=I29826&tree=1)
- **Cassandra Thompson** married Morgan Drewett on 23 Sep 1658 at St Dunstan, Stepney, London; their daughter Hannah Drewett was born 1 Nov 1669 at Shadwell — this Hannah later married William Stockdale and had a son Josiah **Albertson**, tying this fragment into the Albertson surname line. — [WikiTree Thompson-12226](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Thompson-12226), [WikiTree Drewett-16](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Drewett-16)
- **Charlotte (Taylor) Hierlihy** ("The Mother of Tabusintac") was christened in 1755 at the Holborn Lying-In Hospital, London, and died 25 Apr 1841 at Stymiest's Millstream, Tabusintac, New Brunswick (obituary in the Royal Gazette, 5 May 1841); she is the subject of the book *The Nine Lives of Charlotte Taylor* by Sally Armstrong and a dedicated genealogical site. — [charlottetaylor.ca](https://charlottetaylor.ca/tabusintac/)
- A William Stockdale — early Quaker convert and one of the "Publishers of Truth" based at Swarthmoor Hall, active in Scotland by 1655 (per George Fox's Journal), later of Charlemont and Grange meetings in Ireland, author of *The Great Cry of Oppression* (1683), Justice of the Peace for New Castle County (1685, 1689, 1690), member of the Pennsylvania Provincial Council (1689–90) — married Hannah Drewett in 1689 and died in Philadelphia, buried 23 Jul 1693. — [Geni: William Stockdale](https://www.geni.com/people/William-Stockdale/6000000027408709952), [Immigration of the Irish Quakers into Pennsylvania](https://freepages.rootsweb.com/~billie0w/books/quakers/appendix3.htm)

## Corrections to the tree

- **Thomas Taber's mother**: tree lists "Jane Taber" → published genealogies (WikiTree, Access Genealogy, Miner Descent) agree his mother was **Lydia Masters** of Watertown, wife of Philip Tabor. "Jane Taber" does not appear in any external source found.
- **Thomas Taber's spouse list**: the tree carries five spouse entries for Thomas Taber ("Mary Taber" m.1667 Martha's Vineyard, "Hester or Esther Cooke" m. Abt.1667 Dartmouth, "Hannah Gray" m. Jan 1667 Dartmouth, "Mary Tomson," and "Hester or Esther Cooke" again). Documented sources describe only **two** wives: (1) Esther Cook (m. c.1667, d.1671) and (2) Mary Thompson/Tomson (m. June 1672, d.1734). "Mary Taber" and "Hannah Gray" look like duplicate/conflated entries from other researchers rather than real additional marriages — treat with caution.
- **Charlotte Howe Taylor's parents**: tree names them as "General William Charles Howe Taylor" and "Charlotte Ann MacDonald." New Brunswick genealogists who have researched this extensively state flatly there is **no record of a General by that name** in British or North American military/vital records. The one hard document located (the Holborn Lying-In Hospital birth/christening record) points instead to parents **Charles Taylor**, a gardener in Kensington, Middlesex, and his wife **Ann Taylor**. The "General" parentage should be treated as unsupported family legend, not fact.
- **Peter Stringham II's death date**: tree gives "6 August 1726" as the death date, but external sources show this is the date his **will was signed**; the will was proved 13 Feb 1727, meaning his actual death more likely falls in that window (late 1726–early 1727), not necessarily exactly 6 Aug 1726.
- **William Stockdale's birth year (flagged, not resolved)**: the tree's William Stockdale (b.1669, m. Hannah Drewett 1690, d. July 1693 Bucks Co., PA) matches, almost detail-for-detail, a documented Quaker minister of the same name who married Hannah Drewett in 1689 and died in Philadelphia in 1693 — except that minister was already an adult "Publisher of Truth" preaching in Scotland in **1655**, which is impossible for someone born in 1669. Either the tree's birth year is wrong, or two different men named William Stockdale (an elder minister and a younger immigrant) have been merged online. This needs primary-source resolution before treating the "Great Cry of Oppression" biography as this ancestor's.

## New findings

- Charlotte Taylor had **two marriages before Philip Hierlihy**: first to Capt. John Blake in 1777 (two sons plus an adopted daughter), then to William Wishart after Blake's death in 1785 (one son), before marrying Hierlihy in 1787. The tree shows only the Hierlihy marriage.
- Charlotte Taylor is a locally famous figure — "Mother of Tabusintac" — the subject of a full-length popular history book and a dedicated genealogy/history website, which is unusual for this batch of otherwise obscure ancestors.
- The Drewett/Stockdale/Albertson line (Cassandra Thompson → Hannah Drewett → William Stockdale's household → son Josiah **Albertson**) is a documented Quaker migration chain from London/Stepney through Ireland to Pennsylvania/New Jersey, consistent with this repository's apparent Albertson-surname focus.
- The Stringham/Tringham family carries a family legend of Huguenot origin — "de Tringham" refugees fleeing France after the 1572 St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre, settling in Herefordshire/Worcestershire before anglicizing the name. This is repeated on several genealogy sites but is not primary-sourced; treat as legend.
- Mary Tomson (Thompson), wife of Thomas Taber, was reportedly a granddaughter of *Mayflower* passenger Francis Cook of Plymouth through her mother, Mary Cook Thompson — a potential Mayflower descent line worth flagging for anyone chasing Mayflower Society lineage, though this claim was not independently traced back to a primary Mayflower-Society-grade source in this pass.

## Open questions

- Is the tree's William Stockdale (b.1669) actually the documented Quaker minister and pamphleteer, or a different, younger man of the same name and same wife's name? The birth year and preaching-since-1655 biography are in tension.
- Who were Charlotte Taylor's real parents? Even dedicated researchers of her life describe this as an unresolved mystery — the "General Howe Taylor" story is legend, and the Holborn hospital record giving "Charles and Ann Taylor" is the best lead but not conclusively tied to this Charlotte.
- What is the correct reading of "Mariae Gugliomi," listed as wife of Richard/Richarde Thompson/Thomson and mother of Cassandra Thompson? No record under this name (or an obvious English original) was found; it may be a mistranscription.
- Is "Capt. Barnabas Horton II" (husband of Mary Swazey) correctly distinguished from the many other Barnabas Hortons in the Southold, Long Island Horton genealogy? Sources confirm a Horton–Swazey/Swezey neighborly and marital connection in the area generally, and a son "Silas Horton" (b.1730, d.1820, Goshen NY) matching the tree's child, but the exact husband was not pinned to a specific, disambiguated Barnabas Horton in this pass.
- Is Söfring Söfringsson Krabbe's surname genuine for a Dalarna farming family, or does it reflect confusion with the unrelated Danish/Swedish noble Krabbe family? No record was found either way.
- Which of Alexander Steel's (Bathgate, West Lothian) or Margaret Steel's ("of Ayrshire") recorded locations is correct, given they are in different historic counties?
