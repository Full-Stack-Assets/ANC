# The Crosby Line (Surname Line 4) — Generations 9-11

## Overview

This is a short surname-line fragment (only 4 people supplied, generations 9-11, roughly 1620s-1755) tracing Catharine Crosby (1675-1755), who married into the Fairlamb family of Chester County, Pennsylvania, back through her father Richard Crosby (1646-1718), an emigrant from Cheshire, England, to his purported father John Crosby (b. 1623, Settle, Yorkshire). Richard Crosby's presence as a landowner in Ridley Township, Chester County, PA in the early 1700s is corroborated by independent land-record evidence, but every date, place, and parent-child link beyond that rests on unsourced compiled online trees (FamilySearch Family Tree / WikiTree / rootsweb-style pages), and the line's oldest claimed link — John Crosby's parents "Lord Robert Crosby of Holme Hall" and "Lady Constance Brigham" — is very likely a false merge with an unrelated, well-documented East Riding Yorkshire family. The documented line effectively ends at Richard Crosby (b. c.1646); everything above him is unsupported.

## Verified facts

- Richard Crosby (the presumed father of Catharine/Katharine Crosby Fairlamb) was a real, documented landowner in Ridley Township, Chester County (now Delaware County), Pennsylvania in the early 1700s: on 28 August 1705 Richard Crosby and John Crosby received a deed for a mill and 63 acres on Ridley Creek; in 1685 Robert Pennell bought 100 acres from Richard Crosby, and in 1711 Crosby sold the remainder of a 370-acre tract to William Pennell. — Ridley Township History (ridleytownshiphistory.com) and Takingthelongerview.org, "Robert Pennell and Hannah Hyandson" (https://takingthelongerview.org/index.php/2019/07/24/robert-pennell-and-hannah-hyandson/)
- The Historical Society of Pennsylvania holds an archival manuscript titled "Notes on the Crosby (Crosbye, Crosbie) family of Cheshire, England and Chester (now Delaware) County, Pennsylvania," confirming that genealogists have long treated this as a Cheshire-to-Chester-County emigrant family — but the manuscript itself was not accessible for this research (not digitized). — HSP Library Catalog, https://discover.hsp.org/Record/marc-74044
- Nicholas Fairlamb (b. 1678, Stockton-on-Tees, Durham, England; d. 1722, Chester/Delaware Co., PA) married Katharine Crosby in 1703 in Chester County, PA; she is identified as the daughter of Richard Crosby and Ellinor Done, born 19 October 1675 in England — an independent secondary compilation that corroborates (with a ~2-month day/month date discrepancy vs. the tree's "19 Dec 1675") the tree's data on this couple. — Fairlambs USA family history, compiled by Ethel Dougherty, http://www.us.fairlambs.org/generations.htm
- A well-documented, unrelated Yorkshire Crosby family exists that the tree's ancestor claims appear to be confused with: Robert Crosby of Holme Hall (Holme-on-Spalding-Moor, East Riding of Yorkshire, b. 30 Oct 1596, son of John Crosby and Jane Webster) married Constance Brigham (b. c.1602, daughter of Thomas Brigham and Isabel Watson) on 22 July 1622; Robert died between 1634 and 1638. Widow Constance and her children (documented as daughters, including Mary Crosby who married a Longhorne) emigrated to New England, settling in Rowley, Essex County, Massachusetts by 1643; Constance died there before 25 Jan 1684. — WikiTree Brigham-88 (https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Brigham-88); Miner Descent blog, "Robert Crosby" (https://minerdescent.com/2010/06/29/robert-crosby/); FindAGrave memorial 38453033 (https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/38453033)

## Corrections to the tree

- "Lord Robert Crosby of Holme Hall" and "Lady Constance Brigham (widow Crosby/Crosbee) 1st cousin of Tho Brigham, Immigrant" listed as parents of John Crosby (b. 1623, Settle, Craven, North Yorkshire) -> **No documentary support found; almost certainly a false merge.** The real, documented Robert Crosby of Holme Hall married Constance Brigham in 1622 in Holme-on-Spalding-Moor (East Riding), a different part of Yorkshire from Craven/Settle (West Riding/North Yorkshire border, many miles away). Robert died 1634-1638, and the couple's documented children are daughters who emigrated with their widowed mother to Rowley, Massachusetts by 1643 — there is no record of a son named John who stayed in Yorkshire, married, and fathered a Richard Crosby who later emigrated to Pennsylvania. The titles "Lord" and "Lady" are not supported by any source found; Robert Crosby of Holme Hall appears in records as untitled Yorkshire gentry, not peerage. This looks like a classic "attach to the famous/documented family" tree error — the compiler likely grafted an obscure, unrelated Craven-area Crosby onto the well-documented (and Wikitree/Geni/Miner-Descent-published) Crosby-Brigham-of-Rowley line for false prestige/completeness. Treat the entire "John Crosby of Settle" generation and above as **legend/unsupported**, pending direct Yorkshire parish-register verification.
- No correction found to Richard Crosby's or Elinor Done's core vital data (birth/death years, Chester PA death location) — these could not be independently confirmed or refuted from sources found; see Open Questions.

## New findings

- Independent corroboration (not previously in the tree) that Nicholas Fairlamb, husband of Catharine/Katharine Crosby, was born in **Stockton-on-Tees, County Durham, England** in 1678 and died in 1722 in Chester/Delaware County, PA — giving his likely immigrant origin point, distinct from his wife's Cheshire origin. — Fairlambs USA (us.fairlambs.org)
- The 1705 Ridley Creek mill deed names **both** "Richard and John Crosby" as grantees together — suggesting Richard Crosby likely had a son, brother, or other close relative also named John Crosby active in Chester County in 1705 (not to be confused with the English John Crosby, b. 1623, claimed as Richard's father). This is worth flagging as a possible source of name confusion/duplication within the existing tree data itself (a second "John Crosby," a generation younger, active in PA) — Takingthelongerview.org.
- The Historical Society of Pennsylvania's dedicated manuscript on the "Crosby (Crosbye, Crosbie)" family of Cheshire and Chester Co., PA (HSP catalog record marc-74044) is a specific, citable lead for a follow-up archive visit or scan request — it was not accessible online in this research pass but is the single most promising concrete source for verifying Richard Crosby's English origins and parentage.

## Open questions

- No primary source (parish register, will, deed, or passenger record) was found that directly documents Richard Crosby's birth in Runcorn, Cheshire in 1646, his death in Chester, PA in 1718, or his marriage to Elinor Done — all currently rest on unsourced/compiled online trees. The HSP manuscript (marc-74044) is the best lead to resolve this.
- Elinor Done's parents are given in the tree as "Robert Doane" and "Elizabeth Bellin" — note the surname inconsistency (Done vs. Doane). Done is a genuine, prominent Cheshire gentry surname (the Done family of Utkinton, hereditary Foresters of Delamere), while Doane is a well-documented, unrelated Cape Cod/Eastham Massachusetts family (descended from Deacon John Doane). No source was found connecting an "Elinor Done/Doane" of Frodsham, Cheshire to a Robert Doane/Done and Elizabeth Bellin; this parentage looks unverified and possibly another cross-family name conflation, though not as clearly disprovable as the Holme Hall claim. Needs direct Cheshire parish-register or Done-family-pedigree verification.
- Where exactly the "immigrant ancestor" transition occurs is unclear: the tree implies Richard Crosby himself emigrated (birth in Runcorn, Cheshire; death in Chester, PA), which would make **Richard Crosby the immigrant**, but no passenger list, naturalization, or Quaker meeting migration certificate confirming his emigration (as opposed to the family simply having Cheshire origins several generations back) was located.
- Is the "John Crosby" named as co-grantee on the 1705 Ridley Creek deed the same person as Richard Crosby's claimed father (who per the tree would have been an elderly ~82-year-old still in Yorkshire, not Pennsylvania, in 1705 — implausible), a different son of Richard Crosby, or an unrelated Chester County Crosby? This needs resolution to avoid conflating two different John Crosbys.
