# The Cheeseman Line (Surname Line 4) — Generations 7–12

## Overview

This is a direct-line reconstruction of the Cheesman/Cheeseman surname (spelling varies by generation and record) from the immigrant ancestor, **Thomas Cheesman** (b. c.1640, Kingston-upon-Thames, Surrey, England; d. 1713, Hempstead, Long Island, NY), down through five generations of a colonial/early-republic New Jersey farming family to **Peter Thomas Cheeseman "Major"** (1771–1856) of Gloucester/Camden County, NJ. The tree data supplied for this line has a **missing generation**: it jumps from generation 9 (Peter C. Cheeseman, b. 1704) directly to generation 11 (Thomas Cheesman, the immigrant, b. 1640) with no generation-10 record present, even though generation 9's stated parents ("Richard Cheeseman Sr" and "Hannah Halstead") clearly correspond to a real, well-documented person who is absent from the file. Published/compiled genealogies (WikiTree, Long Island Surnames database citing *The Cheesman Family: Three Centuries in Southern New Jersey* by Albert Stirling Adams, 1985) fill this gap and largely corroborate the tree's names, dates, and places, with a few corrections noted below.

## Verified facts

- **Thomas Cheesman (gen. 11, immigrant ancestor)**, b. c.1640 Kingston-upon-Thames, Surrey, England; d. 1713, Hempstead, Queens Co., Long Island, NY. Son of Ephraim Cheesman and Ann Rason/Reason of Kingston-on-Thames. Married (before 1680) Mary "Margaret" Valentine, daughter of Richard Valentine, Marshall of Hempstead and a first settler there (1647). Thomas held an 8-acre plantation called "Hungry Harbour" north of Herricks, Hempstead Township. — Find a Grave memorial for Thomas Cheesman (1640–1713); FamilySearch tree entry LJJ9-458; WikiTree Cheesman family pages; Olive Tree Genealogy, "17th Century Immigrants to New Netherland & New York."
- Thomas and Mary's children included **Richard Cheesman** (b. 9 Nov 1674, Hempstead, LI), plus William, Samuel, Rachel, Ephraim, Joseph, and others named in the 1698 Census of Hempstead, L.I. — Olive Tree Genealogy; WikiTree.
- **Richard Cheesman Sr. (generation 10 — MISSING from tree data)**, b. 9 Nov 1674 Hempstead, LI; d. 2 Sep 1744, Cheesman Plantation, Waterford Township, Gloucester Co., NJ (will dated 19 Mar 1743/4, proved 7 Sep 1744, naming wife Hannah and sons Thomas, Benjamin, Richard, William, Peter). Married 10 Jan 1694, St. George's Church, Hempstead, LI, to **Hannah Stringham** (b. c.1676, Flushing, Queens Co., LI; d. c.1749). — WikiTree Cheesman-22 and Stringham-7; genealogieonline.nl "From Castles to America."
- **Peter C. Cheesman (generation 9)**, b. 16 Jun 1704, Old Upton, near Blackwoodtown (now Blackwood), Gloucester Co., NJ; d. between 19 Apr and 28 Jun 1788 (will dated 19 Apr 1786, Gloucester Township). Son of Richard Cheesman Sr. and Hannah Stringham. On 22 Aug 1753 he purchased 350 acres originally owned by Henry Roe; he was among the pioneer settlers of Turnersville, NJ, alongside John Turner, Henry Hurff, and John Williams. Named committee service in 1774 per *Prologue to Independence*. — Long Island Surnames database (citing Adams, *The Cheesman Family*, and Peter Cheesman's 1786 will); FamilySearch KLYD-97M; WikiTree Cheesman-112; Find a Grave memorial #103851021.
- Peter C. Cheesman married (secondly, c.1746) **Ann Ellis** — matching the tree's spouse "Ann Hannah Ellis." His will (1786) names children Richard, Thomas, Mary Ann (Jackson), Deborah (Pierce), and Margaret (Pierce). — Long Island Surnames database; WikiTree.
- **Richard D. Cheeseman Jr. (generation 8)**, b. 6 Oct 1749 (WikiTree gives 16 Oct 1749), Old Upton, Gloucester Co., NJ; d. 3 Nov 1799, Gloucester Co./Blackwood, NJ, age 50. Son of Peter C. Cheesman and Ann Hannah Ellis. Married Rachel Williams (b. 10 Mar 1751, Gloucester Co., NJ). Siblings recorded elsewhere include Thomas Cheesman (b. 1734) and Mary Ann (Cheseman) Jackson (b. 1746). — WikiTree Cheesman-110 (citing Ancestry Family Trees and Find a Grave memorial #103849860).
- **Peter Thomas Cheeseman "Major" (generation 7)**, b. 9 Mar 1771, Cheesman Mill (Old Upton), Gloucester Co., NJ; d. 24 Mar 1856, Blackwood, Camden Co., NJ. Son of Richard D. Cheeseman Jr. and Rachel Williams. He acquired (by 1814) and operated a stone gristmill near Turnersville, NJ, and carried the courtesy title "Major." His daughter Catherine (Cheeseman) is corroborated as "Catherine (Cheeseman) Shone" in outside compiled trees, matching the tree's listed child. — Geni "Major Peter Thomas Cheesman"; WikiTree Cheesman family pages; Long Island Surnames database.
- The place-name shift from "Gloucester" to "Camden" across generations 7–9 is a real administrative change, not a data error: Camden County, NJ was created out of Gloucester County in 1844, so identical locations (e.g., Old Upton/Blackwood) are correctly recorded as "Gloucester" in 18th-century records and "Camden" in 19th-century ones.

## Corrections to the tree

- **Peter C. Cheeseman's mother, listed as "Hannah Halstead" -> better rendered "Hannah Stringham" (with Halstead as her birth surname).** Evidence: Multiple independent compiled sources (WikiTree Cheesman-22/Stringham-7, genealogieonline.nl) agree Richard Cheesman Sr. married "Hannah Stringham" on 10 Jan 1694. Genealogical notes explain the apparent conflict: Hannah's biological father was Joseph Halstead (d. 1679); her mother Susannah (Harcutt) Halstead remarried Peter Stringham in/after 1679, and Hannah was thereafter known under her stepfather's surname, Stringham. So "Halstead" is not wrong — it is her birth father's surname — but "Stringham" is the surname under which she married Richard Cheesman and by which she is recorded in nearly every published pedigree. The tree should carry both, e.g., "Hannah (Halstead) Stringham."
- **Missing generation-10 person: Richard Cheesman Sr. (1674–1744) and wife Hannah Stringham/Halstead are absent from the supplied tree data as standalone records**, even though generation 9 (Peter C. Cheeseman) lists them by name as parents. This is a structural gap, not a factual error in the existing records — but it means the tree file, as given, cannot actually connect generation 9 to generation 11 (Thomas Cheesman, the immigrant) without this inserted person. Recommend adding Richard Cheesman Sr. as a formal generation-10 node.
- **Minor date variant:** Richard D. Cheeseman Jr.'s birth is given in the tree as 6 Oct 1749; the WikiTree/Ancestry-derived compiled record gives 16 Oct 1749. Not resolved to a primary record in this pass — flagged as an open question rather than a confirmed correction.

## New findings (facts not in the tree)

- Thomas Cheesman's parents are named in compiled sources as **Ephraim Cheesman and Ann Rason (Reason)** of Kingston-upon-Thames — this matches the tree's generation-11 parent listing exactly, corroborating rather than adding, but it is worth noting this pushes the documented family one more generation back into England (Ephraim Cheesman, c.1610–1657, per Geni) — that English generation is compiled/secondary only and was not independently verified against English parish registers in this pass.
- Thomas Cheesman married **Mary "Margaret" Valentine**, daughter of Richard Valentine, described in Hempstead town records as its "Marshall" and a first settler of Hempstead (1647) — an added biographical detail not in the tree.
- Richard Cheesman Sr. and Hannah Stringham had **12 children**, per his 1743/4 will (naming sons Thomas, Benjamin, Richard, William, and Peter specifically) — the tree currently shows only one child (Peter C. Cheeseman) for this couple, understating the sibling group.
- Peter C. Cheeseman appears to have had **three wives**: an unnamed first wife (mother of the children named in his 1786 will), Ann Ellis (m. c.1746 — matching the tree's listed spouse), and Christian Fisher Roe (m. 5 Feb 1781). The tree lists only Ann Hannah Ellis; it is not clear from this research pass whether Richard D. Cheeseman Jr. (the tree's gen-8 link) was a child of the first wife or of Ann Ellis specifically.
- Peter C. Cheeseman purchased 350 acres from Henry Roe on 22 Aug 1753 and was among the founding settlers of Turnersville, NJ — a specific land record not present in the tree.
- Peter Thomas Cheeseman "Major" (gen. 7) is independently attested in compiled sources as having acquired and operated a stone gristmill near Turnersville by 1814, and outside compiled trees (Geni, ancestry-derived WikiTree pages) list a much larger set of children than the tree's single entry (Catherine): Jonathan W., Jacob S., Bathsheba, Thomas, Richard William, and Benjamin S. Cheeseman are all named as his children/siblings-of-Peter-T. in various secondary compilations. These have **not** been independently verified in this pass and may reflect a different generation or a spouse mix-up (see Open Questions).
- A scholarly, citable article exists specifically on this family: "Thomas Cheesman, Shipwright of New York and His Family," *New York Genealogical and Biographical Record*, Jan. 1978 — not accessed in full text during this research pass but identified as a strong lead for verifying the immigrant generation.
- A full published genealogy exists — Albert Stirling Adams, *The Cheesman Family: Three Centuries in Southern New Jersey* (Gloucester County Historical Society, 1985, 284pp.) — held at Archive.org (item `cheesmanfamilyth00adam`) but currently **restricted/unavailable** for online reading (the item returns "not available due to issues with the item's content"); this is the primary compiled source underlying most WikiTree/Long Island Surnames entries for this line and would be the best next step for deeper verification.

## Duplicate-record / data-quality flags

- The tree's spelling inconsistently alternates between "Cheeseman" and "Cheesman" generation to generation (e.g., "Cheeseman" for gens 7–9, "Cheesman" for gen 11); this is consistent with how period records themselves spell the name inconsistently, but it also increases the risk that this family has **unmerged duplicate person-records elsewhere in the tree** under the alternate spelling — worth a dedicated dedup pass across "Cheesman"/"Cheeseman"/"Chesman" variants.
- Conflicting spouse/children sets exist across secondary sources for Peter Thomas Cheeseman "Major" (Elizabeth Jackson per the tree vs. "Eliza (Jackson) Cheesman" vs. "Sarah (Smallwood) Cheesman" appearing in different aggregator search snippets) — this smells like two or more unmerged Ancestry-style trees describing the same man with different (possibly sequential) wives, or an actual duplicate/confused person-record. Needs primary-record resolution (marriage record, will, or Find a Grave) before treating any single spouse name as settled beyond "Elizabeth Jackson," which is at least consistent with the tree.
- One search result described a Thomas Cheesman "alive as late as 7 Dec 1736" per Hempstead town records, versus the consensus death year of 1713 used by the tree, Find a Grave, and most compiled pedigrees. This is very likely a case of two different men of the same name (the immigrant Thomas, d. 1713, vs. a son or grandson also named Thomas) being conflated in at least one online tree — flagged here so it is not mistaken for evidence against the tree's 1713 death date, which is corroborated by Find a Grave memorial 103852022.

## Documentation status of each parent-child link (generations 7 -> 11)

1. Peter Thomas Cheeseman Major (g7, 1771–1856) <- Richard D. Cheeseman Jr. (g8, 1749–1799): **documented** (matching birthplace "Cheesman Mill/Old Upton," corroborated by independent WikiTree/Ancestry-derived compilation and Find a Grave).
2. Richard D. Cheeseman Jr. (g8) <- Peter C. Cheeseman (g9, 1704–1788): **documented** (Peter's 1786 will names a son Richard; matches birthplace Old Upton).
3. Peter C. Cheeseman (g9) <- Richard Cheesman Sr. (g10, 1674–1744, missing from tree file): **documented** but the link is currently unrepresented as a node in the supplied tree data — needs the missing generation inserted.
4. Richard Cheesman Sr. (g10) <- Thomas Cheesman (g11, immigrant, 1640–1713): **documented** (Richard's birth record and Thomas's 1698 Hempstead census listing of children agree).
5. Thomas Cheesman (g11) <- Ephraim Cheesman & Ann Rason of Kingston-upon-Thames (implied g12, not in tree as a full record): **inferred/compiled-secondary** — consistent across multiple aggregator sites but not verified in this pass against an English parish register; treat as plausible, not proven.

**The solidly documented line, verifiable through published/compiled genealogies with primary-record citations (wills, town records, church marriage record), runs from Thomas Cheesman the immigrant (g11, arrived/born England c.1640, d. Hempstead LI 1713) down to Peter Thomas Cheeseman "Major" (g7, d. 1856).** Beyond Thomas Cheesman, the line becomes lore/compiled-only (Ephraim Cheesman and Ann Rason of Kingston-upon-Thames) and was not independently confirmed against English parish records in this research pass.

## Open questions

- Confirm which of Peter C. Cheeseman's three wives (unnamed first wife, Ann Ellis, or Christian Fisher Roe) was the mother of Richard D. Cheeseman Jr. — the tree currently attributes Richard's parentage to Ann Hannah Ellis, but Richard's birth year (1749) predates Peter's marriage to Ann Ellis (c.1746) only narrowly, and it's unclear if that marriage date is itself firm.
- Resolve the conflicting wife name for Peter Thomas Cheeseman Major: is "Elizabeth Jackson" (per tree) the same person as "Eliza (Jackson) Cheesman" reported elsewhere, and is "Sarah (Smallwood) Cheesman" a second wife, an unrelated duplicate record, or a data-entry error in an outside tree?
- Verify Richard D. Cheeseman Jr.'s birth date (6 Oct vs. 16 Oct 1749) against a primary record (parish/town register or Bible record) if one survives.
- Obtain and check the Adams 1985 book (*The Cheesman Family: Three Centuries in Southern New Jersey*) and the 1978 NYG&B article "Thomas Cheesman, Shipwright of New York and His Family" directly — both are the authoritative compiled sources behind almost every online secondary citation used here, but neither was accessible in full text during this pass.
- Confirm the English origin generation (Ephraim Cheesman & Ann Rason of Kingston-upon-Thames) against English parish registers; this generation is currently supported only by American compiled/secondary genealogies (Geni, WikiTree), not by any English primary source seen in this research.
- Add the missing generation-10 record (Richard Cheesman Sr. and Hannah Stringham/Halstead) to the tree so the generation-9-to-11 chain is structurally complete.
