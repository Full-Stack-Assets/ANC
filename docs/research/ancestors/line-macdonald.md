# The MacDonald Line (4)

## Overview

This "line" is actually two unconnected MacDonald threads bundled under one surname label, and neither is well documented. Thread A is a Scottish Highland family from North Uist, Inverness-shire — Murdo MacDonald & Kate MacInnes (gen 8) → son Neil MacDonald (gen 7, b. abt 1785) m. Mary → son Laughlan Macdonald. Thread B is a claimed English gentry family — Charles MacDonald & Hannah Mc Donald (gen 10) → daughter Charlotte Ann MacDonald (gen 9, b. 1730 London, d. 1816 Southampton) m. "General William Charles Howe Taylor" → daughter Charlotte Howe Taylor. Every single event on all four tree records is flagged `[unsourced]`, and web research shows why: Thread B's central claim — that the well-documented New Brunswick pioneer "Charlotte Taylor" (1755–1841) was daughter of a "General Howe Taylor" and a MacDonald wife — is treated by dedicated genealogical researchers as debunked family legend, not fact. Thread A could not be corroborated or refuted with the sources available in this pass; it remains simply unsourced.

## Verified facts

- All four people in this tree segment (Murdo MacDonald, Neil MacDonald, Charles MacDonald, Charlotte Ann MacDonald) have `sources: []` in the tree data and every event is explicitly marked `[unsourced]`. This is an accurate self-assessment by the tree, not an oversight — no independent record substantiates any of them. *(tree data itself)*
- Charlotte Howe Taylor, listed in the tree only as a child's name, corresponds to a real, well-documented historical woman: Charlotte Taylor (b. 5 Nov 1755, baptized Holborn, Middlesex; d. 25 Apr 1841, Tabusintac, Northumberland Co., New Brunswick), an early settler remembered locally as "the Mother of Tabusintac." Her life is documented via New Brunswick provincial marriage records, W.F. Ganong's "The History of Tabusintac" (*Acadiensis*), and an 1841 Royal Gazette obituary, and is the subject of Sally Armstrong's popular biography *The Nine Lives of Charlotte Taylor* (2007). *(WikiTree Taylor-13772; charlottetaylor.ca)*
- Specialist research into Charlotte Taylor's parentage (WikiTree profile Taylor-13772, and the dedicated research site charlottetaylor.ca) concludes there is **no record of a "General Howe Taylor"** in British or North American military or vital records, and states plainly "there is no strong evidence" for the "General Charles William Howe Taylor 1730–1788" parentage story. *(WikiTree Taylor-13772; charlottetaylor.ca "Before Blake")*
- The same research identifies the more probable parents of the historical Charlotte Taylor, based on period London christening records, as **Charles Taylor and Ann** (surname of "Ann" unconfirmed), baptized 9 Nov 1755 at Holborn Lying-In Hospital — with no MacDonald surname anywhere in that reconstruction, and the researchers explicitly noting that oral tradition never even preserved a story about the mother's identity. *(charlottetaylor.ca "Before Blake")*
- FamilySearch and Geni both host user-submitted profiles for "Gen. William Charles Howe Taylor (1730–1788)" — but these are secondary, uncited compiled-tree pages, not primary-source confirmations; their existence shows the legend is widely copied between family trees, not that it is true. *(ancestors.familysearch.org/en/GQFX-RY8; geni.com profile 6000000153113826886)*

## Corrections to the tree

- **Wrong:** Charlotte Ann MacDonald (m. "General William Charles Howe Taylor") → correct/documented mother of Charlotte Howe Taylor (the historical Charlotte Taylor of Tabusintac).
  **Correct:** This parentage is unsubstantiated family legend. Dedicated genealogical research (charlottetaylor.ca; WikiTree Taylor-13772) finds no record of a "General Howe Taylor" ever existing and identifies Charles Taylor & Ann — no MacDonald connection — as the more probable parents based on period christening records.
  **Evidence:** WikiTree Taylor-13772 ("no strong evidence," "no record of a General Howe Taylor"); charlottetaylor.ca "Before Blake" (discussion of 11 candidate Charlotte Taylor christenings, 1749–1755, with Charles Taylor/Ann as the leading candidate).

- **Suspicious/likely artifact:** Charlotte Ann MacDonald's tree birth year of **1730** is identical to the birth year given (in the same legendary tradition, per FamilySearch/Geni) for her supposed husband, "General William Charles Howe Taylor (1730–1788)." This looks like a copy-paste/date-conflation artifact within the legend itself rather than an independently attested birth year for a woman named Charlotte Ann MacDonald — no source ties 1730 specifically to her.

- **Structural/duplicate-record issue:** Thread A (North Uist, gen 7–8, Highland Scotland, roughly 1700s–1785) and Thread B (Southampton/London, gen 9–10, roughly 1710–1816) share only a surname and do not connect anywhere in the supplied data or in web research. Grouping them as a single "MacDonald line (4)" misrepresents them as one continuous lineage. Recommend splitting into two separate line records (e.g., "MacDonald of North Uist" and "MacDonald/Howe Taylor legend line") so the unrelated threads aren't conflated in reports or charts.

## New findings

- The historical "Charlotte Taylor" (tree's "Charlotte Howe Taylor") married four times and had roughly ten children: (1) Pad Willisams/Williams (m. c.1774) — daughter Elizabeth Williams b. 1772; (2) John Robert Blake (m. c.12 Jun 1776) — children John, Mary Ann, Robert Blake; (3) William Wishart (m. c.1783) — son William Wishart Jr.; (4) Philip Hierlihy (m. 11 Sep 1787) — children Philip Jr., Elenor, James William, Honoria, Charlotte Mary. None of this is reflected in the tree, which stops at "Charlotte Howe Taylor" as a bare name.
- A secondary/tertiary compiled tree (genealogieonline.nl, "McDonald and Potts family tree") also carries the "Charlotte Howe Taylor (1755–1841)" figure under the same unverified MacDonald parentage — evidence the legend has propagated across multiple amateur compiled trees, reinforcing that its wide circulation is not itself evidence of truth.
- No trace of Murdo MacDonald, Kate MacInnes, Neil MacDonald, or Laughlan Macdonald was found in any searchable secondary source (WikiTree MacDonald genealogy category, Highland Family History Society pedigree indexes, Clan Donald histories on Electric Scotland, rootschat forum threads on North Uist MacDonalds). This branch appears to rest solely on unsourced family tradition with no corroborating trace located in this pass.
- No immigrant-ancestor / crossing-to-America event applies to this line as given — Thread A stays in the Scottish Highlands (Inverness-shire) through the generations supplied, and Thread B is English (London/Southampton/Lanarkshire) with the one link to North America (Charlotte Taylor's emigration to New Brunswick) resting on a mother identity that most likely isn't a MacDonald at all.

## Where the documented line ends

- **Thread A (North Uist):** No documentation found at any generation. The line is entirely unsourced from Murdo MacDonald (gen 8) forward; nothing here should be treated as documented.
- **Thread B (Southampton/London):** The only genuinely documented person in this thread's descent is Charlotte Taylor (1755–1841) herself, and specialist research disputes that she was a MacDonald descendant at all. Charles MacDonald (gen 10) and Charlotte Ann MacDonald (gen 9) as presented have no documentary support; the "General Howe Taylor" marriage that would connect them into Charlotte's ancestry is the specific claim genealogists have been unable to substantiate.

General William Charles Howe Taylor" story, and how does it relate to the "Kenneth MacInnes" family-history compilation referenced by charlottetaylor.ca researchers as a source of Taylor family lore? (Possible, unconfirmed coincidence: the surname MacInnes appears independently in both unrelated MacDonald threads — Kate MacInnes in Thread A, and a MacInnes-compiled genealogy behind the Thread B legend. No evidence connects the two; flagged only in case it points to a shared secondary-source compiler.)
- Is "Charles MacDonald" (b. abt 1710 Southampton, d. Barony, Lanarkshire) an independently attested person, or was he constructed solely to give Charlotte Ann MacDonald a MacDonald pedigree?
