# Jonathan Pine (abt. 1661–1694) — Generation 10

## Overview

Jonathan Pine was born about 1661 in Hempstead, Queens/Nassau Co., Long
Island, New York, son of Capt. James Pine/Pyne (an immigrant settler of
Hempstead) and Susan Armitage, and died 17 December 1694 in Cape May, Cape
May Co., New Jersey, at about age 33. He moved from Hempstead to Cape May
in the mid-1680s, was appointed Constable of Cape May by the colonial New
Jersey legislature on 25 November 1685, and sold off the Hempstead lands he
had inherited from his father in 1690–92 after resettling. This tree carries
him as **two separate person records** that share identical name and dates —
a duplicate-import, not two different men — and this dossier resolves which
copy is canonical for the direct line.

## Verified facts

- **Jonathan Pine, b. abt. 1661 Hempstead, d. 17 Dec 1694 Cape May, NJ**, son of Capt. James Pine and Susan Armitage. [documented — WikiTree Pine-62, Geni]
- **He relocated from Hempstead, Long Island to Cape May, NJ** and was made **Constable of Cape May** by the New Jersey colonial legislature on 25 November 1685. [documented — WikiTree Pine-62]
- **He sold his Hempstead lands (inherited from his father) in 1690–92**, after establishing himself in Cape May — consistent with a permanent relocation rather than a temporary posting. [documented — WikiTree Pine-62]
- **His father, Capt. James Pine/Pyne**, was born 4 June 1630 Exeter, Devon, England and died 12 Oct 1686 Hempstead, NY; married Susan Armitage (b. 1632 Bradford, Yorkshire, England; d. 1 Jan 1685 Hempstead) in 1652, Hempstead. Both are documented in this tree's own records (`I182625984824`, `I182625984903`) with citations to *U.S. and International Marriage Records, 1560-1900* (S516008989) and the Geneanet Community Trees Index (S508118847), and James/Susan's record lists **both** duplicate Jonathan Pine ids among their twelve children — direct internal evidence that the two Jonathan Pine records are one person imported twice. [documented]
- **His son, Jonathan Pine (b. 1687, Cape May, NJ; d. 26 May 1733, Elizabethtown, Essex Co., NJ)**, is independently confirmed by WikiTree (Pine-62) at the same birth year and place as this tree's own record (`I182620314823`) — the birth immediately follows the father's move to Cape May, consistent with the relocation timeline above. [documented]

## Corrections to the tree

- **Duplicate-person, not two men**: `I182620321916` ("JONATHAN PINE," all-caps import, unsourced, single parent link, one child) and `I182625984979` ("Jonathan Pine," sourced to Geneanet S508118847, two parent links, no children) are the same individual, imported twice from what were likely two different source trees merged into this pedigree. Their shared father's record independently lists both ids as children, which is itself sufficient proof of the duplication regardless of the external research below.
- **Canonical record: `I182620321916`.** Although it carries no source citation of its own, it is the copy that actually continues the direct line: its listed son, Jonathan Pine (`I182620314823`, b. 1687 Cape May), is externally confirmed (WikiTree Pine-62) and itself continues, already-traced in this tree, through Benjamin C Pine (`I182541957605`) and John Pine (`I182541932596`) to Ann Pine (`I182197732094`), who married John Albertson (`I182197731994`) — the couple at the center of this tree's already-resolved **gen 6 John Albertson & Ann Pine duplicate-couple** case (see `docs/research/direct-line-issues.md`, "Interpretations") — and on down the documented Albertson spine to the present day. This mirrors the pattern already established for several other duplicate-person resolutions in this tree (e.g. Samuel Jacobsson, gen 7; Britta Gabrielsdotter, gen 8): canonicalness is not about which copy has a source tag, it's about which copy actually connects to a verified descendant.
- **Duplicate record: `I182625984979`.** It carries a real citation (Geneanet Community Trees Index, S508118847) and both parent links, but lists no children at all — a dead end in this tree's graph, disconnected from the line that reaches the Albertsons.
