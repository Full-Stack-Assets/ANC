# Fragments Batch 00 — Triage Dossier (40 people, generations 7–12)

## Overview

This batch is a grab-bag of small surname fragments (Grossmann, Stowell, Cheesman,
Andersson, Austin/Austen, Eichorn/Acorn, Albertson, Allen, Anderson, Appelquist,
Arfvidsson, Armstronge, Bull, Burn, Burrough, Balchin, Barker, Bassett, Bayers,
Beatty, Bellen) spanning colonial New Jersey/Massachusetts Quakers, Kent/Warwickshire
England gentry and clothiers, Scottish Lowlands families, Swedish Bohuslän/Västra
Götaland parish lines, Broad Bay (Waldoboro, Maine) German settlers, and a Loyalist
New Brunswick family. Per the triage brief, all 40 were assessed for plausibility from
tree data alone; 9 of the most findable/notable were then checked against 1–3 web
sources each. No living people appear in this fragment set (all individuals have
death years, are pre-1940 births with no modern indicators, or are clearly historical).

## Researched in depth (9 of 40)

### 3/24/5/26. The Cheesman and Austen/Austin lines (Kent & Surrey, England)

- **Ephraim ("Eframe/Efraime/Ephriam") Cheesman** (c.1610 Kingston-upon-Thames, Surrey – bur. 19 Jan 1657, Kingston) is documented on WikiTree (Cheeseman-215), Geni, and Find A Grave as **Ephraim Cheeseman**, son of Thomas Cheesman and Mary Lilley, m. Anne Rason 18 Jul 1639 at Kingston-upon-Thames — matching the tree's parents and spouse closely (tree spells spouse "Anne Rasin (Rason\Roison)"). One WikiTree note flags some cross-tree confusion about whether Thomas Cheesman was Ephraim's father or son; the tree's own listing (parents: Thomas Cheesman/Mary Lilley; child: Thomas Cheesman) reproduces exactly this recurring ambiguity in the public record, not a fresh error.
- **Francis Austin/Austen Sr.** (bap. 1 Sep 1644 Goudhurst, Kent – d. Mar 1687, will proved 1688 Canterbury) and his father **Alexander Austen** (bap. 22 Jun 1606 Goudhurst – d. 1645) belong to a documented Kentish clothier family. Geni/WikiTree (Austin-4030) and the "Landed families of Britain and Ireland" blog independently confirm: Francis Austen, clothier of Grovehurst, Horsmonden, married Elizabeth on 30 Mar 1668 at Horsmonden (the tree's "spouse: Elizabeth m. 1668 @ Horsmonden" is exactly this, and is the one event in this cluster the tree itself already marks **[sourced]**, citing *England, Select Marriages, 1538-1973*), with a son Francis baptized 4 Jun 1677 (tree gives 1672 for son Francis Austin's birth — a ~5-year discrepancy worth flagging). Alexander's father is given elsewhere as John Austen (c.1560–1621), a Goudhurst/Horsmonden yeoman-clothier — broadly consistent with the tree's "John Austin" (1577–1657) as Alexander's father, though the birth year for John differs by ~17 years across sources.
- **Notable finding:** the landed-families blog is explicit that this same Horsmonden/Grovehurst Austen clothier family is the **great-great-grandfather line of novelist Jane Austen** (via a different, non-emigrant branch — Francis Austen 1698–1791, Jane's great-uncle, descends from a later Horsmonden Francis Austen). This does **not** mean the tree's ancestors are Jane Austen's direct ancestors — the emigrant branch (Francis Austin Jr. b.1672, to Evesham, Burlington Co., NJ) and the gentry branch that produced the novelist both radiate from the same 16th/17th-century Goudhurst-Horsmonden Austen stock, but the exact connecting point was not confirmed in this pass. Flagged as an open question, not a claimed fact.

### 10/11/12. The Albertson family (Quaker, West New Jersey)

- **Josiah Albertson** (b. Oct 1738 – d. ~Dec 1782/7 Mar 1783, Timber Creek/Gloucester Co., NJ) is documented on Geni as "Josiah Alberson (Albertson) Jr." with a will dated Dec 1782 and estate inventory dated 7 Jan 1783, implying death in **late 1782 or very early January 1783** — the tree's death date of "7 Mar 1783" is plausible but runs slightly later than the probate paper trail suggests. This is a minor date discrepancy, not a fundamental identity problem; the tree's extensive source list (Quaker Meeting Records, NJ wills, Find A Grave, two published local/family histories) indicates this branch has already had real archival work done on it.
- **William Albertson** (the immigrant, d. 1709) is a genuinely well-documented figure: multiple independent sources (rootsweb, Geni, WikiTree Alberson-96, FamilySearch, and the digitized *Burlington Court Book*) describe him as a **Quaker weaver who emigrated from Castledermot, Ireland, arriving in West New Jersey in 1681/82**, settling in Newton Township, Gloucester Co., later removing to Poquessing, Bucks Co., Pennsylvania, where he died in 1709 owning mills and land. He was a founding trustee of Newton Meeting (1681) and served in the West Jersey colonial legislature (1685).

### 36/35/37. The Bassett family of Lynn, Massachusetts

- **Capt. William Bassett** (bap. 30 May 1624, Dorking, Surrey, England – d. 31 Mar 1703, Lynn, Essex Co., MA) is extensively documented (WikiTree Bassett-464, FamilySearch, Geni, the Bassett Family Association site, Find A Grave). He emigrated to Massachusetts Bay in 1635 on the ship *Abigail*, son of Roger and Ann (Holland) Bassett — matching the tree's parents exactly. He served in King Philip's War (Sergeant 1675, Quartermaster 1682, later Ensign) and held Lynn town offices (constable, selectman, juryman).

### 39. Honoria Beatty / Hierlihy family (Miramichi & Tabusintac, New Brunswick)

- **Honoria "Honour" Hierlihy** is a documented daughter of **Philip Hierlihy** (Loyalist sergeant, Prince of Wales American Regiment, settled Miramichi 1783/married 1787) and **Charlotte Taylor**, the well-known "Mother of Tabusintac." The Charlotte Taylor local-history project (charlottetaylor.ca) lists her among the five Miramichi-born children but gives her birth as **1792**, while the tree (and Ancestry/WikiTree-style trees) give **4 Mar 1794** — a two-year discrepancy between the dedicated local-history site and the broader online-tree consensus (see Open Questions).

## Corrections to the tree

- **Capt. William Bassett Sr., name field "... Gunsmith Bassett Sr ++ (Pilgrim) Sr." -> drop "Gunsmith"/"Pilgrim" epithets (unsupported for this individual).** Evidence: independent sources for this William Bassett (b.1624 Dorking, arrived 1635 on the *Abigail*, d.1703 Lynn) describe him as a militia officer and town official, not a gunsmith, and do not call him a Mayflower/early Plymouth Pilgrim. The "gunsmith" and "Pilgrim" labels match a **different, earlier William Bassett (c.1590–1667)** who came to Plymouth on the *Fortune* — a distinct person per Wikipedia's "William Bassett (d. 1667)" article. The tree's name field appears to have merged the identities of two same-named Bassetts.
- **William Albertson's tree birthplace "1635 @ Bybury, Bucks, Pennsylvania, USA" is anachronistic/likely wrong -> best-documented origin is Castledermot, Ireland (Quaker meeting).** Evidence: Bucks County, PA did not exist in 1635 (Pennsylvania wasn't founded until 1681/82), so a birth there is impossible; multiple independent genealogies instead trace him to the Castledermot/Dublin Quaker meeting in Ireland, from which he emigrated in 1681/82. The tree's alternate unsourced entry ("1635 @ Stemeyck, Hollande") may reflect a genuine, older genealogical dispute (Dutch vs. Irish origin) rather than a simple error, so this is downgraded to a flag rather than a hard correction — but the "Bucks, Pennsylvania" entry specifically should be removed as impossible.
- **Francis Austin (son, tree b. "4 Jun 1672 @ Horsmonden") likely should read 1677, not 1672.** Evidence: the documented baptismal record for Francis Austen Sr.'s son Francis is 4 Jun 1677 at Horsmonden (per WikiTree/Geni), a 5-year difference from the tree's date — possibly a transcription slip (1672 vs 1677) rather than a different child.

## New findings (facts not in the tree)

- William Albertson was a founding trustee of Newton Meeting (Quaker), Gloucester Co., NJ, established 1681, and served in the West Jersey colonial legislature in 1685 — civic/religious detail absent from the tree's bare vital-events list.
- Capt. William Bassett of Lynn held a documented military career in King Philip's War (Sergeant 1675, Quartermaster 1682, later Ensign) and served Lynn as constable and selectman — none of which appears in the tree's spare event list.
- The Horsmonden/Goudhurst Austen clothier family (ancestors of Francis Austin Sr. and Alexander Austen in this tree) is the same broad Kentish family that later produced novelist Jane Austen's great-uncle, per the "Landed families of Britain and Ireland" compiled history — an intriguing but unconfirmed possible wider-family connection, not a direct-line claim.
- Philip Hierlihy died around 1802 in a boating accident and was buried at Bartibog, Miramichi; his wife Charlotte Taylor, "Mother of Tabusintac," lived until 1840 — neither death detail is in the tree (which only carries the children's data via Honoria Beatty's parent fields).

## Open questions

- Is the tree's "Ann Austin" (b. 1703 Evesham, Burlington Co., NJ, d. 1767, m. Josiah Albertson 1727) definitely a distinct person from the famous Quaker missionary **Ann Austin** who, with Mary Fisher, was the first Quaker to be imprisoned in Boston in 1656? The dates make them clearly different women (the 1656 Ann Austin was already "stricken in years" with five children by 1656, decades before the tree's Ann Austin was even born), but because both share the surname and Quaker affiliation, this is worth a one-line disambiguation note in the tree to prevent future confusion.
- Honoria Beatty/Hierlihy's birth year: tree and most online trees say 4 Mar 1794; the dedicated Charlotte Taylor local-history site (charlottetaylor.ca) says 1792. Which is correct is unresolved from this pass — the site may be conflating her with sister Charlotte Mary (b. 1794).
- Was the discrepancy between Josiah Albertson's will (Dec 1782) / inventory (7 Jan 1783) and the tree's stated death date (7 Mar 1783) simply the interval between drafting a will and actual death, or a transcription slip of "Jan" vs "Mar"? Not resolved.
- Is there a real, sourceable connecting link between this tree's Kentish Austen line (Alexander Austen → Francis Austin Sr. → Francis Austin Jr., emigrated to NJ) and the landed Austen family of Horsmonden/Grovehurst that produced Jane Austen's great-uncle — or do the two branches only share a distant common Goudhurst ancestor? Not established here.
- No independent verification was attempted this pass for the 31 people marked "plausible" or "thin" in the triage table; a follow-up pass could prioritize the Swedish parish lines (Andersson/Arfvidsson/Rånge — good record availability in Swedish church books) and the PEI Eichorn/Acorn/Applequest cluster (Loyalist-era PEI records).
