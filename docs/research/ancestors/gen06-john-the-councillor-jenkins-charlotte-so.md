# John "The Councillor" Jenkins (1812–1894) & Charlotte Sophia Murray (c.1819–bef.1891) — Generation 6

No "Michael Murphy" or "Catherine Kane" appears anywhere in the research corpus's item files (checked all `items/*.json`). ## Overview

John Jenkins, nicknamed "the Councillor," was born about 1812 at Mount Albion, Queens County, Prince Edward Island, the son of George Jenkins and Margaret Acorn, and a grandson of Nicolaus Henckell (anglicized "Jenkins"), a Hessian regimental drummer who settled on the Island after the American Revolution. He married Charlotte Sophia Murray on 21 March 1838 at Charlottetown, PEI; she was born about 1819 in the Tabusintac/Miramichi area of Northumberland County, New Brunswick, the daughter of John Murray and Honoria Hierlihy. The couple raised at least eleven children in Queens and Kings Counties, PEI (Mount Albion/Birch Hill, Lot 49, then Greek River, Lot 63). John died 12 Feb 1894 at Greek River; Charlotte's death is undocumented but must have preceded the 1891 census, in which John appears as a widower — contradicting the tree's claim that she also died in 1894.

## Verified facts

- **John's parentage and birthplace**: son of George Jenkins and Margaret (Acorn) Jenkins "of Mount Albion, PEI." Source: WikiTree profile Jenkins-6778, citing "The Descendants of Johan Heinrich Henckell" (Hazel Sanford, P.E.I. Family Lineages, Island Register). This is a compiled/secondary source but is itself grounded in PEI Public Archives baptismal, marriage, death, and census citations throughout.
- **Marriage to Charlotte Sophia Murray, 21 Mar 1838, Charlottetown, Queens, PEI**: "Prince Edward Island Marriage Registers, 1832-1888," FamilySearch, ark:/61903/1:1:QVBJ-KF9K, citing PEI Public Archives, Charlottetown; FHL microfilm 1,630,091. This is a **primary marriage-register source**, not merely secondary.
- **John's death, 12 Feb 1894, Greek River, Lot 63, Kings County, PEI**: matches the tree's sourced entry; corroborated by WikiTree biography (citing the Henckell descendants compilation).
- **1881 Census household**: John and Charlotte Jenkins enumerated together with their youngest children — District Queens, Sub-District Lot 49, Family No. 4, Page 2, Microfilm C-13164, RG31 Statistics Canada, Item 70086. (Confirms the couple still together and Charlotte still living as of the 1881 census.)
- **Charlotte's parents were John Murray and Honoria Hierlihy, not "John Murray and Honoria Beatty" as recorded in the tree.** John Murray (b. 21 Aug 1791, Bedeque, Prince Co., PEI; d. c.1821, Tabusintac, Northumberland, NB) married Honoria Hierlihy about 1813 in Tabusintac; Honoria was daughter of Philip Hierlihy and Charlotte Howe Taylor. Source: WikiTree Murray-2863, citing New Brunswick Genealogical Society files (MC1/Murray #2, MC80/1535 "Descendants of John Murray, United Empire Loyalist") and a 1810 NB land grant record (Provincial Archives of New Brunswick, RS686, Vol. E, p.272, Grant #568, Tabusintac, Northumberland Co.).
- **Charlotte was one of at least four children of John Murray & Honoria Hierlihy**: James (b. c.1814), Ann Jane (b. c.1815, m. John Blake), Mary (b. 1817, m. James Robertson of Birch Hill, PEI), and Charlotte Sophia (b. c.1819, m. John Jenkins). After John Murray's death c.1821, Honoria remarried Ephraim Gay in 1822 (per the 1851 and 1861 New Brunswick censuses, Alnwick Sub-district, Northumberland Co., cited on WikiTree Murray-2863).
- **John "the Councillor" was a widower by the time of the 1891 Census** — WikiTree biography (Jenkins-6778) explicitly states this, citing the 1891 Census, District Queen's #135, Sub-District Lot 48, Family 28, Microfilm T-6384, Item 3221186, which shows "John Sr and John jr's family" (i.e., John Sr. living without Charlotte). This directly contradicts the tree's assertion that Charlotte died in 1894 — see Corrections below.
- **John's paternal grandparents**: Nicolaus Henckell (anglicized "Nicolaus/Nicholas Jenkins"), b. 31 Mar 1755, Treysa, Hessen, Germany; d. 15 Jul 1823, Birch Hill, Lot 49, Queens Co., PEI — a former Hessian regimental drummer (3rd Company, Regiment von Knyphausen) who emigrated after service in the American Revolutionary War and settled first at Vernon River, Lot 49 (c.1785-87), then Cherry Valley, then Pownal/Birch Hill. He married (2nd) Elizabeth Pfluger (widow Baum) about 1783 in PEI. Source: WikiTree Henckell-1, citing the same Henckell descendants compilation, PEI Public Archives death/baptismal records, and the 1798 PEI census ("Nicks Jenkins," Lot 49, household of 11).
- **John's paternal grandmother, Margaret Acorn's parentage**: George Jenkins (b. c.1785, d. 1836) married Margaret Acorn, **daughter of John Acorn and Eleanor Williams**. "Acorn" is the anglicized form of the German surname **Eichorn** — John Acorn appears elsewhere in these same lines as "John Eichorn, U.E.L." (b. 1761, Broadbay, Lincoln Co., Massachusetts Bay Colony [later Waldoboro, Maine]; d. 19 Jun 1857), consistent with the Palatine-German settlement of the Broad Bay/Waldoboro area and its Loyalist emigration to PEI. Source: WikiTree Henckell-1 and Jenkins-6778 (Grandparents panel).
- **A book-length secondary compilation exists**: *Nicolaus Henckell the Hessian: A Genealogy of Jenkins Families of Prince Edward Island*, by Douglas B. MacDonald (WorldCat OCLC 445232249), which appears to be the ultimate source behind much of the online Henckell/Jenkins material (including the "Doug's Genealogy" blog, jenkins-ballem.blogspot.com, evidently by the same researcher/family).

## Corrections to the tree

- **Charlotte Sophia Murray's death year: tree says 1894 (unsourced) → should be corrected to "before 1891" (year/date otherwise undetermined).** The 1891 Census of Canada (taken April 1891) shows John Jenkins as a widower, per the WikiTree biography's citation of that census return (District Queen's #135, Sub-District Lot 48, Family 28). Since John and Charlotte are shown together as late as the 1881 census, her death falls in the 1881–1891 window, not 1894. The tree's "1894" appears to be an error, possibly conflated with John's own well-documented 1894 death. **Status: documented correction** (primary census citation contradicts the tree), though the exact date/place of Charlotte's death remains unfound (see Open Questions).
- **Charlotte's mother: tree says "Honoria Beatty" → should be Honoria Hierlihy** (daughter of Philip Hierlihy and Charlotte Howe Taylor, of Tabusintac, Northumberland Co., New Brunswick). Hierlihy is a documented Loyalist-era New Brunswick surname (cf. "Hierlihy's Rangers/Independent Companies" and the Loggieville/Miramichi Hierlihy family), and the identification is backed by NB land-grant and New Brunswick Genealogical Society file citations. "Beatty" does not appear in any source found and is likely a transcription or family-tree compilation error. **Status: documented correction** (secondary/compiled sources with archival citations; no single primary vital record was directly viewed).
- **John's birthplace: tree says "Vernon River Lot 49, Queens, PEI" → likely should be Mount Albion / Birch Hill, Lot 49, Queens, PEI.** WikiTree's biography (citing the Henckell descendants compilation) gives his birthplace as "Mount Albion, PEI," and separately shows his father George Jenkins located at "Birch Hill, Lot 49." Vernon River, Lot 49 was where great-grandfather Nicolaus Henckell first settled decades earlier (c.1785-87) before the family moved on to Cherry Valley and then Pownal/Birch Hill — the tree's birthplace may be an intergenerational conflation of the immigrant ancestor's landing place with the grandson's actual birthplace. Both Mount Albion and Birch Hill are localities within/adjacent to Lot 49, so this is a plausible mix-up rather than an unrelated place. **Status: inferred correction** (moderate confidence; no baptismal record for John himself was located to settle it definitively).
- **John's father's name: tree says "George Holland Jenkins" → no source found for the middle name "Holland."** Every primary-adjacent source found (WikiTree Jenkins-6778 and Henckell-1, both citing PEI Public Archives death/baptismal records) calls him simply "George Jenkins," born c.1783–85, died 1836. The middle name "Holland" may derive from an Ancestry Family Trees compilation not independently verified here. **Status: unconfirmed / possible error** — not corrected outright, but flagged as unsupported by the sources located.
- **"Maragaret Acorn Eachorn" (tree's rendering of John's grandmother) → Margaret Acorn, daughter of John Acorn (né Eichorn) and Eleanor Williams.** The tree's compound name appears to conflate Margaret's given/married name (Margaret Acorn) with her father's original German surname variant (Eichorn/Eachorn). This is not so much an error as a garbled merge of two data points; the underlying facts (anglicized-German Acorn/Eichorn family, PEI) are confirmed. **Status: clarified, not strictly a correction.**

Sources: WikiTree Jenkins-6778 biography, each citing PEI Public Archives baptismal/marriage records and 1881/1891 census returns (full citation list on the page).
- **Immigrant-ancestor story**: John's great-grandfather Nicolaus Henckell was a Hessian mercenary drummer who served with the Regiment von Knyphausen in the American Revolutionary War, arrived at Saint John's Island (PEI) in 1779 with Hessian/British forces, later married Elizabeth Robinson (1st) and then Elizabeth Pfluger (2nd, widow Baum) in PEI, worked as a shoemaker (trade learned from his own father, a master shoemaker in Treysa), and left a will (probated 1823, signed "Nicolaise Henckell") dividing his farm among his sons. A 19th-century Island newspaper notice ("A Long Lived Family") on his death, reproduced on WikiTree, lists the striking longevity of his children (Susannah reportedly still living at age 100; James at 88; Elizabeth at 81, etc.).
- **German-Palatine ancestry on both the Jenkins and Acorn sides**: the Jenkins line descends from Hessian immigrant Nicolaus Henckell; the Acorn line (Margaret Acorn, John's grandmother) descends from John Acorn/Eichorn, a German-Palatine-descended United Empire Loyalist from Broad Bay (Waldoboro), Massachusetts Bay Colony/Maine. Both surnames — Henckell→Jenkins and Eichorn→Acorn — are anglicization pairs, matching the pattern of name-variant families flagged for this project (cf. Prospero/Prospus/Prosper, Regan/Reagan, Hohmann/Hohman elsewhere in the tree).
- **Charlotte's birth family in New Brunswick**: her father John Murray (1791–c.1821) was himself the son of a John Murray (b. bef. 1761, Canonbie, Dumfriesshire, Scotland) and Eliza Chambers, who emigrated from Bedeque, PEI to Tabusintac, Northumberland Co., NB in 1799; John Jr. received a New Brunswick land grant in Tabusintac in 1810 (290 acres, Grant #568). Charlotte's siblings included Ann Jane (m. John Blake) and Mary (m. James Robertson of Birch Hill, PEI) — meaning Charlotte had a sister who also relocated to PEI's Birch Hill area, in the same neighborhood as the Jenkins family, plausibly explaining how John Jenkins and Charlotte Murray of New Brunswick came to meet.
- **Published secondary source**: *Nicolaus Henckell the Hessian: A Genealogy of Jenkins Families of Prince Edward Island* by Douglas B. MacDonald (OCLC 445232249) — a book-length treatment of this exact family line, likely the best next research step for primary-source-level detail (parish registers, wills, land grants) beyond what is indexed online.
- **"The Councillor" nickname is unexplained in every source found.** No source connects it to an actual Legislative Council seat (the PEI Legislative Assembly's own historical MLA biographies index does not include a John Jenkins matching this profile), so it may be a purely local/family nickname rather than a reference to political office. This is worth treating as folklore/legend pending better evidence.

## Open questions

1. **Exact date and place of Charlotte Sophia Murray's death.** Established only that it falls between the 1881 census (shown alive with John) and the 1891 census (John shown as widower). PEI death registration in this period was inconsistent/indexed only from 1906 for reliable civil registration; the pre-1906 PEI Death Index (unidentified newspaper source, as used elsewhere in these same source chains) or a Free Church of Scotland/Presbyterian burial register for Lot 49/Birch Hill would be the next place to check.
2. **George Jenkins's (John's father) exact birth and death dates are inconsistent between compiled sources**: one WikiTree page gives "1783 – 23 Aug 1836," another (citing the same underlying PEI death-index entry, which itself only records the year 1836) gives "b. c.1785, d. 30 Oct 1836." The underlying primary record is only a bare year; the day/month figures on both pages appear to be later, unsourced embellishments. Needs the actual pre-1906 PEI Death Index entry or church burial register to resolve.
3. **Origin of "George Holland Jenkins" middle name and "Honoria Beatty" surname** in the tree — likely both trace back to an uncorroborated Ancestry Family Trees compilation; the specific tree/user that introduced them was not identified.
4. **John Jenkins's own birth record**: no baptismal or birth record (as opposed to secondary compiled biography) was located pinning down his exact 1812 birth date or confirming Mount Albion vs. Vernon River as birthplace.
5. **"The Councillor" nickname's origin** — is it a genuine reference to some (perhaps unofficial or local) council role, or purely a family nickname/legend? Not resolved by any source found.
6. **Full extraction of the 1881 and 1891 census images** for the John Jenkins household (District Queens, Lot 49, Family 4, Microfilm C-13164, Item 70086 for 1881; District Queen's #135, Lot 48, Family 28, Microfilm T-6384, Item 3221186 for 1891) would likely nail down Charlotte's death window precisely and show the full household composition at each date.
7. **Burial location for John and Charlotte** — no Find A Grave memorials were located for either despite searching; a cemetery record from Greek River, Lot 63, Kings Co. (where John died) or Birch Hill/Mount Albion, Lot 49, Queens Co. would be worth checking against PEI cemetery transcription projects (e.g., islandregister.com cemetery pages).
