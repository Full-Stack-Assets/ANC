# Fragments Batch 04 — 40 Direct Ancestors, Generations 7–12

## Overview

This is a triage pass over 40 disconnected tree fragments (mostly single individuals or spouse-pairs, generations 7–12, roughly 1580–1850) spanning colonial Long Island/Massachusetts/New Jersey, England (Northumberland, Wiltshire, Yorkshire, Hampshire), Germany (Hesse, Bavaria, Baden), Ireland/New Brunswick/Nova Scotia (the Hierlihy Loyalist family), and Prince Edward Island (a Hessian-mercenary-turned-settler). Ten-plus threads were selected for targeted web verification based on unusual names, existing source citations, dedicated one-name/one-family websites, or internal red flags. Two colonial Long Island clusters (Horton and Hawkins) checked out cleanly against a published 1911 genealogy and WikiTree/Geni. A German Palatine-Hessian settler of PEI (Nicolaus Henckell/Jenkins) turned up a rich, independently documented biography including Revolutionary War military service not in the tree. The Hierlihy/Taylor cluster surfaced the batch's most serious problems: a likely conflation of two different real people into one "Cornelius Hierlihy Lt. Col," wrong parents for Philip Hierlihy, and a colorful "General William Charles Howe Taylor" that a dedicated biographical site explicitly debunks as family legend with no vital-record support.

## Verified facts

- **Capt. Zachariah Hawkins** was baptized 25 Oct 1639 in Charlestown, Suffolk Co., MA, son of Robert and Mary Hawkins (emigrated from England to Charlestown ca. 1635); he became an original proprietor of Brookhaven Township and settled at Setauket by 1655, married Mary Biggs, and his will was dated 17 Nov 1698 and proved 27 Apr 1699 (so his death is more precisely late 1698/early 1699, not simply "November 1698" as the tree's single death event implies). — [WikiTree: Hawkins-2913](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Hawkins-2913); [Geni: Zachariah Hawkins](https://www.geni.com/people/Zachariah-Hawkins/6000000003791670767)
- **Capt. Eleazor Howell Hawkins**, son of Zachariah Hawkins and Mary Biggs, was born 7 Aug 1688 in Setauket, Suffolk Co., NY and died 22 Sep 1772 — matching the tree's dates exactly. — [WikiTree: Hawkins-1031](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Hawkins-1031); [Geni: Capt. Eleazer Hawkins, Sr.](https://geni.com/people/Capt-Eleazer-Hawkins/6000000003300436579)
- **Capt. Barnabas Horton II** was born 1690 in Southold, Long Island, NY and died 9 Oct 1782 in Goshen, Orange Co., NY, with wife Mary Sweazy/Swazey — matching the tree's dates, places, and spouse exactly. He was a great-grandson of the original immigrant Barnabas Horton (b. 1600, one of the 13 founders of Southold, 1640). — [Horton Genealogy: Chronicles of the Descendants of Barnabas Horton (1911), Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/53259/53259-h/53259-h.htm); [Silas Horton family tree (GenoPro)](https://familytrees.genopro.com/azrael/2945739/HORTON-Silas-I579847433.htm)
- **Silas Horton Sr**, son of Barnabas Horton II and Mary Sweazy, is independently documented as born in Goshen, Orange Co., NY (one secondary source gives 25 Dec 1729, close to but not identical with the tree's primary "27 December 1730" — the tree itself already carries the 25 Dec 1729 variant as an alt event) and died 28 Feb 1820, age ~90, in Goshen — matching the tree's death year and place. — [WikiTree: Horton-4477](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Horton-4477)
- **Mary Elizabeth Haskell**'s parents, William Haskell of Gloucester, Essex Co., MA and Mary Tybbot(t), are independently documented as married 6 Nov 1643 with nine children including a daughter Mary who married a Dodge — consistent with the tree's parents ("Captain William Osborn Haskell" and "C25. Mary Tybott") and spouse (Edward Dodge). One source gives the daughter's marriage as April 1673 and does not corroborate "Osborn" as William Haskell's middle name (see Corrections). — [Empty Branches on the Family Tree: William Haskell & Mary Tybbot](https://emptybranchesonthefamilytree.com/2020/01/william-haskell-mary-tybbot-essex-county-ma/); [WikiTree: Haskell-233](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haskell-233)
- **Nicolaus (Henckell) Jenkins** was born 28/31 Mar 1755 in Treysa, Hessen, Germany and died 15 Jul 1823 at Birch Hill, Lot 49, Queens Co., Prince Edward Island — matching the tree's dates and death place. He married Elizabeth (Pfluger), previously widowed as "Baum," about 1783 at Birch Hill — consistent with the tree's spouse "Sabrina Elisabetha Pfluger." His will (probated 1823, signed "Nicolaise Henckell") names children Henry, George, Nicholas, William, James, John, Susannah, Catherine, Elizabeth, and Sarah — the tree's listed child "George Holland Jenkins" matches "George" among these. — [WikiTree: Henckell-1](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Henckell-1); [Geni: Nicholas Hanckell (Jenkins)](https://www.geni.com/people/Nicholas-Hanckell/6000000002209825699); [FamilySearch: Nicholas Jenkins (Henckel), 1755-1823](https://ancestors.familysearch.org/en/LCSC-WPR/nicholas-jenkins-henckel-1755-1823); [Doug's Genealogy blog, "Nicholas Henckel's Military Record"](http://jenkins-ballem.blogspot.com/2007/08/nicholas-henckels-military-record.html)
- **Philip Hierlihy**, baptized 14 May 1749 at St. Mary's Catholic parish, Cork, Ireland, served as a sergeant in the Prince of Wales American Regiment (a Loyalist unit) during the American Revolution, came to New Brunswick in 1783, married Charlotte Taylor in September 1787 at Miramichi, received a 1,214-acre Crown grant in 1798 along the Miramichi River with others, and shortly after led a small group to found Tabusintac, NB — consistent with the tree's spouse and general place/era. — [Charlotte Taylor: Her Life and Times — "The Hierlihy Years"](https://charlottetaylor.ca/hierlihy-years/)

## Corrections to the tree

- **Joanna Hale**'s tree-listed mother, "Nancy Joanna Cutler" -> genuinely uncertain / likely wrong. Independent sources identify her (as "Joanna Hale") only as possibly "sister of Robert Hale," a claim explicitly disputed by genealogist Douglas Richardson, who argues the first wife of her second husband John Penticost was *not* Edward Larkin's sister of that name. No source found gives a mother named "Nancy Joanna Cutler," and the given name "Nancy" is anachronistic for a woman supposedly born ca. 1616–1620 in Puritan New England (the name form doesn't appear in common use until the 18th century). Treat the parentage as unresolved rather than as "Cutler." — [WikiTree: Hale-991](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Hale-991)
- **Mary Elizabeth Haskell**'s father's middle name "Osborn" -> unsourced/disputed. A dedicated Haskell-family research page explicitly flags that a prior source reported "Osborn" as a Haskell middle name with no citation given. Treat as unverified rather than as a settled fact. — [Empty Branches on the Family Tree](https://emptybranchesonthefamilytree.com/2020/01/william-haskell-mary-tybbot-essex-county-ma/)
- **JOSEPH Hibbert** -> surname is almost certainly **Hibbard**, not Hibbert. The tree's own listed child is spelled "Jeremiah Hibbard," and the only period-appropriate published genealogy located for a Salem/Beverly, MA family of this name and era is "Genealogy of the Hibbard family, descendants of Robert Hibbard of Salem, Massachusetts" (1901). A Joseph of that family married Elizabeth Graves 20 Oct 1670 and had a will witnessed 30 Jun 1701 in Salem — consistent with the tree's death year 1701, and adding a spouse (Elizabeth Graves) not present in the tree at all. — [Genealogy of the Hibbard family (1901), Archive.org](https://archive.org/stream/genealogyofhibba00hibb/genealogyofhibba00hibb_djvu.txt)
- **Philip Hierlihy**'s parents -> likely wrong. The tree lists his father as "Cornelius Hierlihy Lt. Col" and mother as "Mary A. Monahan." A dedicated biographical site for his wife's family states Philip was the baptized son of **William (Guilielmus) Hierlihy and Mary (Marie) Wall** in Cork, and that the identity/relationship of a "Cornelius Hierlihy" (who is otherwise documented as having emigrated to Middletown, Connecticut ca. 1753) to Philip is unresolved — possibly an uncle or cousin, not necessarily his father. Neither "Cornelius" nor "Mary A. Monahan" as Philip's parents is corroborated by this source. — [Charlotte Taylor: Her Life and Times — "The Hierlihy Years"](https://charlottetaylor.ca/hierlihy-years/)
- **"Cornelius Hierlihy Lt. Col"** (I182625292213) -> appears to conflate two different real people. The genuine Loyalist Lieutenant Colonel in this family was **Timothy Hierlihy (1734–1797)**, commander of "Hierlihy's Corps" in the Prince of Wales American Regiment and founder of Antigonish, Nova Scotia — not a "Cornelius." Tellingly, the tree records this person's death place as "Town Point, Provence [sic], Nova Scotia, Canada," which matches Timothy Hierlihy's documented death location at Town Point, Antigonish, NS, almost exactly. A separate, non-military "Cornelius Hierlihy" is documented only as having emigrated to Middletown, CT around 1753 — he is not otherwise linked to a Lt. Col. rank or to Nova Scotia. Whoever assembled this tree entry appears to have merged Timothy's rank/death-place with Cornelius's name. — [Wikipedia: Timothy Hierlihy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Hierlihy); [Charlotte Taylor: Her Life and Times — "The Hierlihy Years"](https://charlottetaylor.ca/hierlihy-years/)
- **"General William Charles Howe Taylor"** (I182625292206) -> family legend, not a documented person. A biographical site dedicated to his supposed daughter Charlotte (Taylor) Hierlihy states directly that "there is no record of a General Howe Taylor in the recorded vital statistics of Great Britain or North America," and that the story likely arose from conflating family lore with the real, famous **Sir William Howe, 5th Viscount Howe** (a genuine British general of the Revolutionary War era) — a claim the same source calls unproven. The site's own best guess for Charlotte's actual parents is "Charles and Ann Taylor," ordinary Londoners, with Charlotte born 9 Nov 1755 in London. Recommend downgrading this whole line from a documented ancestor to labeled family legend. — [Charlotte Taylor: Her Life and Times](https://charlottetaylor.ca/)
- **Joan 17 Herrington**'s birthplace "Southold, Suffolk, , England" -> should read **Southwold**, Suffolk (the tree's own death event for the same person already spells it correctly as "Southwold"). Southold is the Long Island, NY town named after the English Southwold; there is no "Southold" in Suffolk, England. Minor data-entry/OCR fix, not a substantive genealogical error.

## New findings

- **Nicolaus Henckell (Jenkins) was a Hessian mercenary soldier**, not simply a German emigrant. He served as Regimental Drummer in the **Regiment von Knyphausen**, a Hessian unit hired out to the British Crown during the American Revolutionary War — he would have been about 20 years, 11 months old when the regiment departed its garrison at Ziegenhain in March 1776. After the war, rather than returning to Germany with most of the regiment (which sailed home in August 1783), he stayed in North America, married in the Presbyterian Church at Jamaica, Long Island, NY on 27 Jul 1783, and eventually settled permanently on Prince Edward Island, where "Henckell" became anglicized to "Jenkins." None of this military-service or immigration detail is present in the tree data. — [Doug's Genealogy blog, "Nicholas Henckel's Military Record"](http://jenkins-ballem.blogspot.com/2007/08/nicholas-henckels-military-record.html); [WikiTree: Henckell-1](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Henckell-1)
- **Philip Hierlihy's likely cause of death**: a boating accident, around 1802 — two years earlier than the tree's recorded death year of 1804. — [Charlotte Taylor: Her Life and Times — "The Hierlihy Years"](https://charlottetaylor.ca/hierlihy-years/)
- **Philip and Charlotte Hierlihy founded Tabusintac, New Brunswick** after receiving their 1798 Crown land grant, leading a small group of settlers roughly 30 miles north of their original Miramichi grant. This founding-of-a-town detail is not in the tree. — [Charlotte Taylor: Her Life and Times — "The Hierlihy Years"](https://charlottetaylor.ca/hierlihy-years/)

## Open questions

- **Anna Barbara Heil** (b. 1821, tree says "Bayern, Traunstein, Bayern, Germany") is recorded as the daughter of **Johann Georg Heil** (b. 1790, tree says "Fulda, Hesse, Germany"). Fulda (Hesse) and Traunstein (Bavaria) are roughly 350 km apart and in different historical German states — an unusual jump for a father/daughter birthplace pair in this era. Not resolved; could reflect a family relocation, a mis-transcribed place name, or a misattached parent-child link. No independent record located for either person.
- **Adam Henley / Anne Henley (Brinkworth, Wiltshire)**: a dedicated Henly/Henley family history site (henly.org.uk) covers exactly this place and surname and does list an "Alexander Henly (1629–1676)" married to a "Margaret Edwards (d. 1712)" — names matching the tree's listed parents of Adam Henley — but the site's own family tree shows an "Adam Henly (1637–1681)" as Alexander's *brother* (both sons of Jeffery Henly & Elizabeth), not as Alexander's son, and does not extend to a death in 1685 or a daughter married to a Whitlock. It's unclear whether the tree's "Adam Henley" (1651–1685) is a different, later Adam in the same family, a date-shifted duplicate of the 1637–1681 Adam, or a distinct branch. Worth a closer read of the full henly.org.uk site before treating this line as settled. — [Early Brinkworth Henlys](http://www.henly.org.uk/henly/henly16.html)
- **Maurice "Morris J." Hohman** and **Joseph Michael Hohman** (Fulda/Noble Co., Ohio Hohman cluster): the Fulda, Ohio genealogy database clearly documents a large, real Hohman family in this exact place and era (e.g., a Joseph J. Hohman b. 1848 d. 1921 and a John Michael Hohman b. 1845 d. 1909, children of John Sebastian Hohman and Matilda Archer), but I could not confirm this specific Maurice/Joseph Michael Hohman pair or their exact dates against that database in the time available. Likely the same extended family, not necessarily an error. — [Fulda, Ohio genealogy database](https://fuldaohio.org/genealogy/pafg298.htm)
- **Mariae Gugliomi**: the given name form ("Mariae," Latin genitive/dative) and her recorded husband's name ("Richardi\Richarde Thompson," also part-Latin) strongly suggest this entry derives from a Latin-language parish register (a common 17th-century English/European convention), with "Gugliomi" possibly a garbled transcription of an ordinary English or Latinized surname rather than a genuine Italian one. Not resolved; no independent record located.
