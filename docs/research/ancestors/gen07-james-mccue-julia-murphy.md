# James McCue (1775–?) and Julia Murphy (1784–?) — Generation 7

> **Flag being resolved:** `direct-line-issues.md` / `deep_dive_backlog.md` list a *child-after-death* inconsistency for this couple — daughter Margaret McCue was born 9 Mar 1835, six years after both parents are recorded as dying in 1829 — plus a related *parent-too-old* flag on Julia Murphy (age 51 at Margaret's birth, if the 1784 birth year is taken literally).

## Overview

James McCue (b. 1775, Ireland) and Julia Murphy (b. 1784, Ireland) are the tree's earliest-documented ancestors in this Irish immigrant line, known to us only through two daughters who emigrated to Norfolk County, Massachusetts: **Julia McCue** (b. 6 Jun 1826, Ireland; m. James Gilligan, 6 Apr 1850; d. 6 Jul 1892, Weymouth, MA) and **Margaret McCue** (b. 9 Mar 1835, Ireland; m. William Haley, 15 Oct 1854, Randolph, MA; d. 6 Aug 1893, Weymouth, MA). Both daughters' births are documented via Massachusetts marriage-record transcriptions that name their parents as James McCue and Julia Murphy. Neither parent has a single sourced event of their own — their birth years (1775/1784) and shared death year (1829) all carry empty source citations in the underlying data and are best read as unverified estimates from the original Ancestry Family Tree submission, not as facts drawn from any vital, parish, or probate record. The 1829 death year is almost certainly wrong (or at minimum unverifiable), since it predates Margaret's documented birth by six years; no independent record of this couple, under any death year, could be located on the open web.

## Verified facts

- **Two daughters, Julia (b. 1826) and Margaret (b. 1835), both born in Ireland to parents named James McCue and Julia Murphy**, per their respective Massachusetts marriage-record transcriptions. [documented — S446915296, S447115921]
- **Julia McCue** married James Gilligan 6 Apr 1850; the couple settled in Weymouth, Norfolk Co., MA, where Julia died 6 Jul 1892 and where six of their children were recorded (incl. Bridget Mary Gilligan, b. 1861). [documented]
- **Margaret McCue** married William Haley (b. abt. 1833, Ireland) 15 Oct 1854 in Randolph, MA; Margaret died 6 Aug 1893 in Weymouth, MA (death record itself not sourced in the tree, only inferred from family proximity). [documented for the marriage; inferred for the death]
- **The two daughters' documented birth years are 9 years apart (1826, 1835) and both are consistent with a single Irish-emigrant family** that later concentrated in the same small cluster of Norfolk County towns (Weymouth and neighboring Randolph) — the ordinary pattern for chain migration of siblings, not the pattern expected from two coincidentally same-named couples. [inferred, from geographic/temporal consistency]

## Corrections to the tree

- **The parents' shared "died 1829" is very likely a data error and should not be relied upon.** In the underlying record, *neither* James McCue's nor Julia Murphy's death event carries a source citation or a death place — unlike every other death in this family cluster that actually is documented (e.g., Julia McCue's death is pinned to "Weymouth, Norfolk, Massachusetts" with a real citation). A placeless, sourceless, identical death year for both spouses looks like a placeholder guess inherited from the original Ancestry Family Tree (S446914557), not a transcribed record. Because Margaret McCue's 1835 birth **is** documented (via her own marriage record), 1829 cannot be correct for either parent — both must have lived to at least c. 1834. **Correction applied:** both records now carry a `manual.events` entry recording death as "aft. 1834" (superseding, not deleting, the machine-parsed 1829 figure) plus `manual.notes` explaining the reasoning. No corrected year is asserted, because none could be documented.
- **Julia Murphy's "age 51 at Margaret's birth" flag is likely an artifact of the same problem**, not a real fertility outlier: her birth year (1784) is equally unsourced. If either the birth year or the assumption that this is her first/only span of childbearing is off, the "51" figure is not a hard biological fact — it is a byproduct of two unverified estimates interacting.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **No second James McCue/Julia Murphy couple was found.** A same-named-couple mix-up (i.e., Margaret belonging to a different James McCue/Julia Murphy pair) was considered and is not supported: no other Irish-immigrant James McCue/Julia Murphy couple with Massachusetts-area descendants surfaced in FamilySearch, WikiTree, Geni, or open genealogy indexes searched. All "James McCue" hits found externally (e.g., Colonel James Randolph McCue of Co. Antrim, 1791–1846; a Mayo-born James McCue 1820–1880) are clearly different men in different places.
- **Margaret McCue's marriage-record citation carries an odd page string** — "New England Historic Genealogical Society; Boston, Massachusetts; Massachusetts Vital Records, 1911–1915" — attached to both her 1835 birth and her 1854 marriage in the source data. That collection title (1911–1915) cannot itself be the origin of an 1835/1854 record; this looks like a mislabeled or merged citation from the original Ancestry export, not a fabrication of the events themselves. Flagged for awareness; does not change the verdict above, since the surrounding record (source S447115921, "Massachusetts, Marriage Records, 1840-1915") is the plausible correct collection.
- **The Weymouth, Massachusetts town vital-records volume (marriages/deaths to 1850, NEHGS, freely available on archive.org)** was checked directly for McCue, Gilligan, and Murphy entries around 1850; no entries for this family were found in it, meaning the 1850 Gilligan–McCue marriage (and any death of James McCue/Julia Murphy, if it happened in Weymouth before 1850) is not captured in that particular public-domain compilation. This is consistent with the couple dying/marrying outside Weymouth, in Ireland, or after the volume's 1850 cutoff — it does not itself prove or disprove anything, but rules out that easy public-domain check.

## Open questions

- **James McCue's and Julia Murphy's actual death dates and places remain unknown.** Given that both daughters were still being born in Ireland as late as 1835, the couple most plausibly died in Ireland sometime after c. 1835 (pre-Famine or Famine-era), or possibly emigrated later themselves and died in Massachusetts — neither is supported by any record found. Irish civil death registration did not begin until 1864, and pre-Famine Catholic parish burial registers are patchy and mostly not free/searchable online; this is very likely genuinely unrecoverable without knowing the couple's specific parish of origin.
- **Parish of origin in Ireland is unknown**, which is the main blocker to any further research (marriage, other children, death). Neither daughter's marriage or death record specifies a county or parish, only "Ireland."
- **Whether Julia McCue and Margaret McCue are truly full siblings (same mother and father) rather than half-siblings sharing only a father, or coincidentally-matching parent names from separate branches**, cannot be fully proven from the records at hand — the marriage-record parent citations are the only link, and Massachusetts marriage clerks sometimes recorded parents' names from the bride's own unverified statement. Treated here as full siblings on the balance of evidence (matching parent names + consistent geography/timing), but flagged as not ironclad.
- **Margaret McCue's death (6 Aug 1893, Weymouth) is only "inferred" in the tree, with no source** — a logical follow-up but outside the scope of the parents' child-after-death flag.
