# Laurence Larkin & Winifreda Mulken (Generation 6)

Tree IDs I182585270548 (Laurence) / I182585270547 (Winifreda). Irish-born immigrants who
settled in the town of Barton, Washington County, Wisconsin; direct ancestors (generation 6)
of the tree owner, and — per cross-referencing with sibling dossiers on this same family line —
likely the parents of the "Thomas Larkin" thread already researched at generation 5
(`gen05-thomas-larkin-catherine-burton.md`, `gen05-thomas-larkin.md`) and generation 4
(`gen04-thomas-larkin-mary-blackmore.md`), though that connection turns out to be tangled and
is flagged below rather than assumed.

## Overview

Laurence Larkin (b. abt. 1793, Ireland; d. 1868) and Winifreda Mulken (b. abt. 1808, Ireland;
d. 8 Jan 1874, Barton, Washington County, Wisconsin) appear as a household in the 1860 U.S.
Federal Census at Barton, Washington County, Wisconsin, with five children (Thomas, Edward,
James, Catherina, Jane). Barton in this period had a substantial, growing Irish Catholic
community alongside its majority-German settlement, which fits the couple's profile well, but
this dossier was unable to independently confirm any tree event through open web research: Find
a Grave blocks automated access outright (HTTP 403 on every URL pattern tried, including plain
cemetery pages), FamilySearch's person and record-search pages require a logged-in session to
render anything beyond a browser-compatibility notice, and no local Washington County history,
cemetery transcription site, or genealogy-trails page turned up any mention of a Larkin family
in Barton. The most substantive result of this pass is not a new primary-source citation but a
cross-reference: sibling dossiers researching this same Larkin line flag an "1860 Wisconsin
residence" and an "1875 naturalization" event that got attached to the *wrong* Thomas Larkin (a
Liverpool man who died in 1898) and that plausibly belong instead to *this* couple's own son
Thomas Larkin.

## Corrections to the tree

No correction is confirmed in this pass — but one real inconsistency surfaced through
cross-referencing sibling dossiers on this same Larkin family, and is flagged here rather than
silently ignored:

1. **Possible conflated/duplicated "Laurence Larkin & Winifreda Mulken" parentage claim
   elsewhere in the tree — needs reconciliation, not yet resolved.** The generation-5 dossiers
   for a different tree node (Thomas Larkin, I182789737425/I182584905624, m. Catherine Burton in
   Liverpool in 1851, d. 1898 Lancashire) record that *that* node also carries a "Laurence
   Larkin & Winifreda Mulken" parentage claim, tied to a 14 Jul 1839 Galway baptism and a 31 Mar
   1901 Muckanagh, Meelick, Co. Galway residence. Those dossiers independently showed the 1901
   Muckanagh household and the 1839 baptism almost certainly belong to a *different*,
   Ireland-resident Thomas Larkin (b. ~1841, still unmarried in 1901) rather than to the
   Liverpool emigrant — and that the Liverpool Thomas Larkin node separately, and equally
   wrongly, carries an unrelated "1860 Wisconsin residence" and "1 Nov 1875 naturalization"
   event that cannot belong to a man continuously resident in Liverpool through the 1861, 1871
   and 1881 England censuses. **The Wisconsin residence/naturalization pair is a strong
   candidate for actually belonging to our Thomas Larkin** — the son already listed in this
   couple's own children list (A51) — but this has not been proven, only flagged as plausible,
   because Wisconsin naturalization records for this era are not searchable in any free online
   index (Wisconsin Historical Society holds county originals at Area Research Centers only).
   Net effect: the tree may be describing a single "Laurence Larkin & Winifreda Mulken" couple
   whose identity has been scattered and partly conflated with an unrelated Galway/Liverpool
   Larkin family, or there may genuinely be two different couples who happen to share this name
   pair. Neither this dossier nor the two gen-5 dossiers could settle which, given the data
   supplied. See Open Questions.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Historical context for Barton, Washington County, Wisconsin, consistent with the tree's
  setting.** The town was founded in 1845 by surveyor Barton Salisbury (originally "Salisbury's
  Mills"), renamed for him in 1853. Settlers were predominantly German immigrant farmers, but a
  Catholic congregation in the area numbered about 70 Irish families by 1860; Catholics had been
  visited by a priest as early as 1848 and built a log church in 1852, with the first formal
  Roman Catholic service held on Christmas Day 1857. Fr. Caspar Rehrl, an Austrian missionary,
  founded the Sisters of St. Agnes convent and school in Barton on 12 August 1858. This fits a
  Larkin family's Irish Catholic profile arriving in the area by 1860, though no record
  specifically naming this family in these local histories was found.
- **Barton Township cemetery list** (per Links to the Past's Washington County index): Barton,
  Kopp, Newark Centre, St. Mary's Catholic, St. Mary's Immaculate Conception, and Union
  cemeteries — St. Mary's Catholic is the most plausible burial location for an Irish Catholic
  family of this era and place, but this is inference only; no burial record was located for
  either spouse.
- **Cross-reference finding (the most substantive result of this pass)**: see Correction #1
  above — an "1860 Wisconsin residence" and "1 Nov 1875 naturalization" event misattached in the
  tree to an unrelated Liverpool Thomas Larkin very plausibly belong to this couple's own son
  Thomas Larkin instead, which would mean he naturalized as a U.S. citizen in (or in connection
  with) Washington County, Wisconsin around 1875 — a lead worth pursuing directly against county
  naturalization records.
- No town history, genealogy-trails transcription, interment.net page, or full-text-searched
  1912 county history ("Washington County, Wisconsin: Past and Present," S.J. Clarke Publishing
  Co., vol. 1) or 1881 county history ("History of Washington and Ozaukee Counties, Wisconsin,"
  Western Historical Co.) contained the surname Larkin in a context matching this family (the
  1912-history full-text search for "Larkin" found only an unrelated "Millie Larkin, daughter of
  William Larkin... born in Pennsylvania" reference, clearly a different family).

## Open questions

1. **Is the "Laurence Larkin & Winifreda Mulken" parentage claim attached to the Liverpool
   Thomas Larkin node (I182584905624) the same couple as this one, or a coincidentally
   same-named different couple?** This is the single most important open question for this
   family line and cannot be resolved from the data supplied to this dossier alone; it needs
   the tree owner to compare person-ID linkages directly.
2. **Did son Thomas Larkin naturalize in Washington County, Wisconsin on 1 Nov 1875?** Would
   require an in-person or commissioned search of Washington County naturalization records
   (Wisconsin Historical Society Area Research Center) or a paid Ancestry/FamilySearch
   naturalization-index search — not available via free web search.
3. **What Irish county/parish did Laurence and Winifreda emigrate from?** The tree cites
   "Ireland, Catholic Parish Registers, 1655–1915" for both birth events but the tree data
   supplied gives no parish or county name. No baptism record was located via open web search.
4. **What became of children Edward, James, Catherina, and Jane Larkin?** No marriage, census,
   or death record was found for any of them under the Larkin surname in Barton/Washington
   County sources searched.
5. **Winifreda's parents, "James 'Gems' Carey (Mulkarey)" and "Nancy Davis"** — no independent
   trace found. The bracketed "(Mulkarey)" alongside "Carey" and the nickname "Gems" (possibly a
   phonetic rendering of the Irish forename Séamus, i.e., "James") both suggest the underlying
   source is an Irish-language or dialectal record transcribed inconsistently; worth revisiting
   directly against Irish Catholic parish registers if a county/parish can be established (see
   Q3).
6. **Where are Laurence and Winifreda buried?** St. Mary's Catholic Cemetery (Barton/West Bend)
   is a plausible candidate given the family's apparent Irish Catholic affiliation, but Find a
   Grave — the obvious resource to check — blocks all automated access (HTTP 403 on every URL
   pattern attempted, including plain cemetery-listing pages, not just search queries). This
   needs a manual, human-browser lookup.
