# Fragments Batch 09 — Triage Dossier (Surnames Pyne–Siegel, Generations 7–12)

## Overview

This batch covers 40 direct-ancestor fragments spanning surnames Pyne through Siegel, generations 7–12 (roughly 1590s–1815 births), scattered across colonial New England/New York, colonial New Jersey, Scotland, Sweden, Ireland, and southwest Germany (Württemberg/Baden Palatine lands, with an emigrant branch to Broad Bay/Waldoboro, Maine). Per instructions this is a triage pass, not a deep dive: every person got a quick plausibility check against the tree's own dates/places, and the ~9 most findable/notable were run through 1–3 targeted web searches. Several of these turned out to be well-documented colonial-immigrant lines (Severance, Pine/Pyne, Sanford, the Waldoboro Reid/Ried Palatines) with real primary/secondary sources online; a few appear to be data-entry problems in the tree itself (a likely duplicate person-record, a person whose tree record conflates father and son, and one geographically implausible birthplace).

## Researched Highlights

### John Edmund Severans (Severance) Sr, 1609–1682, and daughter Mary Severence (Coffin), 1645–1720
Independently and extensively documented. Born 4 March 1609 (not the tree's undated form) in Rattlesden, Suffolk, England; emigrated to New England, made freeman at Boston 17 May 1637, a founding settler of Salisbury, Massachusetts in 1639. He married first Abigail Kimball (daughter of Richard Kimball and Ursula Scott) in 1635, and, after her 1658 death, later married Susanna Ambrose. He died 9 April 1682 in Salisbury; his will, dated 7 April 1682, names his wife Susanna, son John, son Ephraim (executor), grandson Jonathan Church, and "son in law James Coffyn" — directly corroborating the tree's link from his daughter Mary Severance to James Coffin, ancestor of the well-known Nantucket Coffin family. Mary was born 5 Aug 1645 in Salisbury and died 28 July 1720, matching the tree.

### James Pyne (Pine), 1608–1685, immigrant to Hempstead, Long Island
Confirmed as a real, well-documented colonial immigrant via the New York Genealogical & Biographical Society and multiple compiled trees (WikiTree, Geni). He was christened 4 June 1608, not at Exeter as the tree states, but at **Woodbury, Devonshire** — the tree's birthplace looks like an approximation/error for the actual parish. He settled in Hempstead, Town of Queens (now Nassau Co.), NY by 1656, became the town's largest landholder (~500 acres) by 1685, and was named a Patentee in Governor Dongan's 1685 Hempstead charter. He died 13 June 1685 in Hempstead, matching the tree exactly. His wife, per independent sources, was **Susan Armitage** (b. 1632 Yorkshire, d. 1685/86 Hempstead) — not "Edith N Williams" as the tree records. This is a clear correction candidate (see below), though it's possible "Edith N Williams" belongs to a different, conflated individual.

### Robert Zachariah Sanford, 1644–1713 (likely = Zachariah Sanford, son of immigrant Robert Sanford)
The tree's single record "Robert Zachariah Sanford" (b. 1644 Hartford, d. 18 Jan 1713 Hartford) matches, almost date-for-date, an independently documented person: **Zachariah Sanford** (1644–1713/14), son of the actual immigrant **Robert Sanford** (c.1615/16–1676), who came from Much Hadham, Hertfordshire, to Hartford, Connecticut in the 1630s and fought in the Pequot War. Robert Sr. died in 1676, not 1713 — so the tree's single "Robert Zachariah" record appears to conflate two generations (father Robert + son Zachariah) into one person. Zachariah's daughter Sarah (b. 1661) is documented, matching the tree's next-generation entry Sarah Sanford (Paine), consistent with Zachariah (not Robert Sr.) being her father.

### Waldoboro (Broad Bay), Maine German settlers — Reid/Ried line
The Reid/Ried family (Hannß Jerg Ried → Hans Jerg Reid → Johann Georg Ried "J10g" → Margaret Catherine Reid) fits an independently documented and well-known migration: Samuel Waldo recruited roughly 1,500 German emigrants to the "Broad Bay" plantation (later Waldoboro), Lincoln Co., Maine, chiefly 1741–1753. Johann Georg Ried (c.1708–1776), son of Hannß Jerg Ried of Langensteinbach, Baden, married Mary Magdalena Eichorn in Germany and emigrated to Broad Bay, matching the tree's Reid/Eichorn/Acorn cluster (the tree's "Acorn" surname is the Anglicized "Eichorn" — a documented Waldoboro naming pattern; a Boston lawyer descendant, Edgar O. Achorn, is noted in local histories as descending from the Waldoboro "Eichorns"). A Find A Grave memorial exists for a related Jacob Ried Sr. (1735–1819) of the same Waldoboro Reid family, and Jasper Jacob Stahl's "History of the Town of Waldoboro, Maine" (on Archive.org) covers this settlement in depth — a good primary-adjacent source for a future deep dive on this whole cluster.

### Jacob Shone* (1760–1839) and Mary Skinner (b. 1770), Gloucester Co., NJ
The tree itself already cites three period-appropriate sources for this couple: a New Jersey compiled census/census-substitute index, Ancestry compiled trees, and a Pennsylvania compiled marriage-records index giving their 6 April 1787 Philadelphia marriage. This is internally consistent (Jacob's cited 1780 residence in Gloucester Township lines up with a c.1760 birth), but no independent Find A Grave or primary parish/church record was located in this pass to corroborate further; treat the marriage date as reasonably solid (sourced index) and the birth/death years as plausible but not independently confirmed.

### Ann Rason / Anne Rasin (Rason\Roison) — likely duplicate tree entries
I182198586968 (Ann Rason) and I182625986361 (Anne Rasin) share identical birth date (6 Oct 1611, Kingston-upon-Thames), identical death year (1691), identical spouse (Eframe/Efraime Cheesman), and identical parents (Thomas Rason/Rason and Joan Warman). The Ann Rason record additionally carries a sourced baptism citation ("England, Select Births and Christenings, 1538-1975"). These are almost certainly the same woman entered twice under spelling variants — a tree-hygiene issue worth flagging for merge, independent of whether the underlying baptism record itself checks out (not independently re-verified against the original parish register in this pass).

### Tamzen (Tamzon) Standford, 1692–1740, and the missing father's name
An independent compiled record (Ancestry) corroborates the tree's 1692–1740 dates, Pilesgrove, Salem Co., NJ location, and marriage to Samuel Elwell, and lists her father as **Thomas Stanford** — a name entirely absent from the tree's own parent list (which shows only mother "Miriam Johnson or Cohawkin"). This is a candidate new finding (see below), though it comes from a compiled secondary source, not a primary record, so it should be flagged as inferred rather than documented.

### Barbara Shaftoe, 1590–1630 — geographically inconsistent record
Shaftoe is a genuine, well-attested Northumberland gentry surname (parish records for Shaftoe/Cuthbert names do appear in Northumberland registers such as St Cuthbert Elsdon and Allendale), and her death place (Allendale, Northumberland) fits that pattern. But her birthplace is given in the tree simply as "Germany," which does not fit the surname or the family's apparent English gentry context at all, and no record for her or her stated husband Cuthbert Growe/Crawe was found. This looks like either a data-entry error (wrong birthplace field) or an unsupported placeholder; treat with caution.

## Verified facts

- James Pyne (I182625984931): christened 4 June 1608 (parish likely Woodbury, Devon, not Exeter as tree states); settled Hempstead, Long Island by 1656; named a Patentee in the 1685 Hempstead charter; died 13 June 1685, Hempstead — per New York Genealogical & Biographical Society and WikiTree/Geni compiled trees.
- John Edmund Severans (Severance) Sr (I182541956067): b. Rattlesden, Suffolk, England, 1609; freeman of Boston 1637; founding settler of Salisbury, MA, 1639; d. 9 April 1682, Salisbury — per FamilySearch, WikiTree, Find A Grave, and his own 1682 will.
- Mary Severence (Coffin) (I182541955827): identified as daughter of John Severance in his 1682 will ("son in law James Coffyn"), corroborating her marriage to James Coffin — per the will as summarized in compiled genealogies.
- Johann Georg Ried "J10g" (I182534044241): emigrated from Langensteinbach, Baden, to Broad Bay (Waldoboro), Lincoln Co., Maine, having married Mary Magdalena Eichorn in Germany — per Waldoboro town-history sources (Stahl's "History of the Town of Waldoboro, Maine," Archive.org) and related genealogies.
- Mary Skinner (I182195862796) married Jacob Shone 6 April 1787 in Philadelphia — per "Pennsylvania, Compiled Marriage Records, 1700-1821," cited in the tree itself.
- Ann Rason (I182198586968): baptized 6 Oct 1611, Kingston-upon-Thames, Surrey — per "England, Select Births and Christenings, 1538-1975," cited in the tree itself.

## Corrections to the tree

- James Pyne's wife: tree says **"Edith N Williams"** → independent sources (NYGBS, WikiTree/Geni compiled trees) give **Susan Armitage** (b. 1632 Yorkshire, d. 1685/86 Hempstead) as his wife. Evidence: NYGBS "James Pyne (Pine) of Hempstead, Long Island" article and multiple compiled genealogies. Recommend re-checking whether "Edith N Williams" belongs to a different Pine ancestor or is simply an error.
- James Pyne's birthplace: tree says **"Exeter, Devon"** → sources point to **Woodbury, Devonshire** as the christening parish. Minor, but worth correcting if a more precise parish record is wanted.
- Robert Zachariah Sanford (I182541964402): the tree's single record, with birth 1644 and death 18 Jan 1713 (Hartford), matches the documented **Zachariah Sanford**, son of the actual Hartford immigrant **Robert Sanford** (c.1615/16–1676). Robert Sr. himself died in 1676, not 1713. Recommend splitting this into two generations (Robert Sanford Sr., immigrant, d. 1676; his son Zachariah Sanford, 1644–1713) rather than one merged "Robert Zachariah" person, and re-attaching Sarah Sanford (Paine) as Zachariah's daughter rather than Robert Sr.'s.
- Likely duplicate person: Ann Rason (I182198586968) and Anne Rasin "Rason\Roison" (I182625986361) share identical birth/death dates, spouse, and parents and are almost certainly the same individual recorded twice under spelling variants. Recommend merging.

## New findings

- Tamzen (Tamzon) Standford's father is named in an independent compiled record as **Thomas Stanford**, a name not present anywhere in the tree's own parent list for her (tree shows only her mother, "Miriam Johnson or Cohawkin"). Source is secondary/compiled (Ancestry), so treat as inferred, not documented.
- The Waldoboro "Acorn" surname branch (children of Margaret Catherine Reid and Matthias Eichorn "Acorn") reflects a documented local Anglicization pattern of German "Eichorn" to "Acorn"/"Achorn" among Waldoboro's German-immigrant descendants — including a noted 19th-century Boston lawyer, Edgar O. Achorn, described in local histories as descending from the Waldoboro Eichorns. This is useful context for the family's later Maine history not currently reflected in the tree's notes.
- John Edmund Severans Sr.'s 1682 will (as summarized in compiled sources) names his wife Susanna, sons John and Ephraim (executor), grandson Jonathan Church, and son-in-law James Coffin — richer detail than the tree currently carries, and independent corroboration of the Severance–Coffin marriage link.

## Open questions

- Is "Edith N Williams" (tree's wife for James Pyne) a genuine alternate/first wife, a confusion with another ancestor, or simply an error for Susan Armitage? Worth a dedicated look before trusting either name.
- Should the tree split "Robert Zachariah Sanford" into two separate people (Robert Sanford Sr., d. 1676, and his son Zachariah Sanford, 1644–1713)? If so, does Sarah Sanford (Paine) belong under Zachariah rather than Robert Sr.?
- Are Ann Rason and Anne Rasin (Rason\Roison) truly duplicate entries for the same woman, or is there a genuine second person with the same near-identical details? Recommend a merge review.
- No record was found at all for Rose Riley (Balrothery, Dublin, 1760–1841), Barbara Shaftoe (birthplace conflict), Thomas Reily (Co. Monaghan), or Jane Robbins (no dates) — worth flagging these branches as needing primary-source work (Irish parish/civil records, Northumberland parish records) rather than treating them as settled.
- Several German-Württemberg entries (Anna Maria Saemaennin, Anna Maria Schobers, Bartholomäus Seemann, the Sachs/Siegel cluster) are internally coherent generationally but were not independently matched to a specific archival record in this pass — a targeted look at Kirchenbuch (parish register) transcriptions for Eberdingen/Metterzimmern would be the next step if this branch becomes a priority.
- The tree's own record for Hannß Jerg Ried contains multiple mutually inconsistent birth entries (different years and different towns, some marked "val=None") — this looks like leftover merge artifacts from multiple source imports and should be cleaned up regardless of which value is ultimately correct.
