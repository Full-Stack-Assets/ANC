# Fragments 06 — Batch Triage (40 people, generations 7–12)

## Overview

This is a triage pass over 40 surname-fragment ancestors (initials L–M) spanning
17th-century Scotland, colonial New England, Cape Breton Acadia/Nova Scotia,
Palatine-German New York, and 18th–19th-century Prince Edward Island. Most
entries are thin (single unsourced birth/death lines pulled from a compiled
tree) and were assessed only for internal plausibility. Ten were selected as
findable/notable and checked against secondary genealogical sources
(WikiTree, Geni, FamilySearch, local-history blogs). One entry — the
"9th Earl of Crawford" claim — is a well-known genealogical fantasy that
should be corrected or flagged; a second (Johannes Magnusson) has an internal
date contradiction despite being the best-sourced person in the batch.

## Researched in depth

### David Lindsay, "9th Earl of Crawford" (I182381537279) and Helene Lindsay (I182381537266)

The tree gives David Lindsay as the "9th Earl of Crawford," born 16 Aug 1636
at Edzell Castle and died 1698 at Dunfermline. **This title attribution is
wrong.** The actual, documented 9th Earl of Crawford was David Lindsay of
Edzell (nicknamed "the Interpolated Earl" by historians), who inherited the
earldom in 1542 and died in **1558** — nearly 80 years before the 1636 birth
date in our tree (Wikipedia; FamilySearch; WikiTree Lindsay-484; thepeerage.com).
The 1636–1698 "David Lindsay of Edzell" appears to be a real, later member of
the Lindsay-of-Edzell family, but the "9th Earl" title attached to him in
Ancestry-style compiled trees is a documented mix-up between two different
people who share a name and a place (Edzell). I could not independently
confirm the 1636–1698 individual's actual identity, parents, or his claimed
daughter Helene Lindsay (b. 1667, Edinburgh) marrying a Joseph Banks — no
period source for either surfaced. Both entries should be treated as
**legend-grade** (tree lore riding on a title that does not belong to this
person) rather than documented nobility.

### Edward Larkin (I182381536645) and Hannah (Penticost) Larkin (I182381536270)

Edward Larkin is a real, documented early settler of Charlestown, Massachusetts
— a wheelwright admitted an inhabitant 30 May 1638, whose will (dated 15 Jul
1651, proved 6 Feb 1652) is on record (FamilySearch, WikiTree Larkin-46,
Find a Grave #32696054, Nutfield Genealogy "Larkin of Charlestown"). Two
corrections to the tree:

- **Birthplace**: the tree says "Berkhampsted, Suffolk, England (Kent, England)."
  Berkhamsted is in Hertfordshire, not Suffolk or Kent — the place field
  conflates at least two different counties. No period source found actually
  documents his English birthplace; most compiled genealogies simply guess.
- **Wife's maiden name**: the tree names her "Joanna Hale." Genealogists who
  have examined the record (per a Rootweb/Geni discussion thread) note this is
  **disputed** — the only firm fact is that Edward, Robert, and John Larkin
  each married a woman named Joan/Joanna, and the Larkin, Hale, and Penticost
  families were closely associated in Charlestown by 1651. "Hale" is a
  plausible but not conclusively documented identification; "Butler" has also
  been circulated with even less support. What is well documented: after
  Edward's death, his widow Joanna remarried ropemaker **John Penticost**
  (after Mar 1657/8) — which explains why a daughter in some trees carries
  the name "(Penticost) Larkin" (a stepfather's surname, not a maiden or
  married name of her own). This is worth noting as a caution rather than a
  hard correction: "Hale" should be labeled *inferred/disputed*, not sourced.

### John LeVatte (I182197770092) and Catherine Martell (I182197770113)

Both are confirmed via secondary compiled trees (Geni "Catherine Martell";
a hosted GEDCOM at vbtree.heroictenor.com) as a real Cape Breton, Nova Scotia
couple: Catherine Martell, b. 1803 Main-à-Dieu, Cape Breton, d. 6 Nov 1883
Danvers, Essex, MA — matching the tree exactly. **New finding**: secondary
sources give her parents as **Charles Martell** (b. 12 Feb 1733 Dublin,
Ireland – d. 5 Nov 1819 Main-à-Dieu) and Susannah Mehitable Wilcox — not
present in our tree at all. Her children per the same sources include Martin
LeVatte, Elizabeth (LeVatte) Verner, Catherine (LeVatte) Leonard, and Mary Ann
(LeVatte) MacDonald — consistent with our tree's "Martin LeVatte" and
"Susannah Joanna LeVatte" (the repo's own gen06 file
`gen06-laughlan-macdonald-susannah-joanna-levat.md` corroborates the MacDonald
marriage). A later LeVatte descendant, Barbara Elizabeth LeVatte (b. 1876,
Louisbourg), recorded as a daughter of Martin LeVatte, further corroborates
the family's continuity in the Louisbourg/Main-à-Dieu area. All sourcing here
is secondary/compiled (Geni, hosted GEDCOMs) — no primary parish or civil
record was located in this pass.

### Judith "Madiver" (Maddiver) (I182620337720)

The tree's name field is corrupted with embedded research notes
("...\My 6th great-grandmother \RICHARD INKERSALL\ INGERSOLL LINE\Line that
belonges to my children Buffey, Spring, April, and Bill") — this should be
cleaned to simply **Judith Maddiver**. She is independently attested on
WikiTree (page "Madiver-1") and multiple hosted family trees as the wife of
**Samuel Ingersoll** of Gloucester, Essex Co., Massachusetts, matching the
tree's birth (1660, Purpooduck, Cumberland/Maine) and death (11 May 1720,
Gloucester, MA) exactly. **New finding**: Samuel Ingersoll is identified in
secondary sources as the son of **George (Lt.) Ingersoll and Elizabeth Lunt**
— which explains the odd compound name "SAMUEL LUNT INGERSOLL" used for her
spouse in our tree (Lunt is his mother's maiden name, folded into his name by
a compiler). Children found in secondary sources (Samuel Jr., Solomon b.1699,
Nehemiah b.1705) don't include the tree's listed child "Josiah
Ingersoll\Ingersal," which may be an additional, differently-sourced child or
a naming variant not covered by the sources checked here.

### John McArthur of Milton, Dunoon (I182381537173) and Margaret McArthur (I182381537159)

"MacArthur of Milton" (the mill-town of Dunoon, Argyll) is a real, documented
cadet branch of Clan MacArthur: descendants of a MacArthur who moved from
Drishaig to Dunoon around 1590, whose family bought land from the 8th Earl of
Argyll in 1653 and founded the House of MacArthur of Milton and Ascog
(clanarthur.org; Plaidwerx Clan MacArthur genealogy). The clan history's
"John MacArthur of Milton" died about 1674 — one generation earlier than our
tree's John McArthur (b.1650, d.1697), so our John is plausibly a son or
grandson of the family founder rather than the founder himself; I could not
pin down an exact match to a specific generation in the clan pedigree in this
pass. **Flag**: his daughter Margaret McArthur's recorded dates (b.1664,
d.1775) imply an 111-year lifespan, which is not plausible for the period —
almost certainly a death-date transcription error (compare her husband
Alexander Moir's death in 1723 and her own son James Moir's death in 1784;
a death year in the 1720s–1740s would fit the family timeline far better than
1775).

### Anna Veronica Menges-Crist (I182534044195)

West Camp, in the Town of Saugerties, Ulster County, NY, is a real, well
documented historical site: one of two camps set up along the Hudson by the
English colonial government to house German Palatine refugees displaced by
Louis XIV's invasions, before their relocation inland from about 1710
(WikiTree "Space: West Camp, New York"; Rootsweb "Palatines East and West
Camp"). This confirms the tree's stated birthplace ("Upper German Colony,
West Camp, Ulster, New York," 1715) is historically coherent for a
Palatine-immigrant-descended family, and St. Paul's Lutheran Church at West
Camp has surviving records (1709–1899) that could resolve this person with
further primary-source digging. No record naming Anna Veronica Menges (or
Menges-Crist) specifically was located in this pass — this remains
plausible/context-consistent rather than individually documented.

### Thomas Merrick (I182541960298)

The tree's birthplace field, "Wales, Massachusetts," is almost certainly a
conflation error: there is a well-documented immigrant **Thomas Merrick
(c.1620–1704)** who was born in Wales (St. David's Parish, Pembrokeshire) and
settled in Springfield, Massachusetts — i.e., "Wales" (country of origin) and
"Massachusetts" (place of settlement) appear to have been merged into one
place-name field by a compiler (WikiTree Merrick-10; Familypedia; the
Brownlow family tree). However, that well-documented Thomas Merrick married
**Elizabeth Tilley**, not Mary Griggs, and died in 1704 in Springfield, not
1684 in Middletown, CT — so he does not cleanly match our tree's person
(b.1600, d.1684 Middletown, spouse Mary Griggs, child "Margaret"). A search
for "Mary Griggs" with a Middletown, CT context instead surfaced an unrelated
couple, **Thomas Stow (1615–1684) m. Mary Griggs**, of Roxbury/Concord/
Middletown — suggesting our tree's Thomas Merrick entry may itself be a
mixture of two or more distinct colonial families with overlapping given
names and Connecticut River Valley geography. Net assessment: **suspect** —
the identity as recorded doesn't correspond cleanly to any single documented
person found in this pass.

### Johannes Magnusson (I182197443344)

This is the best-sourced person in the whole batch — the tree cites three
record series (Sweden Indexed Death Records 1840–1947; Jönköping Church
Records 1633–1860; Sweden Church Records 1451–1943) for birth (25 Sep 1791,
Backgården Hadäng farm, Velinge, Skaraborg), death (27 Jan 1873, Nya Varv,
Skaraborg), and a burial record dated **26 Sep 1824** at Alseda församling,
Jönköping. **This is an internal contradiction**: a burial in 1824 cannot
belong to a man who died in 1873 — either the burial record is misattached
(possibly belongs to a different Johannes Magnusson, or the death year on
that specific record was misread), or one of the two dates was
mistranscribed. This wasn't resolved with the sources available in this pass
and should be checked directly against the cited Jönköping church-record
scan. Parentage (Magnus Månsson Ånge & Britta Gabrielsdotter) was not
independently re-verified here but is consistent with the surrounding
"Månsson Ånge" cluster already in this fragment set (Marit Margareta
Larsdotter's husband is "Mäster Måns Andersson Ånge," a plausible relative).

## Corrections to the tree

- David Lindsay (I182381537279): "9th Earl of Crawford" → **not the 9th Earl**
  (the real 9th Earl died 1558, ~78 years before this person's 1636 birth
  date). Evidence: Wikipedia "David Lindsay, 9th Earl of Crawford";
  FamilySearch L5ZW-QTL; WikiTree Lindsay-484; thepeerage.com.
- Edward Larkin (I182381536645) birthplace: "Berkhampsted, Suffolk, England
  (Kent, England)" → geographically incoherent (Berkhamsted is in
  Hertfordshire); no period source documents an English birthplace at all.
  Treat as unsourced speculation, not fact.
- Edward Larkin's wife "Joanna Hale" → should be labeled **disputed/inferred**,
  not documented; genealogists note the Hale attribution lacks firm evidence
  (per Rootsweb/Geni discussion referenced above).
- Margaret McArthur (I182381537159) death year 1664 birth/1775 death → implies
  an implausible 111-year lifespan; likely a transcription error, plausibly
  for a year in the 1720s–1740s (evidence: internal family timeline —
  husband Alexander Moir d.1723, son James Moir d.1784).
- Johannes Magnusson (I182197443344): burial event dated 1824 predates his
  own recorded death (1873) by 49 years — internally contradictory as
  currently entered; needs to be checked against the cited Jönköping church
  record.
- Thomas Merrick (I182541960298) birthplace "Wales, Massachusetts" → likely a
  conflation of two separate facts (Welsh origin + Massachusetts settlement)
  belonging to a similarly-named immigrant; the person as recorded (spouse
  Mary Griggs, Middletown CT, d.1684) does not match the documented
  Springfield, MA Merrick line (spouse Elizabeth Tilley, d.1704).

## New findings (not in the tree)

- Catherine Martell's parents: **Charles Martell** (b. 12 Feb 1733 Dublin,
  Ireland – d. 5 Nov 1819 Main-à-Dieu, Cape Breton) and **Susannah Mehitable
  Wilcox** (secondary sources: Geni; hosted GEDCOM at vbtree.heroictenor.com).
- Samuel Ingersoll (husband of Judith Maddiver) was the son of **George (Lt.)
  Ingersoll and Elizabeth Lunt** — explaining the "Lunt" folded into his name
  in our tree ("SAMUEL LUNT INGERSOLL").
- Edward Larkin's widow Joanna remarried **John Penticost** (ropemaker, d. 19
  Oct 1687) after March 1657/8 — the likely source of the "(Penticost)"
  parenthetical attached to their daughter Hannah's name in the tree.
- The MacArthur-of-Milton (Dunoon) family is independently documented back to
  a founder who moved from Drishaig to Dunoon around 1590 and purchased land
  from the 8th Earl of Argyll in 1653; the family produced a Kintyre bailie
  and a chamberlain to the Marquess of Montrose by the mid-1680s.

## Open questions

- Who, if anyone, was the real father of the 1636–1698 "David Lindsay of
  Edzell," and does he actually connect to any Earl of Crawford at all, or is
  the whole line a fabrication riding on a shared place-name (Edzell)?
- What is Edward Larkin's wife's actual maiden name — is it resolvable from
  Charlestown town/church records rather than compiled trees?
- Which of the two 1824/1873 dates on Johannes Magnusson's record is the
  error, and is the burial record actually for a different Johannes
  Magnusson entirely?
- Is our "Thomas Merrick" a genuine distinct individual, or a garbled
  composite of the Springfield Merrick line and the Roxbury/Middletown Stow
  line?
- Does our John McArthur (b.1650 d.1697) correspond to a specific,
  identifiable generation in the published MacArthur-of-Milton pedigree
  (Hugh Peskett's research, referenced by Clan Arthur)?
- No primary-source (parish register, civil record, Find a Grave) confirmation
  was attempted for: the Larsdotter/Larsson Swedish cluster, the McKie/Moir/
  Banks Prince Edward Island cluster, Elizabeth Lotz, Susanna Sarah Maus, or
  James McCue — these remain plausible-but-unverified.
