# The Elwell Line — Lineage Survey (Generations 7–10 in tree data; Gloucester, MA → Salem/Pilesgrove/Pittsgrove, NJ)

## Overview

This is a surname line of direct ancestors carrying the Elwell name from colonial Essex County, Massachusetts into West/South Jersey (Salem County) in the 1680s. The tree data supplied covers only generations 7–10 (Elizabeth Elwell, b.1781, back to Samuel Elwell/Thomas Elwell Jr, b. ~1678–1685), not generations 11–12 as the assignment description implied — the two generations above that (the actual Massachusetts-born mover and the transatlantic immigrant) are named only inside "parents" fields, not as full person records. The best documentary anchor for this line is a dedicated published genealogy, Elmer Garfield Van Name's *The Elwell Family: Seventeenth and Eighteenth Centuries, Southern New Jersey*, digitized on Archive.org, cross-checked against the older Jacob Thomas Elwell/Charles Henry Pope *Elwell Family in America* (1899, NEHGR) and WikiTree. The reliably documented chain runs cleanly from the Gloucester-born Thomas Elwell (1654–1706) down through his son Samuel Elwell (d. 1740) and grandchildren, but breaks at the Isaac Elwell (b. 1751) generation — his parentage and his marriage to "Hannah Paulin" could not be corroborated in any source found, and the generation-9 "Samuel Jacob Elwell" node appears to be a duplicate-record conflation of two distinct brothers.

## Verified facts

- **Thomas Elwell Jr / "Thomas (3)"** (tree gen 10, b. 26 Apr 1678 Gloucester, Essex Co., MA; d. 14 Aug 1722 Pilesgrove, Salem Co., NJ) — birth date and place match exactly. Son of Thomas Elwell (1654–1706) and Sarah Bassett. *Source: Van Name, "The Elwell Family," https://archive.org/stream/elwellfamilyseve00vann/elwellfamilyseve00vann_djvu.txt (generation-3 entries).*
- **Samuel Elwell** (tree gen 10, b. c.1685–1688 NJ, d. "early in 1739/40," will dated 5 Jan 1739/40, Pilesgrove) — son of Thomas Elwell and Sarah Bassett; his will disposed of a 60-acre grist-mill property, a plantation over 100 acres, a 50-acre tract bought from William Paulin, and a cedar swamp; wife and son Jacob were named to sell the Paulin tract. *Source: Van Name (as above), p. 17–18; corroborated by WikiTree Elwell-175, https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Elwell-175.*
- **Samuel Elwell's wife was named Tamzen, connected to the Standford family** — Van Name cites Miriam Standford's will naming "Samuel(3) Elwell, husband of Tamzen," and notes Samuel Elwell assisted Miriam Standford as executrix under Thomas Standford's will dated 26 Nov 1722. This *confirms* the tree's "Tamzen OR Tamzon STANDFORD" as correct, and contradicts a low-quality secondary web snippet (an AI-summarized Ancestry/WikiTree aggregate) that named her "Tamzen Paulin" — that appears to be a conflation with the unrelated Paulin family who later married into this line (see below). *Source: Van Name, as above.*
- **Susanna Elwell (daughter of Thomas Elwell Jr) married her first cousin, a son of Samuel Elwell** — Van Name states plainly: "[Samuel, son of Samuel (3)] married his first cousin, Susanna Elwell (daughter of Thomas 3)." WikiTree's profile for this Samuel Elwell (Elwell-402) gives the marriage as 25 Nov 1745 in Pittsgrove, NJ, and independently flags the couple as first cousins. *Sources: Van Name; WikiTree Elwell-402, https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Elwell-402.*
- **A Jacob Elwell, son of Samuel Elwell, b. 1714, d. 26 Sep 1773** is separately documented in Van Name as married to **Catherine Du Bois** of Ulster County, NY — not to Susanna Elwell. His children as listed by Van Name were David, Samuel (b. 10 Sep 1747, d. 10 Jul 1822), Jonathan (d. 1826, m. Peggy Summerill), Rhoda (m. Henry Richman), and Rachel (m. Jacob Hutchinson, later Jonathan Sneighin) — no son named Isaac. *Source: Van Name, as above.*
- **Pennsville Township was called "Lower Penns Neck Township" until a 1965 referendum renamed it** — so the birthplace given for Elizabeth Elwell (1781) and her death place (1862) as "Pennsville" is a modernized/anachronistic place label; the period-correct name was Lower Penns Neck Township, Salem Co., NJ. *Source: Wikipedia, "Pennsville Township, New Jersey," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsville_Township,_New_Jersey.*
- **A John Pedrick and Elizabeth Pedrick of Pennsville/Lower Penns Neck appear as parents of a Margaret Pedrick, b. 1804** in an Ancestry family-history index snippet — consistent with (though not conclusive proof of) the tree's claim that Elizabeth Elwell married John Pedrick, since it places a couple of exactly this name in the same place in the right era, alongside the tree's known child Francis Augustus Pedrick. *Source: Ancestry.com search index, https://www.ancestry.com/genealogy/records/results?firstName=margaret&lastName=pedrick (secondary/compiled).*
- **The true transatlantic immigrant for this line is Robert Elwell**, not anyone in the supplied tree data. Robert Elwell (c. 1605/1609 England – c. 1683, Dorchester then Gloucester, Essex Co., MA) is the immigrant ancestor documented in the Elwell/Pope 1899 genealogy; his son **Thomas Elwell (1654 Gloucester – 1706 Pilesgrove)** married Sarah Bassett (daughter of William Bassett) in Gloucester on 23 Nov 1675 and *removed* — a domestic migration, not an immigration — from Massachusetts to West Jersey around 1685–1688. This Thomas is the person garbled in the tree's data as "Thomas Dolliver ELWELL 8gg Sr," referenced only inside a "parents" field, not as a standalone person record. *Sources: Elwell/Pope, "The Elwell Family in America" (1899), https://archive.org/details/elwellfamilyinam00elwe; Geni, "Thomas Elwell (1654-1706)," https://www.geni.com/people/Thomas-Elwell/6000000002305665055; WikiTree Elwell-60, https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Elwell-60.*

## Corrections to the tree

- **"Thomas Dolliver ELWELL 8gg Sr" → "Thomas Elwell" (1654–1706).** No source uses "Dolliver" as part of this man's name. The Dolliver connection is a real family link (Robert Elwell's daughter Mary Elwell married Samuel Dolliver in Gloucester in 1654, and Robert Elwell's own wife's surname has been disputed as possibly Dolliver-related) but it belongs to a sibling/spouse relationship, not to Thomas's own name. This looks like a prior researcher's disambiguation tag ("Dolliver" branch) that got folded into the name field. *Evidence: no occurrence of "Dolliver" attached to Thomas Elwell in Van Name or the 1899 Elwell/Pope genealogy; the "8gg" (8th-great-grandparent) suffix confirms this was a generation-counting tag, not a real middle name.*
- **"Sarah Elizabeth Bassett" → "Sarah Bassett."** Every source found (Van Name, Elwell/Pope, Geni, WikiTree) calls her simply Sarah Bassett, daughter of William Bassett of Gloucester; no source supplies a middle name "Elizabeth." Likely an invented disambiguator.
- **"Sarah [all have same mom] Bassett-Ewell" (listed as Thomas Elwell Jr's parent) is a corrupted field, not a person's name.** It is evidently a merge of the note "[all have same mom]" (i.e., a researcher's reminder that several Elwell siblings share the same mother, Sarah Bassett, despite conflicting online trees) with a garbled surname "Bassett-Ewell" (Ewell being a common misspelling/variant of Elwell). The actual mother is Sarah Bassett, wife of Thomas Elwell (1654–1706) — same woman as in the Samuel Elwell record. **Flag as a duplicate/data-corruption artifact requiring a manual fix in the source tree**, not a genealogical finding.
- **"Susanna Post" (spouse of Thomas Elwell Jr) is unsupported.** Van Name gives her only as "Susanna ---" — no maiden name recorded. "Post" could not be corroborated in any source checked and should be downgraded to unsourced/legend pending a primary record (Gloucester or Salem Co. marriage record).
- **Generation-9 "Samuel Jacob Elwell" (b. 1714/1717, d. 1772/1773) is very likely a conflation of two distinct brothers**, both sons of Samuel Elwell (d. 1740): (1) **Samuel Elwell**, who married his first cousin Susanna Elwell (25 Nov 1745, Pittsgrove) and died May 1772; and (2) **Jacob Elwell**, born 1714, died 26 Sep 1773, who married Catherine Du Bois of Ulster Co., NY. The tree entry keeps Jacob's birth year (1714) and precise death date (26 Sep 1773) while also keeping Samuel's spouse (Susanna Elwell) and Samuel's death year (1772/1773 rounding) — i.e., it has merged two people's vital data into one node. This matches the task's expectation of "many unmerged duplicates" and should be split into two separate person records if the underlying database allows it.
- **Place name "Pennsville, Salem, New Jersey" for 1781/1862 events → "Lower Penns Neck Township, Salem Co., NJ."** The township was not renamed Pennsville until a 1965 referendum; using the modern name for 18th/19th-century events is an anachronism (though it likely reflects modern gazetteer auto-fill rather than a factual location error).

## New findings

- Samuel Elwell's 1740 will (abstracted in Van Name) itemizes real estate not present anywhere in the tree: a 60-acre grist-mill property, a plantation of "considerably more than 100 acres," a 50-acre tract bought from William Paulin, and a cedar swamp — useful material detail on the family's economic standing in Pilesgrove Township.
- The Paulin family (later intermarried into this Elwell line via "Hannah Paulin," wife of Isaac Elwell in the tree) already appears as land-sellers to Samuel Elwell as early as the 1730s–40s, which is a plausible (if unconfirmed) explanation for why the two families later intermarried — the "Tamzen Paulin" confusion in one secondary source may in fact be an unconscious foreshadowing of this later, real Paulin marriage one generation down.
- The Elwell family's Massachusetts branch was caught up in the Salem witch trials of 1692–93 — several close relatives of Thomas Elwell (1654–1706) were accused — which is the likely reason the family is well remembered in New England antiquarian genealogies (hence two independent published genealogies exist for this surname, an unusually good documentary base for a colonial line of this size).
- An unrelated documented Elwell/Pedrick marriage exists one branch over: "John (8) Elwell married Georgianna Pedrick" per Van Name-adjacent Salem County records — not the same couple as Elizabeth Elwell/John Pedrick in this line, but confirms the Elwell and Pedrick families were repeatedly intermarried in Salem County across multiple generations, which raises (but does not resolve) the possibility of further duplicate/cross-branch confusion in Pedrick-side records.

## Open questions

- **Where does the documented line actually end?** The Van Name and Elwell/Pope genealogies robustly document the chain Robert Elwell → Thomas Elwell (1654–1706) → Samuel Elwell (d. 1740) → [Samuel or Jacob, his son] → [Susanna Elwell or Catherine Du Bois branch]. Neither source located turns up a son named **Isaac Elwell (b. 1751)** in either brother's children list. This is the true break point in the chain: everything from Isaac Elwell (tree gen 8) downward to Elizabeth Elwell (gen 7) rests on the tree's unsourced assertions only, until a primary record (Salem Co. deed, will, or Quaker meeting minutes) or the full (non-excerpted) text of Van Name's book can confirm Isaac's parentage.
- Is "Hannah Paulin," wife of Isaac Elwell, connected to the William Paulin who sold land to Samuel Elwell in the 1730s? Plausible given the family's documented land dealings in the same township, but unconfirmed.
- Does the full Van Name book (only accessed via search-engine excerpts here, not read cover to cover) contain a fuller children list for "Samuel (4) Elwell x Susanna Elwell" that includes Isaac? A direct read of the Archive.org page images (not just the OCR text stream) is recommended.
- Is Elizabeth Elwell's marriage to John Pedrick independently documented anywhere beyond the circumstantial Ancestry index match (mother "Elizabeth Pedrick" of a Margaret Pedrick born 1804, same town)? A Salem County marriage record or church register would settle this.
- What is the actual surname of Thomas Elwell Jr's wife "Susanna ---"? "Post" is unsupported by every source checked.
