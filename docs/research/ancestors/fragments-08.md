# Fragments 08 — Batch Triage (40 people, generations 7–12)

## Overview

This batch covers 40 surname-fragment ancestors (initials O–P) spanning
17th–18th-century Rhineland/Hesse Germany (Otto/Henckell), colonial Long
Island/New Jersey (Owen, Paullin, Pine, Pedrick, Pell/Pinckney), Sweden
(Persson/Pehrsdotter patronymic lines), Baden-Württemberg Germany (Pfluger),
and Bristol-to-Massachusetts (Pitt/Newell). All are deceased with no living
descendants named in this batch, so full web research was in scope for
everyone. Ten people (in eight family clusters) were selected for targeted
checks. The strongest result is **Captain James Pine of Hempstead**
(1630–1686), a well-documented immigrant with a published NYGBS descendant
article, whose tree entry lists three more children than published sources
support. The most interesting correction concerns **Sabrina Elisabetha
Pfluger**, whose Prince Edward Island destination and death place (Birch
Hill, Lot 49) check out precisely against a dedicated Henckell/Jenkins family
genealogy — but secondary sources call her "Elizabeth (Pfluger) Baum," not
"Sabrina," suggesting a possible prior marriage and/or given-name
transcription error.

## Researched clusters

### Captain James Pine (Pyne) of Hempstead, 1630–1686 — verified

WikiTree (Pine-33) and a New York Genealogical & Biographical Society piece
("James Pyne (Pine) of Hempstead, Long Island, and Some of His Descendants")
both document a James Pine born 1630 in Exeter, Devon, England, son of James
Pine and Edith (Williams) Pine, who settled in Hempstead, Queens (now
Nassau), New York, and died there in 1686 (will dated 12 Oct 1686). He
married a daughter of Thomas Armitage, given as Susan(nah) — matching the
tree's "Susan Armitage." This lines up closely with the tree's entry.

**Correction candidate:** published secondary sources list **nine** children
— Nathaniel, John, Susanna (m. Searing), James, Samuel, Jonathan, Leah (m.
Williams), Sarah, and William. The tree lists **twelve**, adding Agnes Pine
and Benjamin Pine, and listing "Jonathan Pine" twice (once in title case as
a separate child entry from "JONATHAN PINE"). The duplicate Jonathan is
almost certainly the same child double-listed; Agnes and Benjamin are not
corroborated by the sources found this pass and may be additions from a
less careful compiled tree, or may reflect children the WikiTree article
simply omitted — worth flagging as open, not resolved.

### Sabrina Elisabetha Pfluger & the Henckell/Jenkins family of PEI — verified with correction

A dedicated genealogy, *"Nicolaus Henckell the Hessian: A Genealogy of
Jenkins Families of Prince Edward Island"* (cataloged at WorldCat, discussed
on WikiTree Henckell-1 and a Jenkins-family blog), documents Nicolaus
(Nicholas) Henckell, a Hessian soldier born 1755 in Treysa, Hessen, Germany,
who deserted/departed his unit and settled in PEI, anglicizing the family
surname to Jenkins. This matches the tree's "Nicolaus Nicholas Nicolai
Henckell Jenkins," son of Anna Elizabeth Otto (whose own father Andreas Otto
died in Schwalm-Eder-Kreis, Hesse — the same district as Treysa), a
plausible geographic tie.

Web search results (not independently confirmed via a direct fetch) state
that Nicholas Jenkins "married Elizabeth (Pfluger) Baum about 1783 in Birch
Hill, Lot 49, Queen's County, Prince Edward Island" — and the tree's own
entry for Sabrina Elisabetha Pfluger gives her death as "30 July 1823, Birch
Hill Lot 49, Prince Edward Island," an exact place-name match. This is a
strong corroboration of the core PEI destination and the Pfluger surname.

**Open discrepancy:** the secondary source calls her **"Elizabeth"** (with a
maiden or prior-married surname pattern "(Pfluger) Baum"), not "Sabrina."
A separate blog account of the same family names a different, later, second
wife "Elizabeth Robinson," married 1783 in Jamaica, Long Island — suggesting
either two wives are being conflated across sources, or "Sabrina" in the
tree is a transcription variant of "Sabina" (a common period German given
name easily confused with the modern name "Sabrina"). This needs a look at
the actual PEI Family Lineages source cited by the Henckell genealogy before
it can be resolved either way; flagged as an open question below rather than
a firm correction.

### Henry Paullin (Pawlin/Palling), 1644–1723, and the "Captain Henry Sr. Paulin" of Pasquotank, NC — mixed

Geni ("Henry Paullin, Jr.") and WikiTree (Pawlin-7, "Henry Pawlin Jr.
abt.1650–1723") both independently document a Henry Paullin who died in 1723
in Pilesgrove, Salem, NJ, married to Sarah (Sandford/Sanford) — matching the
tree's death year/place and wife exactly. Published sources give his birth
as **circa 1650 in Padbury, Buckinghamshire, England**, a modest but
noteworthy difference from the tree's "1644, ENGLAND." His documented
residence in Bucks County, PA before the move to NJ also matches the tree's
own `[sourced]` residence event citing Bucks County court records
1684–1700 — that citation appears to be a real, checkable published source
(Bucks County PA Quarter Sessions/Common Pleas records have been printed).

However: no source found this pass corroborates the tree's claimed father,
"HENRY Sr. PAULIN Captain, Immigrant" (1620–1656, Buckinghamshire →
Pasquotank, North Carolina). Searches for a Paulin/Pawlin/Palin family in
Pasquotank turned up only a distinct, unrelated Palin/Paulin lineage there
(with unrelated 1660s+ dates and an oral-history detail about marrying a
Native American woman) — not a match to the "Henry Sr." in this tree. The
"Jr." designation on the confirmed Henry Paullin also implies a "Henry Sr."
existed, but nothing ties that senior Henry to Carolina rather than to
England or directly to Bucks County, PA. This looks like a probable
**line conflation**: two same-name families (English/Bucks-PA-NJ Paullins,
and a separate Pasquotank, NC Paulin/Palin family) may have been merged.

### Jacob Pedrick and "Pedricks Neck," Salem County, NJ — contextually verified

Local Salem County history confirms that Roger Pedrick and his wife Rebekah
(Holeman) Pedrick, English Quakers, bought 1,000 acres from John Fenwick in
1675, settled first in Chester, PA, then on the New Jersey side of the
Delaware, and that Quaker meeting minutes from the 1720s refer to the area
as "Pedricks Neck" (later "Pedricksburg," now Pedricktown). This directly
corroborates the tree's place name for Jacob Pedrick (1715–1758), though the
exact parent-to-child link from Roger/Rebekah down to this Jacob was not
traced in the time available — flagged as an open question.

### Ithamar "Tamar" Pell and Sarah Pinckney — verified

Both Geni and WikiTree carry a profile for "Ithamar (Tamar) Pinckney (Pell),"
born about 1684 in Eastchester, NY, died 1770 — an exact match to the tree's
dates. She was daughter of John Pell III and married **William Pinckney** in
1703 in New Rochelle. Her listed children include a "Sarah Pinckney Hunt" —
which explains, and confirms, the tree's Sarah Pinckney (1712–1769) marrying
Caleb Hunt. This resolved what initially looked like a surname anomaly (a
"Pell" mother having a "Pinckney" daughter): the surname is correct because
the father was Pinckney, not Pell.

**Correction:** the tree lists no spouse for Ithamar "Tamar" Pell, but she
should be recorded as married to William Pinckney — this is a missing-fact
correction, not a wrong-value one.

### Mary Pitt, 1607–1684, and the Newell family of Charlestown, MA — partially verified

A Geni profile for "John Newell I (1634–1704)" and a FamilySearch library
catalog entry, *"The Pitt family of Bristol, Gloucester, Charlestown..."*,
both state that John Newell's mother, Mary Pitt, was daughter of William
Pitt, "sheriff of the city of Bristol," and that while her husband Andrew
Newell (a Bristol merchant) stayed in England, Mary emigrated to
Massachusetts with her sons; John Newell died 14 Oct 1704 in Charlestown,
Middlesex Co., MA. This corroborates the core outline of the tree's entry:
Mary Pitt, English merchant-sheriff's daughter, emigrant to Charlestown, MA,
mother of John Newell.

The tree's entry for her father carries an elaborate string of titles —
"1578\*\*\*++# Sir, Captain, Ship Owner, Sheriff of Bristol, East Indies
Company, Merchant, Trader, Militia Officer" — the `***++#` markup is a
telltale sign of unattributed amateur-tree flourish. The underlying facts
(Bristol merchant, East India/Turkey trade, described as "sheriff of the
city of Bristol" in a period record) do check out against WikiTree/Geni/
FamilySearch, so this reads as **exaggerated presentation of a real person**
rather than outright fabrication — a useful distinction to note rather than
a simple thumbs-down.

## Verified facts

- Captain James Pine (Pyne) of Hempstead was born 1630 in Exeter, Devon,
  England, and died 1686 in Hempstead, NY, per WikiTree (Pine-33) and a
  New York Genealogical & Biographical Society descendants article.
- Sabrina Elisabetha Pfluger's death place, "Birch Hill, Lot 49, Prince
  Edward Island," matches the documented marriage location of a Henckell/
  Jenkins family (Nicolaus Henckell m. "Elizabeth (Pfluger) Baum," c.1783,
  Birch Hill, Lot 49) per secondary PEI genealogical sources.
- Henry Paullin (d.1723, Pilesgrove, Salem Co., NJ), married to Sarah
  Sanford/Sandford, is documented on WikiTree (Pawlin-7) and Geni, matching
  the tree's death year, place, and wife.
- "Pedricks Neck" (now Pedricktown), Salem County, NJ, is a documented place
  name tied to the Quaker Pedrick family (Roger & Rebekah Pedrick, arrived
  1675) per local Salem County history sources.
- Ithamar "Tamar" Pell (1684–1770) is documented on Geni and WikiTree as
  "Ithamar (Tamar) Pinckney (Pell)," daughter of John Pell III, married to
  William Pinckney, with a daughter "Sarah Pinckney Hunt" matching the
  tree's Sarah Pinckney m. Caleb Hunt.
- Mary Pitt (Bristol merchant-sheriff's daughter, emigrant to Charlestown,
  MA, mother of John Newell d.1704) is documented via Geni and a FamilySearch
  library catalog entry for "The Pitt family of Bristol, Gloucester,
  Charlestown..."

## Corrections to the tree

- **Duplicate person records:** Joshua Pond (I182541959437 and
  I182541966277) and Mary Phillips (I182541959564 and I182541966209) each
  appear twice under different person IDs with identical data. These should
  be merged/de-duplicated in the underlying tree.
- **Missing spouse:** Ithamar "Tamar" Pell has no spouse recorded in the
  tree, but secondary sources consistently show her married to William
  Pinckney — evidence: Geni/WikiTree profile "Ithamar (Tamar) Pinckney
  (Pell)."
- **Henry Paullin's birth year/place** — tree gives 1644, "ENGLAND"; Geni and
  WikiTree give circa 1650, Padbury, Buckinghamshire, England. Minor (~6
  year) discrepancy, evidence: Geni "Henry Paullin, Jr." and WikiTree
  Pawlin-7.
- **Captain James Pine's children list** — tree lists 12 children including
  a duplicated "Jonathan Pine" entry and two names (Agnes, Benjamin) not
  found in the WikiTree/NYGBS published list of 9. Likely tree-side
  duplication/over-addition; not fully resolved (see open questions).
- **Mary Susannah Owen sharing her husband's surname ("Owen") as her own
  maiden name** reads as a tree data artifact rather than a genealogical
  fact — no evidence found either way, flagged as a likely error pattern
  rather than a confirmed correction.

## New findings

- Sabrina Elisabetha Pfluger appears in secondary PEI genealogical sources
  as "Elizabeth (Pfluger) Baum," implying a **prior marriage to a Baum**
  that is not recorded anywhere in the tree — not previously known from the
  tree data.
- Ithamar "Tamar" Pell's husband, **William Pinckney** (m. 1703, New
  Rochelle, NY), is a wholly new fact not present in the tree.
- The Henckell/Jenkins family's documented emigration route (Hessian soldier
  Nicolaus Henckell, deserting/departing his regiment from Ziegenhain in
  1776, eventually settling PEI) gives plausible historical texture to how
  the German Otto/Pfluger family lines ended up in Canada — not previously
  explained by the tree's bare vital-event data.
- "Pedricks Neck" as a place name is independently documented back to Roger
  and Rebekah (Holeman) Pedrick's 1675 purchase from John Fenwick, giving a
  concrete origin story for the family/place-name link implicit in Jacob
  Pedrick's (1715–1758) birth/death location.

## Open questions

- Is "Sabrina Elisabetha Pfluger" the same woman as the "Elizabeth (Pfluger)
  Baum" and/or "Elizabeth Robinson" named in different secondary PEI
  sources? The name and marriage-order conflict needs the underlying PEI
  Family Lineages submission or a parish/family Bible record to resolve.
- Is "HENRY Sr. PAULIN Captain, Immigrant" (Buckinghamshire → Pasquotank,
  NC, d.1656) a real ancestor of the confirmed NJ Paullin line, or is this a
  conflation with an unrelated Pasquotank Palin/Paulin family? No source
  found this pass links the two.
- Does Jacob Pedrick (1715–1758) descend directly from Roger & Rebekah
  Pedrick, the documented 1675 settlers of "Pedricks Neck"? Plausible but
  not traced generation-by-generation this pass.
- What is the actual list of Captain James Pine's children — 9 (per
  WikiTree/NYGBS) or 12 (per tree)? Worth checking the NYGBS article in full
  and/or Hempstead town records directly.
- George Festus Owen II and Mary Susannah Owen (Cork, Ireland → Brookhaven,
  NY) turned up no independent corroboration this pass — worth a dedicated
  search in Brookhaven town records or Bayles' *History of Suffolk County*.
- John Pine (1745–1779), marked `[sourced]` in the tree for both birth and
  death, was not corroborated by any record found this pass despite several
  targeted searches — worth checking NJ Revolutionary War pension/muster
  records directly (Gloucester/Salem militia).
