# James C. VanEmburgh (1799–1860) & Lucy Lucia Hale (1806–?)

Generation 6 direct ancestors. Researched 3 July 2026 from web sources (Archive.org vital-records transcription; IAGenWeb Allamakee County, Iowa census/cemetery/marriage/obituary transcriptions; cross-reference to the already-researched Generation 5 dossier for their daughter Almina). **Note on task mismatch:** the task header supplied for this job named "Jean Baptiste Drolet & Marie Louise Bergevin," but the tree-data file actually provided (`A37.json`) is for James C. VanEmburgh and Lucy Lucia Hale, matching the `dossier_path` filename given. This dossier follows the supplied tree-data file, as instructed when the header and data disagree.

## Overview

James C. VanEmburgh, born about 1799 in New York, and Lucy Lucia Hale, born 8 December 1806 in Tyringham, Massachusetts, migrated west and were living at Lansing, Allamakee County, Iowa, by the time of James's death in March 1860 — likely explaining why neither appears in the county's June 1860 census enumeration. Direct records for the couple themselves are very thin online; the strongest confirmation comes indirectly, through their daughter Almina's 1904 Iowa death register, which names her parents as "James VanEmburg" and "Lucy (maiden name unknown)." Lucy's own birth is independently confirmed in the printed vital records of Tyringham, Massachusetts.

## Verified facts

- **Lucy Hale's birth date matches a primary source exactly.** *Vital Records of Tyringham, Massachusetts, to the Year 1850* (NEHGS-style town vital-records volume, digitized at Archive.org) records under the Hale family: "Lucy, d. James and Lucy, Dec. 8, 1806." This is an exact match to the tree's birth date (8 Dec 1806) and confirms her parents were named James and Lucy Hale — consistent with the tree's listed parents "Lucy LNU" and "James Hale." — Archive.org, *Vital Records of Tyringham, Massachusetts*
- **The couple's parentage of Almina is corroborated by an independent Iowa civil record.** The Allamakee County, Iowa death register (1902–1904, compiled by Richard O'Brien from state/county sources) lists their daughter as "Dodds, Lucia Almina ... Father/Mother: James VanEmburg / Lucy unkn," i.e., a woman named Lucy whose maiden name the informant did not know — consistent with "Lucy Lucia Hale" as the tree gives it. (Sourced and cited in the sibling Generation 5 dossier for David Crist & Almina VanAmburg, `docs/research/ancestors/gen05-david-hawkins-crist-almina-lucia-lucy-va.md`.) — IAGenWeb Allamakee death records
- **James's absence from the 1860 census of Lansing township is consistent with the tree's death date.** The tree gives his death as March 1860 at Lansing; the 1860 federal census for Lansing township, Allamakee County, was enumerated 3–4 June 1860. No VanEmburgh/VanAmburg household appears in the transcribed Lansing township pages, nor in any of the other nine Allamakee townships transcribed for 1860 (Iowa, Center, French Creek, Union City, Waterloo, Hanover, Union Prairie, Paint Creek, Taylor) — negative evidence consistent with James having died before enumeration and Lucy/family not being enumerated separately under that surname. — IAGenWeb Allamakee 1860 census transcriptions

## Corrections to the tree

- **Death place spelling: "Almakee" → "Allamakee."** The tree's death-place field for both James and Lucy reads "Lansing, Almakee, Iowa, USA." The correct county name is Allamakee County, Iowa (organized 1851; seat Waukon). This looks like a transcription/OCR-style typo rather than a substantive genealogical error, but should be corrected in the tree data.
- No other tree facts were contradicted by records found; most (birth year 1799, birthplace New York, death "Mar 1860," Lucy's birth "8 Dec 1806, Tyringham, Massachusetts," and the parents "Lucy LNU"/"James Hale") are either confirmed or not contradicted by any record located.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **A cluster of VanEmberg burials at Oak Hill Cemetery, Lansing Township, Allamakee Co.**, plausibly James and Lucy's children or other close kin, though no direct parent/child link to James C. VanEmburgh was documented: John VanEmberg (1826–1 Jul 1892); Frank J. VanEmberg (d. 1881; grave-registered as Co. G, 1st Iowa Cavalry, Civil War veteran); Lucretia VanEmberg née Sales (d. 1855); and an unnamed VanEmberg child (b. 15 Mar 1878 – d. c. 7 Sep 1879, whooping cough, per the Lansing Journal of 10 Sep 1879, which places the family at Columbus, the same Lansing Township hamlet where Almina/Mrs. W. P. Dodds later lived). John VanEmberg's birth year (1826) would make him a very plausible son of James (b. 1799), born when James was about 27. — IAGenWeb Oak Hill Cemetery transcriptions (Woodmansee/Moritz merged lists)
- **A "VanEmburgh, Jas. F." appears as a Lansing civic figure in 1886**, listed in the town's Patron's Directory as "money loaner & member of City Council." Given the 26-year gap after James C.'s 1860 death, this is almost certainly a son or grandson (perhaps "James F." to distinguish from the father "James C."), not the immigrant himself — an unproven but suggestive lead that the family remained prominent in Lansing after the 1860s. — IAGenWeb Allamakee Land/Patron records
- **Possible Hale kin at Lansing.** Oak Hill Cemetery also holds a cluster of Hale burials — Elisha Hale (1824–1884), Elza Hale (1821–1883), Jane A. Hale (1831 Nov 10–1922 Apr 04), Albert Elisha Hale (1849 Jul 17–1931 Feb 22), Clara Jane Hale (1854–1931), Albert Hale (1851–1925), Etta Hale (1859–1937), and William Hale (1862–1904) — plus an "Elijah Hale, one of Lansing's earliest settlers" named in the 1882 county history (his house hosted Lansing's first sermon in 1851) and an early-born "Alberta Hale." These Hales are too young to be Lucy's own siblings (she was born 1806) but could plausibly be nephews/a brother's family who followed her and James west to Iowa — this link is speculative and unconfirmed by any direct record.
- **Lucy Hale's likely paternal grandparents.** The same Tyringham vital-records volume records a birth "James, s. Josiah (Hail) and Abagail, Feb. 20, 1785" — a James Hale who, if he is Lucy's father, would have been 21 at her 1806 birth. This is plausible but not proven; the volume gives no marriage record tying this James Hale to a wife named Lucy, so the identification of Lucy's father James Hale with this specific 1785-born James Hale (son of Josiah and Abigail Hale) is an inference only, not a documented fact.

## Open questions

- No Find A Grave, obituary, or direct census/land record was located naming James C. VanEmburgh or Lucy Lucia Hale themselves (as opposed to their daughter or presumed relatives). Find A Grave search-engine indexing did not surface a memorial for either; a manual, logged-in Find A Grave and Ancestry/FamilySearch search (full census index, Iowa land patents, probate) would likely be far more productive than the free-web tools used here.
- Exact relationship of John VanEmberg (1826–1892), Frank J. VanEmberg (d. 1881, Civil War veteran), and "Jas. F. VanEmburgh" (Lansing city councilman, 1886) to James C. VanEmburgh — sons? Nephews? No document found states this directly.
- Where and when did James and Lucy marry, and when/why did they leave New York/Massachusetts for Iowa? Not found.
- Lucy Lucia Hale's death date and place were not found; the tree lists no death date and gives only "Lansing Almakee, Iowa, USA" as place. No burial record for her was located under Hale or VanEmburgh at Oak Hill or Wexford cemeteries in the sources checked.
- James C. VanEmburgh's exact 1860 burial location (Oak Hill Cemetery, Lansing, would be the obvious candidate given family later buried there, but no entry for a "James" VanEmberg/VanEmburgh appears in the transcribed Oak Hill lists).
- 1850 census household of the family (would show James, Lucy, and children including young Almina together) — not found in the IAGenWeb 1850 Allamakee transcription; the family may have arrived in Iowa after 1850, or the transcription may be incomplete for their township.
- Identity/parentage of the presumed grandparents Josiah and Abigail Hale of Tyringham — not further researched.
