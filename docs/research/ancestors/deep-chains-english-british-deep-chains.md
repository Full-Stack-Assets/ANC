# Credibility Audit: English/British Deep Chains (Generations 13–25)

## Overview

This audit covers 544 tree entries spanning generations 13–25 (roughly 1370–1720 in birth years, though most cluster 1450–1650), representing the deepest, least-verifiable branches of the tree: English/Welsh gentry and nobility claims, a handful of continental European (German/Swiss/Scandinavian) village lines, and the English-origin research for several Great Migration immigrants to New England. This is a credibility audit of chains, not a person-by-person research pass. The generation-13 "trunk" is largely ordinary parish families with a scattering of genuine Great Migration gateway ancestors; the generations 16–25 "tip" is dominated by a small number of interlocking noble/gentry pedigree claims recycled from an unsourced online mega-tree (heavy Ancestry/Geni/WikiTree/FamilySearch compiled-tree fingerprints: composite hyphenated surnames, invented honorifics, and titles that don't exist in the peerage). Several of the specific structural errors the internal audit flagged (children born after a parent's death, an ancestor duplicated as his own parent/grandparent) were independently reproduced by a systematic date-consistency check of this file and are documented below with the real-world explanation for how each arose.

## Verified facts

- **Howard/Tilney/Bourchier (gen 17–18), core identities are real and well documented.** Elizabeth Tilney (b. 1445/46 Ashwellthorpe, Norfolk – d. 4 Apr 1497 Thetford), daughter of Sir Frederick Tilney and Elizabeth Say, first married Sir Humphrey Bourchier (d. 14 Apr 1471, killed at the Battle of Barnet), then on 30 Apr 1472 married Thomas Howard, later 2nd Duke of Norfolk. — [Wikipedia: Elizabeth Tilney, Countess of Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Tilney,_Countess_of_Surrey); [Wikipedia: Thomas Howard, 2nd Duke of Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Howard,_2nd_Duke_of_Norfolk)
- **Grey, Baron Grey de Wilton (gen 15–17), documented peerage.** William Grey, 13th Baron Grey de Wilton (c.1509–1562) was the son of Edmund Grey, 9th Baron Grey de Wilton (d. 1511) and Florence Hastings, eldest daughter of Sir Ralph Hastings — matching the tree's Edmund Degrey/Florence (Grey) de Hastings couple and their son William. — [Wikipedia: William Grey, 13th Baron Grey de Wilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Grey,_13th_Baron_Grey_de_Wilton)
- **Mansel of Oxwich/Margam, Glamorgan (gen 16–18), documented Welsh gentry.** Sir Rice (Rhys) Mansel (1487–1559), Vice-Admiral and High Sheriff of Glamorgan, purchaser of Margam Abbey at the Dissolution, was son of Jenkyn Mansel of Oxwich Castle and Edith, daughter and heir of Sir George Kyme of Well Hall, Eltham, Kent — matching the tree's Jenkin Mansel/Edith Maria Kyme couple. — [Wikipedia: Rice Mansel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_Mansel); [WikiTree: Jenkin Mansel](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Mansel-56)
- **Stanley/Le Strange, Barons Strange of Knockin (gen 18), documented peerage.** George Stanley, 9th Baron Strange (c.1460–1503), heir of Thomas Stanley 1st Earl of Derby, married in 1482 Joan le Strange, 9th Baroness Strange (c.1463–1514) — matching the tree's couple and dates closely. — [Wikipedia: George Stanley, 9th Baron Strange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stanley,_9th_Baron_Strange)
- **Throckmorton of Coughton Court, Warwickshire (gen 19–23), the underlying family is genuine, long-documented gentry.** Sir John Throckmorton, Under-Treasurer of England (d. 1445), married Eleanor de Spineto, daughter/coheir of Sir Guy de Spineto of Coughton; a later Thomas Throckmorton (Sheriff of Warwickshire/Leicestershire, d. 1472) married Margaret Olney, daughter/coheir of Sir Robert Olney of Weston Underwood, Bucks — the real originals behind the tree's "De La Spine" and "Olney of Weston Underwood" names. — [soc.genealogy.medieval / Throckmorton pedigree summary](https://groups.google.com/g/soc.genealogy.medieval/c/w75JP91mL6s); [Cracroft's Peerage: Throckmorton of Coughton](http://www.cracroftspeerage.co.uk/throckmortonb1642.htm)
- **Tracy of Toddington, Gloucestershire (gen 23–25), documented gentry with a well-known reformer.** William Tracy (c.1460–1530), son of Sir Henry Tracy (d. c.1506) of Toddington, was posthumously tried and condemned for heresy in 1531 over the Protestant wording of his will, and his son Richard Tracy publicly defended it — a well-documented episode in the English Reformation. — [Wikipedia/Grokipedia: William Tracy](https://grokipedia.com/page/William_Tracy); [History of Parliament: Richard Tracy](https://www.historyofparliamentonline.org/volume/1509-1558/member/tracy-richard-1501-69)
- **Burgoyne of Sutton, Bedfordshire (gen 23–24), documented gentry, ancestors of the Burgoyne baronets.** John Burgoyne acquired the manor of Enderbies in Sutton, Bedfordshire in 1529 and died there in 1540, leaving it to sons Thomas, Bartholomew (a churchman), and Robert — the same family later produced the Burgoyne baronets of Sutton (created 1641) and General "Gentleman Johnny" Burgoyne of the American Revolution. — [WikiTree: John Burgoyne (1460–1540)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Burgoyne-48); [Wikipedia: Burgoyne baronets of Sutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgoyne_baronets_of_Sutton_(1642))
- **John Whitney, immigrant to Watertown, Massachusetts (gen 13), a documented Great Migration gateway ancestor.** Baptized 20 Jul 1592 at St Margaret's, Westminster, son of Thomas and Mary (Bray) Whitney; lived at Isleworth; emigrated 1635 on the *Elizabeth and Ann*; died 1 Jun 1673 in Watertown, Massachusetts. This is one of the best-documented Great Migration immigrant lines (Whitney Research Group / NEHGS-vetted). — [Whitney Research Group family page](http://wiki.whitneygen.org/wrg/index.php/Family:Whitney,_John_(1592-1673)); [FamilySearch](https://ancestors.familysearch.org/en/L525-1J4/john-whitney-1592-1673)
- **Richard Ingersoll, immigrant to Salem, Massachusetts (gen 13), documented.** Born 10 Mar 1587 in Edworth, Bedfordshire, son of George Ingersoll and Alicia Hankyns; recruited (with a "Richard Haward") by the Massachusetts Bay Company in 1629 for Salem; died 21 Jul 1644 in Salem. — [howderfamily.com sourced compilation](https://www.howderfamily.com/genealogy/surnames_i/INGERSOLL_Richard.html); [WikiTree](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Ingersoll-4)
- **Sir Anthony Cooke of Gidea Hall's marriage is real, but the tree has lost him.** Anthony Cooke (1501/1505–1576), Tudor humanist and tutor to Edward VI, married Anne Fitzwilliam, daughter of Sir William Fitzwilliam, Sheriff of London; their children included Sir Richard Cooke and the famous "Cooke sisters" (Mildred Cecil, Anne Bacon, etc.). — [Wikipedia: Anthony Cooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Cooke); [History of Parliament: Cooke, Sir Anthony](https://www.historyofparliamentonline.org/volume/1509-1558/member/cooke-sir-anthony-15056-76)
- **A person named John de Hardy was a real early-Tudor London citizen — but not the person the tree describes.** FamilySearch-linked compiled records show a Sir John de Hardy (b. c.1510, d. 1546), Alderman of Aldersgate then Farringdon Within (1524–1535), Sheriff of London 1527–1528, and Master of the Haberdashers' Company 1528–29, who married Mary Stanley. He was a real civic officeholder, but see "Corrections" below.

## Corrections to the tree

- **Howard/Tilney/Boleyn chain — impossible parentage from a merged double marriage.** The tree (gen 17) shows "Elizabeth Howard" (=Elizabeth Tilney) as wife of Humphrey Bourchier, and lists as their joint children **Margaret Bourchier, Thomas Howard, Elizabeth Boleyn, and Muriel Knyvet**. This is wrong and internally impossible: Humphrey Bourchier died in 1471; his and Elizabeth Tilney's only children were John Bourchier, 2nd Baron Berners, and two daughters. Thomas Howard (later 3rd Duke of Norfolk, b. 1473), Elizabeth Howard (Boleyn's mother), and Muriel Howard (m. Knyvet) were children of Elizabeth Tilney's **second** marriage, to Thomas Howard, 2nd Duke of Norfolk, in 1472 — i.e., they were born *after* Bourchier's death, from a different father. **This is exactly the "children born after parental death" pattern the internal audit flagged**, and it arises from a classic tree-compiling error: collapsing a twice-married woman's two families into one, and mislabeling her by her second married surname (Howard) instead of her birth surname (Tilney). Correct fix: split "Elizabeth Howard" into Elizabeth Tilney (m1. Bourchier, one son + 2 daughters; m2. Thomas Howard 2nd Duke of Norfolk, 11 children including Thomas, Elizabeth, Muriel).
- **Mansel/Kyme — same pattern, smaller scale.** The tree gives Edith Maria Kyme a death date of 1473, but her son by Jenkin Mansel, Sir Rhys Mansel, was not born until 1487 — 14 years after her stated death. Either the death date is wrong or the mother attribution is; this is the same "child after parent's death" defect as above, on a real, otherwise well-documented couple.
- **Throckmorton "De La Spine"/"Bosum"/"Olney" cluster (gen 19–23) — a real 15th-century marriage relocated ~130–150 years too late.** The real Eleanor de Spineto (daughter/coheir of Sir Guy de Spineto of Coughton) married Sir John Throckmorton, Under-Treasurer of England, who died in 1445 — placing that marriage in the very early 1400s. The tree instead places its "Sir Guy DE LA Spine" and "Lady Eleanor De La Spine Spinney" at generation 21–22 with birth years around 1518–1538, roughly five to six generations later than the real couple. The distinctive names (Guy/Eleanor de Spineto → "de la Spine") are too specific to be coincidence; this looks like the same real marriage duplicated and re-dated into the 16th century, then given compound/garbled surnames ("Bosum-Olney," "Burgoyne-Bosum," "Holt-van Wyke-de La Spine\Spinney, of Wyke") that are not attested spellings in any Warwickshire/Bedfordshire visitation or peerage source found. Treat generations 19–23 of this specific "De La Spine"/"Bosum" thread as unreliable even though the underlying surnames (Throckmorton, Besford, Olney, Burgoyne) are genuine.
- **"Sir John De Hardy, Lord Mayor of London, Knight of the Garter" — inflated/invented honors.** A complete list of Lord Mayors of London for 1510–1546 (cross-checked via Wikipedia/British History Online) does not include anyone named Hardy or de Hardy. The FamilySearch-derived record for this individual actually describes him only as an Alderman (1524–1535) and Sheriff of London (1527–1528) — a real, notable civic role, but not the mayoralty. There is also no "Sir John de Hardy" on the rolls of the Order of the Garter for this period. Both titles ("Lord Mayor," "Knight of the Garter") appear to be embellishments layered onto a real minor civic official; downgrade to "Alderman/Sheriff of London" and drop the Garter claim. This node is also duplicated in the tree as the same "Lady Mary Margaret Stanley" (b.1500) appearing as spouse to both "John de Hardy" (gen 17/20) and, separately, to "John Poole" (gen 17) — with an identical list of children in both marriages. That is a merge artifact, not two real marriages.
- **"Sir Hugh I Childers Childress III" — the same person duplicated as his own parent and grandparent.** The tree contains four separate entries, at generations 17, 18, 19, and (as father) 20, all for a person with identical birth/death dates (b. Mar 1500 East Retford – d. 24 Sep 1571 Cantley), each generation citing the previous as "parent." This is the internal audit's flagged "impossible lifespan"/self-referential ancestor pattern in its purest form: a single real (or semi-real) individual has been cloned across several tree generations due to a repeated-name merge error, not an actual dynasty of four consecutive "Hugh Childers"-es born in the same year.
- **Robert Mansfield of Exeter → Lynn, Massachusetts — fabricated noble ancestor grafted onto a real immigrant.** The tree gives Robert Mansfield (1590–1666), a real, sourced Great Migration figure (b. Exeter 1590, m. Grace Townsend 1620, d. Lynn, Massachusetts 1666), a father titled "Sir John Winton, 'Earl of Minorites,' Mansfield" (1572–1610). "Earl of Minorites" is not a real peerage title — "Minorites" is the name for Franciscan friars (Order of Friars Minor), not a place or earldom, and no such creation exists in the Complete Peerage. This looks like an attempt to manufacture a noble father for the Massachusetts immigrant by borrowing the prestige (and confusable surname) of the real, documented Mansel family of Margam/Oxwich, Glamorgan (see Verified facts). Treat any claimed descent of Robert Mansfield from the Welsh Mansels as unsupported legend.
- **John Whitney's name and paternity are garbled.** The documented immigrant is "John Whitney" (1592–1673), son of Thomas and Mary (Bray) Whitney of Isleworth/London. The tree renders him "William John James Whitney," an invented compound given name not found in any period or compiled source, and omits his father Thomas Whitney entirely (only "Lady Mary Beth Bray of Lambert Marsh" is listed as parent).
- **Sir Anthony Cooke has been dropped from his own family.** The tree's "Sir Richard Edward Woodstocke Cooke of Gidea Hall" (b. 1521) is listed with only one parent, "Lady Anne of Brittany Fitzwilliam (COOKE)" — the real Anne Fitzwilliam, wife of Sir Anthony Cooke — but Anthony Cooke himself, one of the best-documented Tudor gentlemen in England, does not appear as a parent anywhere in this node. The epithets "of Brittany" (for Anne Fitzwilliam, an Essex/London family with no Breton connection) and "Woodstocke" (for the son) do not appear in any source found and read as invented flourish typical of this tree's naming style.

## New findings

- The internal audit's two flagged defect classes — "impossible lifespans" and "children born after parental deaths" — are not scattered noise in this segment; they cluster overwhelmingly at exactly the points where the tree tries to graft real English nobility onto other lines (Howard/Boleyn, Mansel/Mansfield, Throckmorton/De La Spine, Childers). That is a useful diagnostic: wherever this tree assigns someone a title ("Sir," "Lady," "Baron," "Earl," "Countess," "Lord Mayor," "Knight of the Garter") in generations 15–23, it is worth extra scrutiny, because the rate of fabrication/conflation in titled nodes is far higher than in the untitled parish-register nodes of generation 13–14.
- Several generation-13 nodes cluster around specific, well-attested emigrant communities that are independently good candidates for future targeted research: Kingston-upon-Thames, Surrey (Cheesman/Rason/Lilley/Warman families), Holme-upon-Spalding-Moor, Yorkshire (Crosby/Webster/Brigham families), and Bristol merchant families (Pitt/Gibbes). None of these were deeply checked in this pass, but none showed the title-inflation red flags seen in the gentry chains.
- A cluster of German/Saarland village families (Christ of Solothurn/Frankfurt; Heimann/Wöhlers of Bietschied/Puttlingen, Saarland; Germen/Becker of Nalbach, Saarland) at generations 13–18 carries specific village-level detail (parish names, exact dates) typical of German church-book (Kirchenbuch) research rather than noble-pedigree fabrication. No independent verification was attempted in this pass; there is no fabrication signature (no titles, no suspicious compound surnames), so this cluster is plausibly genuine parish-register descent, just unsourced in the tree ("unsourced" flags throughout) and not confirmed against primary Saarland church records.
- A small Swedish/Scandinavian branch (e.g., "16:15680 SÖFFRING KRABBE," "15:15681 ELIN," Grangärde/Ludvika Bruk parish, Dalarna) carries Scandinavian ahnentafel-style numeric prefixes characteristic of a Swedish genealogical database export (e.g., DISBYT/Anbytarforum), suggesting a distinct, probably legitimate, source lineage separate from the English gentry chains, again not independently verified here.

## Open questions

- Is the tree's "Margaret Pouncefoot" as William Tracy's wife (gen 23–24) an alternate/second marriage, or a substitution error for the documented wife Margaret Throckmorton, daughter of Sir Thomas Throckmorton of Tortworth? Both appear in different sources; needs a dedicated look at Gloucestershire visitation pedigrees.
- Was the real "Bartholomew Burgoyne" (recorded elsewhere as "in the Church," i.e., clergy) actually the father of "Sir William Bosum," or has the tree attached an unrelated Bartholomew/Anne Freville couple to the wrong Burgoyne generation? The tree also carries two near-duplicate "Bartholomew Burgoyne" / "Anne Freville" entries (IDs I182623664658/655 and I182623664625/602) with slightly different birthplaces and burial details for the same couple — worth reconciling as a probable duplicate-record issue similar to the Childers loop.
- Is there a genuine 16th-century Alexander Besford/Agnes Besford couple (gen 22–23, dates 1497–1567) distinct from the 14th-century Alexander Besford whose daughter Agnes married Thomas Throckmorton in/around 1380? Worcestershire gentry families did recycle ancestral given names across generations, so a later, distinct couple is possible — this needs a specific Worcestershire visitation check rather than assuming the whole node is a chronological duplicate.
- The "Sir John De Hardy" node's real civic career (Alderman/Sheriff, 1524–1535) should be checked against London civic records (e.g., "Beaven's Aldermen of the City of London") to confirm identity and correct the false "Lord Mayor"/"Garter" claims rather than simply deleting the node.
