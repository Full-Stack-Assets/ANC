## Overview

Miriam Johnson — also recorded as "Cohawkin," likely reflecting the Cohawkin Creek/Cohawkin area of Pilesgrove Township, Salem Co., NJ where she died — was born (per the tree, unsourced) in 1680 in Pilesgrove, Salem, New Jersey, and died 8 January 1722 in Cohawkin, Pilesgrove. Her parents are tree-listed as Henry Johnson (I182541964251, b. "Bet. 1645–1673" Philadelphia — a nonsensical machine-generated range anchored to a birthplace that did not exist as a settlement before 1682, already separately flagged as `anachronistic-place` at gen 12) and Miriam Howell (I182541964296, b. 1664 Tamworth, Warwickshire, England; d. 1693 Solebury, Bucks Co., Pennsylvania). Her one listed child is Tamzen/Tamzon Standford (I182541958337, b. 1692 Pilesgrove; d. 16 Feb 1740 Pilesgrove; m. Samuel Elwell, I182381534759).

The math does not work even generously: if Miriam Johnson's own birth year is nudged earlier to make her older at Tamzen's 1692 birth, it collides with her mother Miriam Howell's own 1664 birth — Miriam Johnson cannot plausibly have been born much before the early-to-mid 1680s (her mother would need to be an adult), which still leaves her only 6–12 years old in 1692 under any correction. That rules out "simple birth-year typo" as a fix, the way it worked for some other flagged records in this tree.

External research points to a different, better-documented explanation: Tamzen Standford's independently-documented identity, per WikiTree, is as a **Paullin**, not a Standford — daughter of a Bucks County, Pennsylvania Quaker family that relocated to Salem/Pilesgrove, NJ, exactly where "our" Tamzen married and died. Strikingly, this is the *same* Paullin family independently confirmed elsewhere in this very tree: William Paullin (`I182541959101`) — Tamzen's own brother, per WikiTree — was already matched, in an unrelated resolved case (`docs/research/ancestors/gen11-anne-henley.md`), against WikiTree profile Paullin-17 on his exact birth and death (abt. 1682 Bucks Co., PA – abt. 12 Nov 1757 Pilesgrove, Salem, NJ), and his son Henry Paullin (`I182541958181`, b. 1715) is also already in this dataset. That prior, independent research thread and this one converge on the same real family from two different flagged errors (Anne Henley/Elizabeth Whitlock, and this Miriam Johnson/Tamzen Standford case), which meaningfully raises confidence that the Paullin identification is correct rather than coincidental.

## Corrections to the tree

- **The parent-child link from Miriam Johnson/Cohawkin to Tamzen/Tamzon Standford is very likely a mis-attachment, not a birth-year error.** No adjustment to Miriam's own birth year resolves the age-12 problem without also violating her own mother's 1664 birth year (see Overview). The far better-corroborated explanation is that "our" Tamzen is the Tamzen Paullin documented above — daughter of Henry Paullin, not of Thomas Standford and this Miriam — and that the "Standford + Miriam Cohawkin" parentage in this tree traces back to an uncorroborated compiled Ancestry page ("Tamzen OR Tamzon Mulford STANDFORD 1692–1740," which itself already shows a garbled extra name, "Mulford," that more cleanly belongs to one of Tamzen's own daughters).
- **Not certain**: Tamzen's birth year varies between sources (1688 WikiTree "guess" vs. 1692 in this tree/Ancestry), and no source names Henry Paullin's wife (Tamzen's mother), so the Paullin identification, while materially stronger than the Standford one, is not proven beyond doubt. Per repo convention, the machine-owned `relationships` arrays were left unedited; `manual.notes` were added to Miriam Johnson (I182541958360) and Tamzen Standford (I182541958337) documenting the likely mis-attachment rather than re-pointing the graph.
- **No change made to Miriam Johnson's own recorded birth (1680) or death (8 Jan 1722)** — both are unsourced in this tree but not otherwise contradicted by anything found in this research pass. Her true children, if the Tamzen link is indeed mistaken, are undetermined.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **A real, well-documented Paullin family** of Bucks County, PA → Salem/Pilesgrove, NJ (Henry Paullin Sr. and at least six children) exists independently of this tree and is not reflected in it at all — offered here as context for the Tamzen mis-attachment, not added as new tree data (no record ids exist for Henry Paullin Sr. or Tamzen's siblings in this dataset, so nothing was fabricated).
- **The specific death date, 8 Jan 1722, for Miriam Johnson/Cohawkin** is oddly precise for an otherwise entirely unsourced record, suggesting some real underlying document (a Salem County or Quaker meeting record) may exist for her, even though it could not be located on the open web in this pass.

## Open questions

- **Who were Miriam Johnson/Cohawkin's real children, if any?** If Tamzen Standford is indeed a misattached Paullin, this record may have no verified children left in the tree. No alternative child was identified.
- **Is "Cohawkin" a surname or a place-derived nickname?** Cohawkin (Creek/area) is a real Pilesgrove Township, Salem Co., NJ place name, matching her own death location — the "Cohawkin" in her name is more likely a place-based alias than a true surname, but this was not conclusively resolved.
- **Was there ever a real marriage between a Thomas Standford and a Miriam (of any surname) in Salem County, NJ?** No primary marriage record was located on the open web; several distinct "Thomas Stanford/Standford" men (with widely differing dates — e.g., one with a 1670–1772 lifespan, which is itself questionable) turned up in searches, adding to the sense that this branch's data is a tangle of same-name coincidences rather than one documented couple.
- **Henry Johnson and Miriam Howell (Miriam Johnson's own parents)** — Henry Johnson's record already carries a separately-flagged anachronistic Philadelphia birthplace (gen 12, direct-line-issues.md) and was not further investigated here beyond noting that it reinforces this branch's general unreliability.
- **Henry Paullin's wife's name** (mother of the real Tamzen Paullin) was not found in the open-web sources checked.
