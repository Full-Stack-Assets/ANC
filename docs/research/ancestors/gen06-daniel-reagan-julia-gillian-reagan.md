## Overview

Daniel Reagan and Julia Gillian Reagan were both born in Ireland — Daniel
about 1800 (per the 1880 census-derived age) or about 1811 (per the 1851
census-derived age), Julia about 1816 — and by 1851 the couple was living in
Woolwich, Kent, England, a garrison-and-arsenal town on the Thames southeast
of London that drew Irish labor and soldiery in this period. By 1880 they had
crossed the Atlantic and were living in Boston, Suffolk County, Massachusetts,
placing them among the many thousands of Irish (and Irish-in-Britain) families
who re-emigrated to the industrializing northeastern United States. They
raised at least six children — Catherine, Jeremiah, Michael, John, James, and
Mary — whose surname is recorded in tree data in three different spellings
(Ragan, Regan, Reagan), consistent with the known variant pattern for this
family line. This deep dive found no independently viewable primary record
for Daniel or Julia beyond the two censuses the tree already cites (both are
login-gated at Ancestry/FamilySearch); it did surface general historical
context for Woolwich's Irish population and the Ireland→England→America
migration pattern, and it flags an internal inconsistency in Daniel's two
tree-recorded birth years.

## Corrections to the tree

No tree fact was found to be affirmatively *wrong* (i.e., contradicted by an
independent record), but one internal inconsistency and one open naming
question are worth flagging:

- **Daniel's birth year: "abt 1800" vs. "abt 1811," both marked [sourced]** —
  this is not necessarily an error so much as an unreconciled discrepancy
  between two genuine but independent age-derived estimates: the 1851 England
  census (implying b. ~1811, i.e., age ~40) and the 1880 U.S. census (implying
  b. ~1800, i.e., age ~80). A ~29-year gap between the two censuses, and the
  well-documented tendency of 19th-century enumerators/informants — especially
  elderly immigrants uncertain of their exact age — to round or misstate ages,
  makes an 11-year drift plausible without either record being "wrong" in the
  sense of a data-entry error. Recommend the tree carry both as alternate
  estimates (as it currently does) rather than picking one, unless the
  underlying census images are reviewed to check for a transcription error.
- **"Julia Gillian Reagan" — is "Gillian" a middle name, a maiden name, or a
  transcription artifact?** No independent record was found this pass either
  confirming or refuting this construction (also flagged, unresolved, in the
  gen-5 dossier for the couple's son Jeremiah). Not corrected here for lack of
  evidence either way.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Woolwich's demographic context, 1851:** Per Wikipedia's Woolwich article,
  the town's Victorian-era population included large numbers of unskilled
  Arsenal/factory laborers and soldiers (soldiers alone made up 10–15% of the
  population) — a plausible explanation for why an Irish-born couple would be
  resident there in 1851, though no record was found specifying whether Daniel
  was himself a soldier, an Arsenal/Dockyard laborer, or neither. [documented,
  general context only; not specific to this family]
- **Ireland→Britain→America secondary migration is a documented, known
  pattern:** general Irish-diaspora historical sources (see Sources) describe
  large-scale 19th-century Irish migration to British port/garrison towns,
  with a further, smaller stream of that population re-emigrating on to the
  United States. The Reagan family's own trajectory — Ireland, to Woolwich,
  Kent by 1851, to Boston by 1880 (and, per the gen-5 dossier, son Jeremiah
  married in Lawrence, MA already by 1857) — fits this documented pattern.
  [inferred/contextual; no record directly narrates the family's own
  Atlantic crossing or the reason for the Woolwich stop]
- **Surname-variant evidence is internal to the tree itself:** the tree's own
  list of the couple's six children already renders the surname three ways —
  Ragan (Catherine, Michael, John), Regan (Jeremiah), Reagan (James, Mary) —
  independently corroborating, from the tree's own data, the Regan/Ragan/
  Reagan variant pattern flagged for this family across generations (see the
  gen-5 and gen-4 dossiers for further instances, e.g. "Ellen M. Larkin" line
  and "Jeremiah Ragan"/"Mary Donavan" in an Ancestry-derived snippet).

## Open questions

1. **Daniel's occupation/status at Woolwich in 1851** — Royal Artillery
   soldier, Arsenal/Dockyard laborer, or something else? This would settle why
   an Irish family was resident in Kent, England rather than Ireland or
   America at that date, and is answerable only by reviewing the actual 1851
   England census household page (already cited by the tree, not
   independently re-viewed here).
2. **The couple's transatlantic crossing** — what ship, port, and year did
   Daniel and Julia (and which children) travel from England to
   Massachusetts? Given that son Jeremiah was already marrying in Lawrence, MA
   by 1857, the parents' own crossing likely predates or is contemporaneous
   with 1857 — well before the 1880 census that is the tree's only U.S. record
   for them. UK outbound passenger lists and U.S. arrival indexes (Boston
   passenger lists 1848-1891) are the logical next collections to check.
3. **1860 and 1870 U.S. censuses** — if the family arrived by the late 1850s,
   they should appear in one or both of these; would help pin down an arrival
   window and a Massachusetts town of first settlement.
4. **Marriage record for Daniel and Julia** — not given in the tree; if it
   took place in Ireland, England, or Massachusetts, it would give both
   parents' names and (in an Irish Catholic register) a specific home parish.
5. **Death dates/places and burial** for both Daniel and Julia — entirely
   absent from the tree; Massachusetts death indexes/Find a Grave (once
   accessible with login) are the obvious next step, especially if the family
   is buried near son Jeremiah's presumed Peterborough, NH, or in a Boston
   Catholic cemetery.
6. **Fates of children Catherine, Michael, John, and Mary Ragan/Reagan** — no
   information beyond their names was found in the tree or on the open web;
   1880 Boston census (if the household can be located) would show ages and
   likely occupations for any still living at home.
7. **Resolve the 1800 vs. 1811 birth-year discrepancy for Daniel** by
   reviewing both underlying census images directly, to determine whether one
   is a transcription/indexing error or whether it is simply two independent,
   imprecise age statements 29 years apart.
8. **"Julia Gillian" as a double given name** — worth checking against the
   underlying Ancestry Family Tree/source once accessible, per the same open
   question already flagged in the gen-5 dossier for this couple's son
   Jeremiah.
