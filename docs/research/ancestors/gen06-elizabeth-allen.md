## Overview

Elizabeth Allen was born about 1804 in Crewkerne, a Somerset market town then known
chiefly for agriculture and an emerging girth-web/sailcloth weaving trade, the daughter
of Wm (William) Allen and Sarah Crook. She had at least two children, William Blackmore
and Lucy Allen, indicating a marriage (almost certainly to a Mr. Blackmore) that is not
otherwise detailed in the tree extract. By 1871 she was living in a London parish
recorded only as "St Mark," the same ambiguous parish designation given for her son
William Blackmore's household in the 1871 census, suggesting she was enumerated as a
widowed mother living with or near her adult son's family. Given the extreme
commonness of the names "Elizabeth Allen," "William Allen," and "Sarah Crook" in
Georgian Somerset, and the total absence of independent web-accessible indexes for
this specific combination, this dossier is necessarily thin: it could not
independently corroborate any event beyond what the tree already asserts, and most of
its value lies in cross-referencing the tree's own internal data and in flagging the
work-order mismatch.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Likely household link to her son's 1871 census entry.** The existing dossier for
  Elizabeth's son, `gen05-william-blackmore-eliza-wood.md`, independently notes that
  William Blackmore (and wife Eliza Wood) appear in the 1871 England Census "in a
  parish given only as 'St Mark, London.'" Elizabeth Allen's own 1871 residence event
  in this record uses the identical, unusually vague place-string "St Mark, London,
  England." Two unrelated household members being tagged with exactly the same
  non-standard place string is a strong internal signal (not an independently pulled
  primary source, so flagged as *inferred*, not documented) that Elizabeth — by 1871
  a widow in her late 60s — was enumerated as a co-resident relative (mother or
  mother-in-law) in her son William Blackmore's own household, a very common living
  arrangement for elderly widows in Victorian London. This is worth chasing via the
  underlying RG10 census image, which would show her listed relationship to the head
  of household and resolve which of London's several "St Mark" parishes (Kennington,
  Marylebone/Hamilton Terrace, Whitechapel, etc.) this was.
- **Crewkerne historical context**: per the Victoria County History of Somerset
  (British History Online), Crewkerne in the early 19th century (Elizabeth's birth
  decade) had a population of about 2,576 (1801), was a market town on the London–
  Exeter coaching road, and was in the early stages of a shift from agriculture toward
  girth-web, sailcloth, and hair-seating weaving (an early factory operating at Viney
  Bridge from 1789). This textile trade would come to employ hundreds of Crewkerne
  residents by mid-century and is a plausible (though unconfirmed for this specific
  family) occupational context for an Allen or Crook household of modest means.
- **No independent corroboration was found** via open web search for Elizabeth Allen,
  her parents Wm Allen and Sarah Crook, or her daughter Lucy Allen: no FamilySearch
  "ancestors" tree page, no WikiTree or Geni profile, no findagrave memorial, and no
  freely searchable Somerset parish-register transcript (GENUKI, FreeReg, Somerset OPC
  Project index pages were checked at the index level only — their underlying
  transcripts require direct, form-based searches this pass's tools could not submit)
  turned up a matching baptism, marriage, or burial record. This is unsurprising: all
  three given names are among the most common in Georgian-era Somerset parish
  registers, making a name-only search (with no exact baptism date or parish register
  page) effectively unresolvable without paid Ancestry/FindMyPast image access or a
  manual page-by-page search of the Crewkerne St Bartholomew registers.

## Open questions

1. Who did Elizabeth Allen marry to produce a son surnamed Blackmore? No spouse is
   recorded in the tree extract supplied for this pass; identifying him would likely
   also resolve William Blackmore's Middlesex birth (c.1839) versus Elizabeth's
   Somerset origin (b. 1804, Crewkerne) — a 35-year age gap between mother and first
   recorded child that is plausible but leaves room for other children born earlier
   between Elizabeth's likely marriage (probably 1820s–early 1830s) and 1839.
2. Why does daughter "Lucy Allen" carry the maiden surname Allen rather than
   Blackmore? Possible explanations: she was born before Elizabeth's marriage
   (illegitimate, registered under the mother's surname), the tree is using Lucy's
   birth/maiden surname as a convenience label, or Lucy was from a different union
   entirely. Not resolvable from the data supplied.
3. Is the "St Mark, London" 1871 residence genuinely a shared household with son
   William Blackmore's family (per the New Findings cross-reference above), and if so,
   what does the underlying 1871 RG10 census image show for Elizabeth's stated
   relationship to head of household, marital status, and occupation?
4. What does the second, dateless/placeless "residence" event on Elizabeth's profile
   actually represent, and can it be traced to a specific record?
5. When and where did Elizabeth Allen die, and is there a burial or probate record?
   No death year or event is given in the tree extract.
6. Can a Crewkerne St Bartholomew baptism register entry for an Elizabeth, daughter of
   William Allen and Sarah Crook, c.1804, be located to confirm the exact baptism date
   and confirm parentage (this would require a manual parish-register search beyond
   what open web search/fetch tools could perform in this pass)?
