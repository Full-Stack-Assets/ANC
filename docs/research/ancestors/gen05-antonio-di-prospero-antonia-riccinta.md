## Overview

Antonio Di Prospero and Antonia Riccinta were an Abruzzese couple, both apparently born in or near Chieti province, Italy, in the 1830s–1840s, whose son Angelo Prospus (Prospero) emigrated to Boston and later settled in Brockton, Massachusetts. The tree's own data shows a striking ~18-year gap between the spouses' documented arrivals in America: Antonia is recorded in the U.S. by 1891 and enumerated in the 1900 census in Boston's Ward 6 (the North End, then the city's main Italian immigrant quarter), while Antonio does not appear to have arrived until 2 April 1909, at roughly age 69 — consistent with an elderly parent joining an already-established adult child's household late in life rather than the couple emigrating together. Independent web research (Ancestry, FamilySearch, Find A Grave, and free Italian-genealogy indexes) turned up no records that could be confidently tied to this specific couple; the rare surname "Riccinta" and the generic patronymic "Di Prospero" simply do not surface in any freely searchable index, so nearly everything documented about them still traces back to the tree's own (subscription-gated) source citations.

## Corrections to the tree

1. **No marriage event is recorded for this couple ("spouse: ... m. None @ None") despite a "Massachusetts Marriage Records, 1840-1915" source being attached to both individuals.** Given Antonio's documented residence in Chieti until 1909 and Antonia's presence in Massachusetts from 1891, it is very unlikely this couple married in Massachusetts at all — they almost certainly married in Italy well before either emigrated (consistent with son Angelo's birth c. 1859–1862). The Massachusetts marriage-record citation attached here is most plausibly a **source mis-attachment**, likely belonging instead to their son Angelo's 1889 or 1902 Massachusetts marriages (both independently documented in the gen04 dossier). Recommend detaching this source from Antonio/Antonia's records absent an actual marriage event citation.
2. **"Montarroli," given as a residence for Antonia with no date, does not match any locality found in comune/frazione gazetteers for Abruzzo or elsewhere.** It is likely a garbled OCR/transcription of a genuine Italian place name — Chieti-province candidates worth testing against the original record image include Monteodorisio (a documented comune in Chieti; a Pedigree Resource File hit for a distinct "Riccinta" family happens to be filed under a "monteodorisio" GEDCOM, a weak but suggestive coincidence — see Open questions), Montazzoli, or Montorio. This should be treated as an open transcription question, not a confirmed correction, until the underlying record is re-examined.
3. **The apparent 18-year gap between spouses' arrivals (Antonia ~1891, Antonio 1909) combined with Antonia being ~7-8 years older than Antonio** (b. Dec 1832 vs. "before 1840") is unusual but not inherently contradictory; flagging it here only because the combination (older wife, husband arriving decades later at ~69) is worth double-checking against the source images in case an age or arrival-year transcription error is involved.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Historical context for Ward 6, Boston (1900):** Ward 6 encompassed the North End, which by 1900 was rapidly becoming Boston's dominant Italian immigrant neighborhood — chain migration from southern/central Italy, waterfront day-labor employment, and parishes such as Sacred Heart (North Square) and St. Leonard's (Hanover St., opened 1899) anchored the community. Antonia's 1900 residence there is entirely consistent with this pattern and with her son Angelo's own documented Boston-North-End years before the family's move to Brockton. (Source: Paul Revere House, "Italian Immigration to America and Boston's North End.")
- **No Find A Grave memorial could be located for either Antonio Di Prospero or Antonia Riccinta** under any spelling variant (Di Prospero, Prospero, Prospus, Prosper; Riccinta). This is consistent with the companion Angelo/Vincenza dossier's finding that only Angelo himself (not his parents) has a locatable memorial — it is likely Antonio and/or Antonia died in Italy, or are buried unindexed/unphotographed in Massachusetts (possibly in the same Brockton Calvary Cemetery plot area as their son, if Antonio in fact emigrated permanently in 1909 and died in the U.S.).
- **No passenger manifest, naturalization record, or census entry for Antonio could be located in free indexes** for the 2 Apr 1909 New York arrival cited in the tree; this record almost certainly exists in Ancestry's "Italians to America" / Ellis Island passenger-list collections (subscription) but was not independently reproduced here.
- **The surname "Riccinta" is exceptionally rare** — it does not appear in standard Italian-surname reference lists (Ricci, Riccio, Rizzi/Rizzo are the closest common relatives) and produced only a single unrelated hit ("Nicola Riccinta," Pedigree Resource File, filed under a Monteodorisio-tagged GEDCOM) in broad search. This scarcity itself is a data point: it suggests either a very localized surname pocket in the Chieti area or a transcription variant of a more common name (e.g., a mangling of "Ricci" plus a suffix, or of a two-part surname).

## Open questions

- What is the actual place name behind "Montarroli"? Re-reading the original Massachusetts Town and Vital Records or sacramental-record image (rather than the tree's transcribed field) is the fastest path to resolving this.
- Is the "Massachusetts Marriage Records, 1840-1915" source attached to Antonio/Antonia actually a mis-attached duplicate of son Angelo's marriage record(s)? Worth checking the underlying Ancestry source-citation detail page.
- Did Antonio die in Italy, or did he emigrate permanently in 1909 and die in Massachusetts (Boston or Brockton) alongside his son's family? No death record, obituary, or grave was located for him.
- What became of Antonia after the 1900 census? No death record or grave was located; given her birth year of 1832 she would have been in her late 60s in 1900 and could plausibly have died in Boston any time in the following decade — worth checking the Massachusetts Death Index (1900s–1910s) for "Prospero"/"Di Prospero"/"Riccinta" entries.
- Was there a passenger manifest for Antonio's 2 Apr 1909 arrival that names his hometown/comune specifically, a contact/relative in the U.S. (very likely son Angelo, by then established in the Boston area), and his exact age? This would be the single most valuable unexplored record for this couple.
- Are there other children of this couple besides Angelo? The tree's `children` field lists only Angelo, but a couple married well before 1859 would very plausibly have had other children, some of whom may have stayed in Italy or emigrated separately under different name variants (Prospus/Prospor/Prosper family surname drift is already documented in the gen04 dossier).
- Italian civil-registration records for Chieti province (available 1809–1930 via the Antenati state-archive portal, free) have not yet been searched image-by-image for a Di Prospero/Riccinta marriage or for Antonio's and Antonia's birth records — this is the most promising unexploited free resource for this generation.
