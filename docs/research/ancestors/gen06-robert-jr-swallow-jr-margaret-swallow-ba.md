# Robert (Jr.) Swallow (1820–1904) & Margaret Banks (1816–1883)

Generation 6 direct ancestors. Born and married on Prince Edward Island, Canada; died in Boston, Suffolk County, Massachusetts.

## Overview

Robert Swallow Jr. was born 1 Oct 1820, likely at Grand River, Lot 56, Kings County, Prince Edward Island, son of the Yorkshire-born mariner/farmer Robert (William) Swallow and Catherine "Kate" Blackett of the pioneer Grand River Blackett family. On 17 Dec 1839, on PEI, he married Margaret Banks (b. 28 Apr 1816), daughter of George Banks and Mary McKenzie of the Annandale, Kings County Banks family. The couple's documented son William "Curley" "The Sailor" Swallow (a separate generation-5 dossier in this collection) remained on PEI as a Little Pond farmer, but Robert and Margaret themselves left the Island and resettled in Boston, Massachusetts — part of the well-documented wave of Prince Edward Islanders who emigrated to greater Boston in the 1860s–1890s. Margaret died in Boston on 27 Aug 1883 and Robert on (per the one source found) 9 May 1904, both close to age 84 and 63 respectively at death (Robert; Margaret was 67). No primary Massachusetts or PEI record (vital record, census, city directory, or grave) could be independently retrieved in this session; everything below rests on two mutually-corroborating compiled Island Register genealogies plus one weak secondary hint, so this dossier is a modest addition to, not a wholesale replacement of, the tree.

## Verified facts

- **Robert's birth, 1 Oct 1820** — confirmed by the Island Register's "Descendants of Robert Banks and Mary McKie" (Banks genealogy): "Margaret Banks ... She married Robert Swallow, married 17 Dec 1839, b. 1 Oct 1820, (son of Robert William Swallow and Catherine (Kate) Blackett)." This is a second, independently maintained Island Register file agreeing with the tree's date exactly. (A companion file, "Descendants of Robert Swallow and Kate Blackett," gives only "born Abt. 1820" for the same man, listed as child #2 of Robert Swallow Sr. and Kate Blackett — consistent but less precise.)
- **Margaret's birth, 28 Apr 1816** — confirmed by the same Banks genealogy entry, and independently by the pedigree line for her father: "2. George Banks, b. 1790, d. 1865 in Annandale PEI. He married Mary McKenzie... Children: ... viii Margaret Banks b. 28 Apr 1816."
- **Marriage 17 Dec 1839, Prince Edward Island** — confirmed identically in both Island Register genealogies ("married 17 Dec 1839" / "married 12-17-1839"), a rare case of exact agreement between the two independently compiled files.
- **Robert's parents, Robert (William) Swallow and Catherine "Kate" Blackett** — confirmed by the Banks genealogy ("son of Robert William Swallow and Catherine (Kate) Blackett") and consistent with the Swallow genealogy's own first-generation entry: "1. Robert Swallow #4620 born Abt. 1790, Yorkshire, England, UK, Occ./Title: Seaman, Farmer, married Catherine 'Kate' Blackett ... died 21 Apr 1865, Grand River, Lot #56, Prince Edward Is." Kate Blackett was daughter of John William Blackett and Diana Applecross and died 28 Dec 1867 at Grand River.
- **Margaret's parents, George Banks and Mary McKenzie** — confirmed by the Banks genealogy (see above); George Banks died 1865 in Annandale, PEI, and Mary McKenzie (of St. Peters Bay) died 1868. (The tree's "Margaret Mackenzie" for the mother's given name does not match either Island Register file — see Corrections.)
- **Margaret's death, 27 Aug 1883, Boston** — confirmed by the Banks genealogy: "Margaret Banks, b. 28 Apr 1816, d. 27 Aug 1883 in Boston."
- **Robert's death, in Boston, 1904** — confirmed as to place and year by the Banks genealogy ("d. 9 May 1904 in Boston"); a MyHeritage search snippet independently surfaced "one Robert Swallow [who] passed away in 1904 at age 84 in Massachusetts" (84 = 1904 minus 1820, consistent with this man's birth year), though the underlying MyHeritage record itself could not be opened/verified in this session (the page returned no readable content — likely JavaScript-rendered and blocked). The exact day (1 May per the tree vs. 9 May per the Bands genealogy) is unresolved — see Corrections.
- **Son William "Curley the Sailor" Swallow** — confirmed as their son in both Island Register genealogies and covered in depth in the companion generation-5 dossier for William Swallow & Abigail Jenkins (`docs/research/ancestors/gen05-william-curley-the-sailor-swallow-abigai.md`).

## Corrections to the tree

- **Margaret's mother: "Margaret Mackenzie" -> "Mary McKenzie" (or "MacKenzie")** — Both independent Island Register genealogies give George Banks's wife (Margaret Banks's mother) as **Mary** McKenzie/MacKenzie, not Margaret. The Banks genealogy: "He married Mary McKenzie, b. in St Peters Bay, d. 1868." The Swallow genealogy (in its separate, less-reliable account of this couple) likewise says "daughter of George Banks #4618 and Mary MacKenzie #4617." Two independently compiled files agreeing on "Mary" against the tree's "Margaret" is reasonably strong evidence of a tree transcription error (possibly a simple mother/daughter name echo, since the daughter is also a Margaret).
- **Robert's death day: "1 May 1904" (tree, unsourced) vs. "9 May 1904" (Banks genealogy)** — only one compiled source could be found for the exact day, and it disagrees with the tree by eight days; the year and place (1904, Boston) agree. Not resolved to primary-record confidence — flagged rather than treated as a firm correction. The Massachusetts death index / city registration would settle this (see Open questions).
- **Children list likely conflates/over-extends the couple's family** — the tree lists nine children (Catherine, Robert (III), Ann, Mary A, William "Curley the Sailor," Benjamin, Sarah J., Jeremiah, Richard Harvey Swallow). Neither Island Register genealogy supports this full list. The Banks genealogy gives five children: John "Black John" Swallow (b. 1836 — chronologically impossible as a child of an 1839 marriage; almost certainly misattributed and actually Robert Jr.'s younger brother, per the Swallow genealogy's placement of a John "Black John" Swallow #16371, b. 1836, as a child of Robert Sr. and Kate Blackett), Margaret Ann Swallow (b. abt 1849, m. William Tassell), Sarah Swallow (b. abt 1851), William Swallow (b. 1846/47, "Curley the Sailor"), and Ann Swallow. The Swallow genealogy's own (independent, and internally consistent chronologically) list for Robert Swallow #4612 gives four children: William "Curley" "The Sailor" (b. 10 Jun 1846), Ann (died without issue), an unnamed infant male (b. abt 1848, d. abt 1848), and Sarah (b. abt 1851, died without issue). Taking the union of the two, real children of this couple are almost certainly limited to: **William "Curley the Sailor," Ann, an infant son who died in infancy, Sarah, and possibly Margaret Ann** (m. William Tassell) — i.e., about 4–5 children, not 9. None of Catherine, Robert (III), Mary A, Benjamin, Jeremiah, or Richard Harvey Swallow appear as children of Robert Jr. & Margaret in either source. This mirrors a pattern already flagged in the companion son's dossier, where the tree's data for this same family line was shown to over-count/duplicate/misattribute children (e.g., a "Harvey Swallow" that was actually a son-in-law, Joseph "Harvey" Dingwell). Recommend treating the tree's nine-child list for this couple with real skepticism pending a PEI baptismal-register or 1841/1848/1855/1861 PEI census check.

## New findings

- **Historical context for the Boston move**: Prince Edward Island and other Maritime Canadians emigrated to Boston in large numbers from the 1860s–1890s, driven by the decline of PEI's traditional shipbuilding/lumber/fishing economy and Britain's withdrawal of colonial tariff protection. By the 1880 U.S. Census, fully half of all Prince Edward Island–born people living in the United States were in Massachusetts, and roughly 20% of all Maritime-born immigrants lived in Boston proper, commonly settling in Roxbury, Dorchester, East Boston, and West Roxbury, and — for Scots-descended, Presbyterian families like the Swallows and Banks — often in the "Scotch Corner" enclave of the South End. English fluency and shared Anglo-Protestant culture let these immigrants assimilate quickly, frequently moving from agricultural work in Canada into laboring jobs and, over a generation, into skilled trades or white-collar work in Boston (Global Boston / Boston College, "Canadians"). This fits Robert and Margaret's documented end-of-life location precisely, though no specific neighborhood, address, or occupation for this couple was recoverable in this session.
- **Sibling of Robert Jr. also emigrated toward Massachusetts**: Robert's granddaughter (his son William's daughter) Frances "Fanny" Swallow (Smith) died 2 Mar 1954 in Boston, Suffolk County, MA — showing the Boston branch of this family persisted for at least three generations after Robert and Margaret's own move (per the companion gen-5 dossier, sourced to the Swallow genealogy).
- **Robert Jr.'s known siblings** (per the Swallow genealogy, as children of Robert Sr. and Kate Blackett): William Swallow (b. abt 1822, farmer, m. Catherine "Kate" Blackett — a different, same-named Blackett cousin — d. 6 Jan 1887), Mary Anne Swallow (b. Sep 1829, m. Thomas Taylor), Marie Swallow (b. abt 1830, m. George Chaffey, d. 21 Apr 1865 at Grand River), and John "Black John" Swallow (b. 1836, farmer, m.(1) Mary Ann Howlett, m.(2) Anne Williams Margaret Tassell, d. 28 Aug 1908) — the last of whom the Banks genealogy misattributes as a son of Robert Jr. and Margaret instead (see Corrections).
- **Margaret's Banks siblings** (per the Banks genealogy, children of George Banks & Mary McKenzie): Mary Ann Banks (b. abt 1815, m. Robert Howlett), Jane Banks (m. William Hart), Ada Banks, William Banks (b. 1816, d. 1889, buried Bay Fortune United Cemetery), Sarah Banks, Elizabeth Banks (m. James Simons), John Banks (b. 1812, d. abt 1895, m. Mary McDonald), and Ann Banks (b. abt 1835, m.(1) Jeremiah Enman, m.(2) Capt. Joseph Gardner).
- **Wider Swallow–Banks–Blackett–Chaffey network stayed intertwined on PEI**: Margaret's niece Margaret "Maggie" Banks (daughter of Margaret's brother William Banks) married Robert Chaffey — himself a first cousin of Robert Jr. through his mother Marie Swallow — "7 Feb 1873 in Boston, Mass," per the Banks genealogy, showing a second branch of this extended family also relocating to Boston and even marrying there in the 1870s, a decade or more before Margaret's own death in the city.

## Open questions

- **No independent Massachusetts vital record, census, city directory, obituary, or Find a Grave memorial for Robert or Margaret Swallow could be located or opened in this session.** FamilySearch's Massachusetts death-index search is login-walled from here; Ancestry/MyHeritage snippets could not be opened (JavaScript-rendered pages returned blank); Boston Public Library's obituary index only covers 1932–1941 and 1953–2010, missing both 1883 and 1904; and Find a Grave searches for "Robert Swallow"/"Margaret Swallow" with these dates returned no matching memorials. The Massachusetts Registry of Vital Records and Statistics (mass.gov) or the Massachusetts State Archives' 1841–1910 vital-record index volumes (by five-year period) would be the next concrete step, along with a US Federal 1900 census search for a Robert Swallow, b. abt 1820, PEI-born, in Boston/Suffolk County (Margaret would be deceased by then; Robert may appear as a widower, possibly boarding with a grown child).
- **Exact death day for Robert**: 1 May 1904 (tree) vs. 9 May 1904 (Banks genealogy) — unresolved; a MA death record or Boston Globe death notice from early May 1904 would settle it.
- **Which children actually belong to this couple**: see Corrections above. A PEI baptismal register search (PARO Collections Database, gov.pe.ca/parosearch — a POST-only form not reachable from this session) for children of "Robert Swallow" and "Margaret/Sarah Banks" baptized in the Annandale/Grand River district in the 1840s–1850s would be the most direct way to establish the real, complete sibling set for William "Curley the Sailor" Swallow.
- **When/why did Robert and Margaret leave PEI for Boston, and did they emigrate together or does Margaret's 1883 death predate Robert's own move?** No passenger list, naturalization record, or first Boston-area appearance (e.g., an 1870/1880 census entry) was found. Given Margaret died in Boston in 1883, the couple must have relocated no later than the early 1880s, quite possibly considerably earlier alongside the first wave of PEI emigration to Boston in the 1860s–1870s — a Massachusetts 1880 federal census search or Canadian border-crossing index would help date the move.
- **Burial location**: no cemetery record found for either spouse. Boston's large 19th-century Catholic and Protestant cemeteries (e.g., Mount Hope, Forest Hills, Cedar Grove) that served Roxbury/Dorchester/West Roxbury's Maritime-immigrant communities would be reasonable candidates to search directly on Find a Grave by cemetery rather than by name.
