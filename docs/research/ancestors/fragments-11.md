# Fragments Batch 11 — Triage Dossier (Surnames Upton–Östensson, Generations 7–12)

## Overview

This batch covers 39 direct-ancestor fragments spanning surnames Upton through Östensson, generations 7–12 (roughly 1590s–1786 births), scattered across colonial New England (Massachusetts), colonial New York/New Jersey, England (Northamptonshire, Berkshire, Kent), Wales, southwest Germany (Baden-Württemberg/Hesse), and western Sweden (Bohuslän/Orust). This is a triage pass, not a deep dive: every person got a quick plausibility check against the tree's own dates/places, and about ten of the most findable/notable were run through 1–3 targeted web searches. Several turned out to be well-documented colonial families with real primary/secondary sources online (the Bull–Wells family of Orange Co. NY, the Woodbury family of Beverly MA, the Whitney immigrant line of Concord MA, the Valentine family of Hempstead LI). Two clear tree-data problems emerged: a badly mangled Whitney record (wrong surname spelling, wrong parents, wrong spouse) and a chronologically impossible birthplace/date combination for "Ann Palgrave Youngs." The batch also contains at least six exact or near-exact duplicate person-records (same individual entered twice with cosmetic spelling differences).

## Researched Highlights

### Sarah Wells (1694–1796), m. William Bull — Hamptonburgh/Orange Co., NY
One of the best-documented people in this batch. Sarah (Sarah G. Wells) has a Find A Grave memorial (#19421740) at Hamptonburgh Cemetery, Campbell Hall, Orange Co., NY, matching the tree's dates and burial place exactly. WikiTree (Wells-121) and Geni carry the same profile, and there is a dedicated published book, *The History and Genealogy of the William Bull and Sarah Wells Family of Orange County, New York*. Independent sources agree she was orphaned young, raised by Christopher Denn (matching the tree's "Christopher Denne (adoptive father)" parent entry), and married stonemason William Bull in 1718; together they built the surviving "Bull Stone House," now on the National Register of Historic Places. Family tradition records 12 children, 98 grandchildren, 212 great-grandchildren, and 13 great-great-grandchildren by the time of her 1796 death — consistent with the long list of children in the tree (Bull, Booth, Faulkner, Eager, Weller surnames).

### Richard Whetny [= Richard Whitney] (1624–1719), Concord, MA
This is clearly the same person as the extensively documented immigrant **Richard Whitney**, son of John Whitney the immigrant (1592–1673) — one of colonial New England's most-published lineages. Multiple independent sources (Geni, WikiTree, ourwhitneyfamily.com, genealogieonline.nl) agree on:
- Birth: baptized 6 Jan 1623/24 at **Isleworth, Middlesex, England** (not "Hesworth, London" as the tree has it — "Hesworth" is almost certainly a corruption/OCR error for Isleworth).
- Death: 20 Sep 1719, Concord, Middlesex Co., MA — matches the tree exactly.
- Parents: **John Whitney (1592–1673) and Elinor (Elinor Bray) Whitney** — NOT "William John James Whitney" and "Elinor Ellen Arnold" as the tree records.
- Spouse: **Martha Coldam** (daughter of Thomas and Joanna Coldam), married 19 Jan 1650/51 at Watertown, MA — NOT "Mary Flowers Kettle Kendall Kedell" as the tree records.
- Children born at Watertown include Sarah, Moses, Joanna, Deborah, Rebecca, Richard, Elisha, and **Ebenezer** (b. 30 Jun 1672) — Ebenezer plausibly corresponds to the tree's child "Eleazer S Whitney."
He immigrated to New England aboard the *Elizabeth and Ann* from London in April 1635 with his father and brothers, was made a freeman at Watertown in 1651, and was a proprietor at Stow, MA by 1680.

### Deacon Peter Woodberry III (1666–1706) and Peter Woodbury Sr (1640–1704), Beverly, MA
Both generations are independently and consistently documented across Geni, WikiTree, FamilySearch, Find A Grave, and a local Beverly family history site (mccallroots.com). Peter Woodbury Sr. was born 19 Sep 1640 in Salem, MA, son of John Woodbury (one of the earliest English settlers of the Massachusetts Bay Colony, arriving in the 1620s); he married Sarah Dodge on 2 Jul 1667, became a freeman in 1668, selectman in 1679, and deacon of the Beverly church in 1689; he died 5 Jul **1704** in Beverly. (The tree's own record contains a second, unsourced "death 1709" line that is contradicted by every independent source and by the tree's own sourced 1704 line — 1704 is correct.) His son Peter Woodbury III (Deacon) was born 12 Dec 1666 and died 8 Jan 1706/07 in Beverly, matching the tree's sourced dates; he is also referenced in a U.S. Sons of the American Revolution application, consistent with the tree's citation. Family lore (repeated in multiple 19th-century compiled histories) holds that Peter Sr., as deacon, sheltered people accused of witchcraft on his property during the Salem trials — this is tradition/legend, not a primary-source-documented fact, and should be labeled as such if repeated.

### Anna Marie Margaretha Weber (1786–1854) and Johann "Doc" Hohmann — Mittelkalbach, Germany → Fulda, Noble Co., Ohio
A dedicated local-history genealogy site for the German-American settlement of Fulda, Noble County, Ohio (fuldaohio.org, compiled from church records and a researcher's notes) directly references "Johann and Marie (Weber) Hohman[n]" as a couple with children, citing Mittelkalbach, Germany church records — corroborating the tree's basic claim that this couple existed and emigrated from the Mittelkalbach area of Hesse to the Fulda, Ohio settlement. This is a secondary/compiled source, not a primary church-register image, but it independently confirms the couple's existence and general facts beyond just the tree's own (unsourced) entry.

### Richard Valentine / Mary Margaret Valentine — Hempstead, Long Island, NY
An independently documented Richard Valentine (variously "Vallingtyne") was one of the 66 original proprietors of Hempstead, Long Island in the 1644 land division, a Townsman in 1659, Overseer in 1676, and Constable in 1679; he is described in period sources as descended from a Richard Valentine of Eccles Parish, Lancashire (matching the tree's "Eccles" birthplace claim for I182541956481). His unrecorded 1682 will names a daughter "Mary, wife of Thomas Cheesman" — directly corroborating the tree's Mary Margaret Valentine → Thomas Cheesman/Cheeseman marriage. However, published sources (WikiTree Valentine-517) give this immigrant's birth as c.1595, not 1610 or 1615 as the tree's two separate Richard Valentine records claim, and the tree appears to have split one man into two "generations" — see Corrections.

### Anne Whistler, m. Richard Gray, 1673, East Ilsley, Berkshire
Independently corroborated: Richard Gray (b. 28 Nov 1655, Stansted Mountfichet, Essex) married Anne Whistler (b. c.1655, Berkshire) on 7 Nov 1673 at East Ilsley, Berkshire — matching the tree's cited "England, Select Marriages, 1538-1973" source and marriage date/place exactly.

### JOHN WILLIAMS * (1679–1729), Pentraeth, Anglesey, Wales → Westchester Co., NY
A real, documented "John Williams, b. 26 Jan 1679/80, Pentraeth (Ty Fry estate), Anglesey" does exist in published Welsh-American genealogies — but the best independent match settled in **Hanover County, Virginia** (built the "Studley" plantation, ancestor of the well-known Virginia Williams family), not Westchester, NY. It is possible the tree's Westchester-based "JOHN WILLIAMS *" is a different man who happens to share the same name, birth year, and Anglesey origin, or that the tree has conflated two distinct immigrants. Not resolved either way with the searches performed here — flagged as an open question rather than a correction, since no direct evidence contradicts a Westchester settlement, but the closest documented match points to Virginia.

### *Ann Palgrave Youngs^ (1621–1686)
The tree gives this person's birthplace as "New Haven, New Haven, Connecticut" — but New Haven Colony was not founded until 1638, seventeen years after her stated 1621 birth year, making this combination impossible as recorded. Independent sources describe an "Ann Palgrave" who married Richard Palgrave in Great Yarmouth, Norfolk in 1625/26 and, after his death, married Rev. John Young(s), emigrating to Salem/Southold, Long Island, with six children by 1637 — this Ann Palgrave was an adult in England in the 1620s and cannot be the same birth-1621-in-Connecticut person in the tree. It is more likely the tree's record actually represents a **daughter or granddaughter** of the immigrant Ann Palgrave/Rev. John Young(s) couple (a New England-born "Youngs" who then married Thomas Biggs), with the ancestress's maiden name incorrectly carried down onto the descendant's tree entry. Flagged as suspect/likely conflation rather than corrected outright, since the exact descendant was not pinned down.

## Corrections to the tree

- **Richard Whetny → Richard Whitney** (spelling/identity). Parents recorded in tree as "William John James Whitney" and "Elinor Ellen Arnold" → correct parents are **John Whitney (1592–1673) and Elinor (Bray) Whitney**, per multiple independent compiled genealogies of this well-known immigrant family.
- **Richard Whetny's spouse** recorded in tree as "Mary Flowers Kettle Kendall Kedell" → correct spouse is **Martha Coldam** (m. 19 Jan 1650/51, Watertown, MA), daughter of Thomas and Joanna Coldam.
- **Richard Whetny's birthplace** recorded as "Hesworth, London, England" → almost certainly should read **Isleworth, Middlesex, England** (baptized there 6 Jan 1623/24).
- **Peter Woodbury Sr's death year**: the tree's own record contains two conflicting unsourced entries (1704 and 1709). Every independent source (WikiTree, FamilySearch, Find A Grave, local family histories) agrees on **5 July 1704** — the 1709 line in the tree appears to be an entry error and should be dropped/corrected.
- **Richard Valentine — likely duplicate/split identity**: the tree carries two separate "Richard Valentine" ancestor records (I182625986581, b. 1615 Hempstead, d. 1684 Elizabeth NJ; and I182541956481, b. 1610 Eccles, England, d. 1684 Hempstead NY) that both feed into the same daughter, Mary Margaret Valentine, and both die in 1684. Independent sources describe a single immigrant Richard Valentine of Eccles Parish, Lancashire, born **c. 1595** (not 1610 or 1615), who settled at Hempstead, Long Island — suggesting these two tree records represent one real person split across two "generations," with the birth year off in both cases.
- **Ann Palgrave Youngs — impossible birthplace/date combination**: tree gives birth "Abt. 1621 @ New Haven, New Haven, Connecticut," but New Haven Colony was not founded until 1638. This date/place pairing cannot be correct as recorded; most likely the tree has conflated the English-born immigrant ancestress Ann Palgrave (adult in England through the 1620s, later of Salem/Southold, Long Island) with a New England-born daughter or granddaughter who actually married Thomas Biggs.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- Sarah Wells's foster father, Christopher Denn (matching the tree's "adoptive father" parent entry), and her 1718 marriage produced the still-standing, National Register-listed "Bull Stone House" in Campbell Hall, NY, built over 13 years by Sarah and William Bull themselves.
- Richard Whitney immigrated to New England aboard the ship *Elizabeth and Ann* from London in April 1635, at about age 11 (recorded then as age 9), traveling with his father John Whitney and brothers.
- Richard Whitney was a proprietor at Stow, Massachusetts by 1680, a fact absent from the tree, which only records his Concord, MA death.
- Peter Woodbury Sr. held civic/church offices not in the tree: freeman (1668), selectman (1679), deacon (1689), and deputy to the Massachusetts General Court (1689). Family tradition (not primary-sourced) also credits him with sheltering people accused during the Salem witch trials.
- Richard Valentine of Hempstead held the town offices of Townsman (1659), Overseer (1676), and Constable (1679) — civic detail absent from the tree.
- A compiled Fulda, Ohio genealogy references a researcher ("Maunela/Manuela Kaib") who has worked directly from Mittelkalbach, Germany church records for the Weber/Hohmann family, which could be a lead for deeper primary-source verification.

## Open questions

- Is "JOHN WILLIAMS *" (b. 1679/80, Pentraeth, Anglesey, d. 1729, Westchester Co., NY) the same man as the independently documented John Williams of the same name/birthplace/birth-year who settled in Hanover Co., Virginia (the "Studley" line)? If so, the tree's Westchester, NY death location and descendant line (Stephen Williams Sr., etc.) would need re-examination; if not, this is simply a name/place coincidence.
- Which specific descendant of the immigrant Ann Palgrave/Rev. John Young(s) couple is actually represented by the tree's "*Ann Palgrave Youngs^" (m. Thomas Biggs) record? Not identified in this pass.
- No independent confirmation was found for greta nilsdotter, Maria Ählström, or Torbjörn Östensson (Swedish branch) beyond the tree's own claims — Swedish parish/clerical records (via ArkivDigital or Riksarkivet) would be needed for real verification, which is out of scope for this English-language triage pass.
- Anthony Whitlock's own tree record contains two conflicting birth entries (1645 and 22 Sep 1657) — which, if either, is correct was not resolved.
- Catharina "Catharine" Muller/Moller/Miller (b. 1726, New York) and her connection to a "Philipp Helfrich Müller" could not be matched to any specific documented individual; the only "Philipp Helfrich Müller" found in searches is an unrelated 18th–19th-century German engineer/inventor.
