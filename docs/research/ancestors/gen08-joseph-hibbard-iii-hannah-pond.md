# Joseph Hibbard III (1787–1856) & Hannah Pond (1805–1865) — Generation 7/8

> **Note on generation numbering:** `direct-line-issues.md` lists Joseph Hibbard III and Hannah Pond as "gen 7" (counting from the home person) while their parents (Joseph Hibbard Sr., Samuel Pond, Hannah Stowell) are "gen 8." This dossier is filed at the `gen08` path per the work order but covers both couples, since the duplication runs through the whole branch.

## Overview

The tree contains **two complete copies of the same couple**: Joseph Hibbard III (b. 19 Nov 1787, Penobscot, Penobscot Co., Maine; d. 13 Aug 1856, Hayti/Haiti, West Indies) and his wife Hannah Pond (b. 23 Mar 1805; d. 21 Sep 1865). One copy (`I182541965880` + `I182541965883`) is sourced and sits inside a fully fleshed-out ancestral chain going back to a documented 1751 Massachusetts family; the other (`I182541959308` + `I182541959296`) is an unsourced, thinly-linked stub. Both copies are attached as parents to the **same child record**, "Hannah Pond Hibbard" (`I182541959707`), which is the proof the two "couples" are one couple double-imported into the tree. The duplication is not limited to this one generation — it propagates up through both sets of grandparents (Joseph Hibbard Sr./Mary Lyons vs. a bare stub; Samuel Pond & Hannah Stowell vs. their unsourced mirrors) and into the great-grandparents already flagged elsewhere in `direct-line-issues.md`.

Separately, and more importantly for the tree's overall reliability: existing research on this couple's granddaughter-in-law-once-removed, Ellen F. Donahue (`I182541940428`, gen 5; see `docs/research/ancestors/gen05-patrick-donahue-ellen-f-donahue.md`), already flags the parent link from Ellen up to "Hannah Pond Hibbard" as a **probable wrong-person attachment** — an Ancestry hint mis-merge pulling in an unrelated colonial Yankee Protestant lineage. That question is independent of, and prior to, the duplication question resolved here.

Joseph Hibbard III's death "in Hayti, W.I." is unusual enough to be worth investigating on its own merits: Penobscot/Castine, Maine was one of Maine's leading early-19th-century maritime trade ports, with a documented West Indies trade route (molasses, salt, lumber) that regularly took Maine sea captains and merchants to Haiti (Saint-Domingue) — a plausible, though not confirmed, explanation for a Maine-born man dying there in 1856.

## Verified facts

- **Joseph Hibbard III, b. 19 Nov 1787, Penobscot, Maine; d. 13 Aug 1856, Hayti, W.I.** — per the tree's canonical record `I182541965880`, sourced to *North America, Family Histories, 1500-2000* (S447151841, Ancestry.com). [documented]
- **His parentage is externally corroborated.** A.G. Hibbard, *Genealogy of the Hibbard Family Who Are Descendants of Robert Hibbard of Salem, Massachusetts* (Hartford: Case, 1901), entry 163, records: "Joseph⁶ (s. Joseph⁴, Jeremiah³, Joseph², Robert¹), b. Manchester, Mass., April 23, 1751; m. (1) 1775, Mary Lyons, (2) July 9, 1780, Mrs. Anna Dugan of Marblehead (b. 1760; d. Dec. 19, 1804)." His children are listed as entries 425–432, including **"427. Joseph, b. Nov. 19, 1787"** — an exact match (name, birth date) to this record's father, `I182541965892` (Joseph Hibbard, b. 23 Apr 1751 Manchester, sourced to S447151841 and S508118847, with a second marriage to "Anna Dugard" on 9 Jul 1780 at Marblehead sourced to S446915296 — the "Dugard/Dugan" spelling variance is an ordinary period transcription difference). The genealogy's sibling list also shows the family living around Castine/Penobscot by the 1780s–90s (brother Jeremiah, entry 425, died 1805 "of small-pox on Holbrook's island, Castine, Maine"), consistent with this Joseph's Penobscot birthplace. [documented — 1901 published genealogy, independently found]
- **The 1901 genealogy gives no further information on this Joseph** (no marriage, no death) beyond his birth entry — the family historian evidently lost track of him, consistent with him having left Maine and died abroad. [inferred]
- **He married Hannah Pond**, per the tree (spouse link on `I182541965880`/`I182541965883`); her birth (23 Mar 1805) and death (21 Sep 1865) carry no source citation in the tree, and no independent record was found on the open web this session. [inferred]
- **Duplicate-person: the same couple exists twice in the tree, and the duplication is provable.** The unsourced pairing `I182541959308` (Joseph Hibbard III) + `I182541959296` (Hannah Pond) has identical birth/death dates and places to the sourced pairing, and **both pairs list the same child, `I182541959707` ("Hannah Pond Hibbard," b. 20 Jun 1831 Boston; d. 15 Oct 1905 Wakefield, MA)** as their offspring. That child's record lists all four ids — `I182541959308`, `I182541959296`, `I182541965880`, `I182541965883` — as her parents, which is only possible if the two "couples" are one couple attached twice. [documented — internal tree cross-reference]
- **The duplication propagates upward through both sets of grandparents**, already independently flagged in `direct-line-issues.md` (gen 8): Joseph Hibbard Sr. (`I182541965892` sourced vs. `I182541959347`, a bare stub with no parents, no spouses, and no sources); Samuel Pond (`I182541966151` vs. `I182541959337`); Hannah Stowell (`I182541966157`, with two parents recorded, vs. `I182541959332`, with only one). It continues at least one generation further, into the already-flagged gen-9 duplicates Jonas Pond (`I182541966171`/`I182541959422`), Sarah White (`I182541966163`/`I182541959371`), and Isaac Stowell Jr. (`I182620334338`/`I182541959366`). This is not an isolated duplicate couple — it is an entire branch of the tree imported twice in parallel, once with sources attached and once without. [documented — internal tree structure]
- **Castine/Penobscot, Maine was a documented West Indies trading port in this era.** Per the Castine Historical Society, the years between the War of 1812 and the Civil War were Castine's years of greatest maritime prosperity, built on shipping and salt/fish trade; general histories of the New England–West Indies molasses trade (Wikipedia's "Colonial molasses trade"; Connecticut History; NPS's Faneuil history) confirm Saint-Domingue/Haiti as a standard destination for New England trading vessels throughout the 18th and into the 19th century, with local merchants often maintaining agents or family on the islands to handle cargo. This is offered as historical context for why a Penobscot-born man might plausibly have been in Haiti at his death, not as a documented fact about Joseph Hibbard specifically. [inferred / contextual]

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Independent 1901 published-genealogy confirmation of the sourced branch.** A.G. Hibbard's 1901 *Genealogy of the Hibbard Family* (archive.org, scanned from a Boston Public Library copy) corroborates, without having been consulted by whoever built the sourced tree branch: Joseph Hibbard Sr.'s birth (23 Apr 1751, Manchester, Mass.), his two marriages (Mary Lyons 1775; Anna Dugan/Dugard 1780, Marblehead), and his son Joseph's birth (19 Nov 1787). This is strong independent support for treating the `I182541965880`/`I182541965892` branch as canonical over the sourceless `I182541959308`/`I182541959347` branch.
- **The duplicate branch is a "stub" pattern, not just a second citation of the same facts.** `I182541959347` (Joseph Hibbard Sr.'s duplicate) has *zero* spouses and *zero* parents recorded — it exists only to hold a birth/death date and a pointer to its one child. This is the signature of a partial GEDCOM/hint import that never got fully fleshed out, rather than a competing genealogical claim.
- **A more consequential doubt already exists one generation down.** The existing dossier for Ellen F. Donahue (gen 5; `docs/research/ancestors/gen05-patrick-donahue-ellen-f-donahue.md`) already concluded, independently of this duplication research, that Ellen's link to "Hannah Pond Hibbard" (`I182541959707`, the shared child discussed above) is "almost certainly wrong" — a probable Ancestry hint mis-merge, since Pond and Hibbard are 17th–18th-century New England Congregationalist surnames incompatible with Ellen's 1858 Boston Irish-Catholic Donahue family, and the tree tellingly records no father at all for Ellen via that link. **If that correction is right, this whole Hibbard/Pond branch — both the duplication resolved here and the underlying genealogy — has no place in the true direct line at all**, regardless of which copy is "canonical." The duplication finding and the mis-merge finding are separate problems that happen to sit on the same branch.
- **No external record locates Joseph Hibbard III's death in Haiti, his marriage to Hannah Pond, or Hannah Pond's own vitals.** Searches of Find a Grave, WikiTree, FamilySearch public trees, and Maine genealogical-society transcriptions (Maine Genealogy Archives, genealogytrails.com, *The Maine Genealogist*) turned up no matching profile for this specific couple. The 1901 Hibbard genealogy — the one source that does independently confirm the family — stops at his birth and says nothing about his adult life.
- **Circumstantial historical plausibility for the Haiti death**: Castine (the nearest significant port to Penobscot) was, per the Castine Historical Society, at its commercial peak between the War of 1812 and the Civil War, built on maritime shipping; general Triangle Trade / molasses-trade histories confirm Haiti (Saint-Domingue) as a standard 18th–19th century New England trading destination. This is offered as context, not proof — no shipping record, crew list, consular death report, or newspaper notice naming this Joseph Hibbard was located.
- **A companion pass on this same branch** (recorded independently in `manual.notes` on `I182541965892`, `I182541959347`, `I182541959332`, and `I182541966157`, referencing a forthcoming `docs/research/ancestors/gen08-hannah-stowell-joseph-hibbard.md`) reached the same duplicate-person conclusion from the Hannah Stowell/gen-9 side, and additionally flagged a place-name defect on Joseph Hibbard Sr.'s birthplace: both `I182541965892` and its duplicate `I182541959347` record `["Manchester","Mass","Massachusetts","USA"]`, dropping the county (Essex) and duplicating "Mass." as a false token — the correct form, matching Joseph Sr.'s own father's record (`I182541965901`) and the 1901 published genealogy, is **Manchester, Essex Co., Massachusetts**. Noted here for completeness; not independently re-verified by this dossier.

## Open questions

1. **Is "Hannah Pond Hibbard" (`I182541959707`) really Ellen F. Donahue's mother at all?** This is the higher-priority open question and predates the duplicate-merge question: per the gen 5 Donahue dossier, the honest answer may be "no," in which case this entire Hibbard/Pond ancestral branch should be evaluated for removal from the direct line, not merely de-duplicated. Resolving Ellen's true parentage (her 1858 Boston birth record; her 1882 marriage record) would settle this.
2. **What actually happened to Joseph Hibbard III in Haiti?** No source found beyond the tree's own citation (S447151841). Worth checking, if this branch is retained: Maine probate records (a Mainer dying abroad intestate/testate would often generate a probate file naming heirs), Maine newspaper death notices of 1856–57 (Bangor/Castine papers), U.S. consular death reports for Haiti (NARA has some 19th-century consular correspondence, though coverage is spotty and Haiti's own vital records from this era are difficult to access), and crew-list/customs records for Penobscot-district vessels trading to Haiti in the 1850s.
3. **Was Joseph Hibbard III a sea captain, supercargo, or merchant?** No occupation is recorded anywhere in the tree or in the 1901 genealogy. Given the Penobscot birthplace and the death location, a maritime occupation is the most natural hypothesis but is entirely unconfirmed.
4. **Hannah Pond's own record** (birth 23 Mar 1805, death 21 Sep 1865) carries no source in either copy in the tree. Massachusetts vital records or Dedham/Milton, Norfolk Co. town records (where her parents Samuel Pond and Hannah Stowell lived and died, per the tree) might independently confirm her.
5. **Did Joseph and Hannah have other children besides Hannah Pond Hibbard (b. 1831)?** Only one child is recorded on either duplicate parent pair; a family with an 1831 Boston-born daughter and a father who died in Haiti in 1856 plausibly had other children not yet captured in the tree.
6. Should the merge recommendations here (see table above) actually be executed as a tree edit (deleting the duplicate records / re-pointing `I182541959707`'s parent list to just the two canonical ids), or should the duplicates be retained as-is with `manual.notes` annotations only, per the John Albertson/Ann Pine precedent? This dossier follows the latter, less destructive approach; a future cleanup pass could go further.
