## Overview

Olof Stolpe fd. Andreasson was born 21 July 1799 and died 9 February 1870, both in Torsby parish
(Torsby socken), Göteborg och Bohus län (Bohuslän, on the west coast of Sweden, ~20 km north of
Gothenburg; today part of Kungälv kommun, Västra Götaland county). His name records a classic
Swedish **indelt soldat** (allotted-soldier) naming pattern: "fd." (*förutvarande*, "formerly")
Andreasson identifies his birth patronymic — son of a man named Andreas — while "Stolpe" is a
military name he took on enlisting in Bohusläns regemente, Södra kompaniet, the company that drew
its soldiers from Torsby and neighboring parishes. His father, listed in the tree as
**Andreas Olofsson Färdig**, shows the identical pattern one generation earlier (patronymic
Olofsson, soldier name Färdig), and cross-referencing the tree's own data for a sibling dossier in
this same repository (Johanna Andersdotter, gen06, dossier `gen06-johanna-andersdotter.md`) points
strongly to Olof having married **Johanna Andersdotter (1793–1856)** of Mollösund/Torsby, with whom
he is the shared father of at least three children recorded under two different tree entries —
**Anders Holst**, **Johannes Rönn fd. (Olsson) Soldat**, and **Anna Lovisa Olsdotter** — continuing
the same soldier-naming tradition into a third generation. Direct 18th–19th century Swedish parish
registers (Torsby husförhörslängder, födelse-/dödböcker) that would independently confirm these
identifications were not reachable through open web search in this session; the findings below are
therefore mostly contextual and cross-referential rather than primary-source confirmations of Olof
himself.

## Verified facts

- **Birth: 21 July 1799, Torsby, Göteborg och Bohus län.** Per tree, flagged sourced. Torsby socken
  is a real, well-documented parish in Inlands Södre härad, Bohuslän — medieval origin, later
  Torsby landskommun, merged into Hermansby kommun (1952) and then Kungälvs kommun (1971); its
  indelta soldiers belonged to **Bohusläns regemente, Södra kompaniet**. [Wikipedia: Torsby socken;
  Wikipedia: Bohusläns regemente]
- **Death: 9 February 1870, Torsby.** Per tree, flagged sourced. Consistent with a lifelong Torsby
  resident; parish population there grew from 1,720 (1810) to a peak of 2,592 (1860), the period
  spanning Olof's life. [Wikipedia: Torsby socken]
- **Naming pattern is historically coherent, not an error.** "Stolpe fd. Andreasson" is the standard
  way Swedish genealogical sources render a soldier's military name alongside his birth patronymic
  under the *indelningsverket* (allotment system, in force 1682–1901): a rote (small group of farms)
  was obligated to furnish and support one soldier, who on enlistment typically adopted a short,
  distinctive one-word name (often military/heroic-sounding — Stolpe = "post/pillar," Färdig =
  "ready," Holst, Rönn = "rowan tree") in place of his patronymic, freeing the name up to be
  reused by his successor in the same rote. [soldatreg.se, "Indelningsverket" and "Soldatnamn i det
  yngre indelningsverket"; sok.riksarkivet.se/grill]
- **Parents, per tree: Andreas Olofsson Färdig and Petronella Andersdotter.** The father's name
  shows the identical soldier/patronymic pairing pattern one generation earlier (patronymic
  "Olofsson" = son of an Olof; soldier name "Färdig"), which is internally consistent with Olof
  himself later using the same convention. No independent record of this couple was located on the
  open web in this session (see Open questions).
- **Child, per tree: Anders Holst.** This is independently and richly corroborated on the American
  side: Anders (also recorded "Andres") Holst married **Sabina Olsdotter** on 14 December 1855 in
  Torsby parish, and their son **John Oscar Holst** (b. 1858/59, Göteborg) emigrated to Boston in
  1879, is documented in Massachusetts vital and naturalization records, and is the subject of an
  existing companion dossier in this repository, `gen04-john-oscar-holst-mina-droulet-stone.md`,
  and Sabina Olsdotter's own dossier, `gen05-sabina-oldsdotter.md`. Those dossiers independently
  established the 1855 Torsby marriage and the couple's known children (John Oscar, Axel) — they did
  not, however, have Anders Holst's own parents' names, which this tree record now supplies. [tree
  cross-reference; kristinhall.org via Wayback Machine, cited in the Holst dossiers]

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Likely wife: Johanna Andersdotter (1793–1856) of Mollösund/Torsby.** The tree's own data,
  examined across two sibling records in this same research batch, supports this identification:
  - Olof's record (`items/A39.json`, this dossier) lists his child as **Anders Holst**.
  - The separate tree record for Johanna Andersdotter (`items/A38.json`, dossier
    `gen06-johanna-andersdotter.md`) lists her children as **Anders Holst**, **Johannes Rönn fd.
    (Olsson) Soldat**, and **Anna Lovisa Olsdotter**.
  - The patronymic forms "Olsson"/"Olsdotter" on two of those three children mean "son/daughter of
    an Ola/Olof" — consistent with Olof Stolpe fd. Andreasson (whose given name was Olof) being
    their father, with Anders Holst's own soldier name masking what would otherwise also have been
    an "Olofsson."
  - Johanna died in Torsby in 1856 — the same parish where Olof lived his whole life and where his
    son Anders Holst married in 1855 — geographically and temporally consistent with Olof and
    Johanna being husband and wife and Torsby being their shared household.
  - This amounts to a well-supported **inference from internal tree cross-referencing**, not a
    primary marriage record; no Torsby marriage-register entry for "Olof [Andreasson/Stolpe] ×
    Johanna Andersdotter" was located on the open web (see Open questions).
- **A three-generation (at least) soldier-naming lineage in the same Torsby rote/company.** Putting
  the tree records together yields a pattern typical of the *indelningsverket*: an ancestor named
  Olof (unrecorded generation) → son **Andreas Olofsson**, soldier name **Färdig** → grandson
  **Olof Andreasson**, soldier name **Stolpe** (the subject of this dossier) → great-grandsons
  **Anders [Olofsson]**, soldier name **Holst**, and **Johannes [Olsson]**, soldier name **Rönn**.
  Each generation's soldier name is unrelated to the last, which is expected: soldier names
  belonged to the *rote* (croft), not the family, and were reassigned to whoever next filled that
  specific soldier position — so seeing three different soldier surnames across three generations
  in what looks like one family is itself a small confirmation that the naming logic in the tree is
  historically accurate, even though it means the surname "Holst" that eventually crossed the
  Atlantic with John Oscar Holst was itself only one generation old when he was born. [context:
  soldatreg.se; sok.riksarkivet.se/grill; Wikipedia Bohusläns regemente]
- **Regimental context:** Torsby's indelta (allotted) soldiers served in **Bohusläns regemente
  (I 17), Södra kompaniet**; the regiment had ~904 men across eight companies by 1791 and Södra
  kompaniet persisted as an administrative unit through at least 1833. Roll/rote records for this
  company (the "Grill" indelningsverket database at Riksarkivet, and the Centrala Soldatregistret
  at soldatreg.se) would show which specific rote carried the "Stolpe" name and its enlistment/
  discharge dates for Olof, but these are form-based/subscription-style databases that could not be
  queried directly through automated web fetch in this session. [Wikipedia: Bohusläns regemente;
  soldatreg.se]

## Open questions

- **No primary-source confirmation of Olof's own birth/death records was located.** Torsby parish
  registers (kyrkoarkiv) for the relevant years are catalogued and digitized at Riksarkivet
  (e.g., Torsby kyrkoarkiv husförhörslängder SE/GLA/13565/A I/6, 1833–1834, and A I/11, 1843–1845
  are known to exist), but image access requires a Riksarkivet/SVAR login or ArkivDigital
  subscription not available in this research session.
- **Marriage to Johanna Andersdotter is inferred, not documented.** The Torsby marriage record (if
  the identification is correct, likely mid-1810s to 1820s given son Anders Holst's own 1855
  marriage) would confirm this directly and should be sought first if this line is researched
  further.
- **Parents Andreas Olofsson Färdig and Petronella Andersdotter** could not be verified or dated
  independently; their own birth/death/marriage records, and the specific rote in which Andreas
  held the soldier name "Färdig," remain to be found (likely also Torsby or an adjoining Södra
  kompaniet parish, given the son's lifelong residence there).
- **Olof's military service record itself** — enlistment date, discharge/pension date, specific
  rote number for the "Stolpe" name, and whether he served in any of the era's conflicts (e.g., the
  1808–09 war with Russia/Norway campaigns, or the bloodless 1814 Norway campaign) — was not found;
  the Centrala Soldatregistret (soldatreg.se) and Riksarkivet's Grill indelningsverket database are
  the right next places to check, ideally via their form-based search interfaces or by emailing
  soldatreg.se directly (contact given on their site).
- **Full sibling set for Olof** (children of Andreas Olofsson Färdig & Petronella Andersdotter)
  is unknown.
- **Complete children list for Olof & (presumed) Johanna** — the tree splits the children across
  two separate person records (this one lists only Anders Holst; Johanna's lists three), so it is
  worth checking the underlying tree software/GEDCOM for whether additional children are attached
  to one parent but not the other, and reconciling the two into one family unit.
