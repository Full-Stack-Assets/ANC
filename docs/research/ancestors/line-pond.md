# The Pond Line — Dedham, Massachusetts (Generations 7–12)

## Overview

This is a surname-line survey of the Pond family as it appears in the tree, generations 7–12 (roughly 1672–1858), tracing from Hannah Pond (b. 1805, gen 7) back through Samuel Pond, Jonas Pond, Joshua Pond, and Caleb Pond to Daniel Pond (gen 12, d. 1697/8), a husbandman first recorded in Dedham, Norfolk County, Massachusetts, in 1652. The Ponds of Dedham are an exceptionally well-documented colonial family: Edward Doubleday Harris's *A Genealogical Record of Daniel Pond, and His Descendants* (Boston, 1873), available in full text on Archive.org, independently confirms every link from Hannah Pond (gen 7) down to Daniel Pond (gen 12) with cited town, church, and probate records. This is unusually strong sourcing for a line this deep — the documented chain does not "end" at generation 11 as the tree's own data might suggest, but extends a further generation to Daniel Pond himself, whose own parentage and birthplace the 1873 genealogist explicitly says could not be discovered ("no vigilance has discovered" them). The tree data for this line also contains straightforward duplicate-record artifacts: every one of the four distinct individuals in the supplied JSON (Hannah, Samuel, Jonas, Caleb Pond) appears twice under different internal IDs with identical data.

## Verified facts

- **Hannah Pond** (gen 7), b. 23 Mar 1805, d. 21 Sep 1865, daughter of Samuel Pond and (his second wife) Hannah Stowell, married **Joseph Hibbard**, a seaman/mariner of Boston, on **15 Dec 1824** ("married, Dec. 15, 1824, Joseph Hibbert, seaman, of Boston. She died Sept. 21, 1865, leaving issue"). Both her birth and death dates match the tree exactly. — Edward D. Harris, *A Genealogical Record of Daniel Pond, and His Descendants* (1873), p. 102, entry #837
- **This marriage is independently corroborated by a second, unrelated published genealogy**: Augustine G. Hibbard, *Genealogy of the Hibbard Family... Descendants of Robert Hibbard of Salem* (1901), p. 88, records "Joseph⁶ Hibbard... married (2) 15 Dec 1824, Hannah Pond, of Dedham, Mass." — the identical marriage date from a completely independent source line is strong cross-confirmation. (See companion dossier `docs/research/ancestors/gen06-hannah-pond-hibbard.md`, which researched the *daughter* of this couple, also named Hannah — "Hannah P. Hibbard," b. 1831 — and is a different person from this gen-7 Hannah Pond.)
- **Samuel Pond** (gen 8), b. Dedham 1 May 1764, youngest son of Jonas and Sarah (White) Pond. Overseer of Dedham's poor for many years. Married three times: (1) Rachel Lyon (3 children); (2) in 1803, **Hannah Stowell**, b. 26 Sep 1772, d. Dedham 21 Aug 1829 (mother of the Hannah Pond above); (3) 11 Dec 1831, widow Fanny Frost (one child). He **died at Needham, 2 Oct 1858.** — Harris (1873), pp. 100–102, entry #383
- **Jonas Pond** (gen 9), b. Dedham 11 Oct 1730, baptized there 10 Jan 1730/1, youngest son of Joshua and Dorothy Pond. Served on Castle Island under Capt. Ebenezer Battle, Col. William McIntosh, Dec. 1776. Lived briefly in Roxbury, then Dorchester, "and is said to have died in Dedham, though the date is not recorded." Married **Sarah White, of Dorchester** — matching the tree exactly. His widow and youngest son were appointed administrators of his estate 3 Jan 1804. — Harris (1873), pp. 56–57, entry #143
- **Joshua Pond** (gen 10 — present in the tree only as an unlinked parent name, with no person-record of his own in the supplied JSON), b. Dedham 12 Apr 1700, son of Caleb Pond, styled "husbandman," lived in Dedham/West Roxbury. Bought land near Cow Island in Newton (1709); received a quit-claim release to his brother Nathaniel (1722); his mother conveyed him Dedham real estate (1730). Died early 1775; his widow and son Jonas administered his estate. — Harris (1873), pp. 29–30, entry #46
- **Caleb Pond** (gen 11), b. Dedham 13 Dec 1672, son of Daniel Pond. Granted 4 acres at "spring-field." Married **Priscilla, daughter of Nathaniel Colburn** — matching the tree's "Priscilla Colburn" exactly. He died **23 Feb 1705/6** (i.e., "1706" new-style — matching the tree). His widow was appointed administratrix the following March and was still alive, a widow, in 1731. — Harris (1873), pp. 16–17, entry #11
- **Daniel Pond** (gen 12 — not present at all in the supplied tree JSON, where Caleb Pond's `parents` field is empty), the progenitor of this line: a husbandman who "appeared in the town of Dedham... about the year 1652," purchasing land from Nathaniel Fisher that year and from Ralph Wheelock in 1662/3. Married (1) Abigail, daughter of Edward Shepard of Cambridge (she died 5 Jul 1661); married (2) 18 Sep 1661, Ann Edwards (who survived him and died 6 Jun 1732, aged 92). Selectman of Dedham (1660); took the freeman's oath in 1690. Died **4 Feb 1697/8** at Dedham; his will (transcribed in full in Harris's book from the original at the Suffolk County Probate office) names his wife Ann and numerous children including "my loveing Sons William Calab and Jabez Pond." — Harris (1873), pp. 9–11
- **Genealogist's note on Daniel Pond's own origin**: "The names of his parents and that of his birth-place, no vigilance has discovered." The author further reports that "Dr. Savage" (Rev. James Savage, the noted 19th-century New England genealogist) speculated Daniel Pond was a son of the elder **Robert Pond of Dorchester** (d. 1637) and thus a descendant of a Pond family associated with Groton, England — but the author is explicit that "no positive evidence in regard to the parentage of Daniel Pond can be adduced" beyond a "probability." — Harris (1873), pp. 6–8 (Introduction)

## Corrections to the tree

- **Samuel Pond's death place: "Milton, Norfolk, Massachusetts" → Needham, Massachusetts.** The primary published source states plainly "He died at Needham, Oct. 2, 1858." The death date (2 Oct 1858) matches the tree exactly; only the town is wrong. (Milton and Needham are both real Norfolk County towns near Dedham, so this looks like a simple town mix-up rather than a different person.)
- **Joseph Hibbard's name: "Joseph Hibbard III" → Joseph Hibbard (no "III"), per both primary genealogies used here.** The Harris (Pond) genealogy calls him simply "Joseph Hibbert, seaman, of Boston." The Hibbard family's own 1901 genealogy identifies him as "Joseph⁶ Hibbard" — the sixth in a paternal line of men named Joseph (Joseph⁶, son of Joseph⁵, son of Joseph⁴ "Capt. Joseph"...), b. Penobscot, Maine, 19 Nov 1787, married (1) Prudence Greely (d. 1815), married (2) Hannah Pond (1824), owner of several vessels, died at Hayti (Haiti), W.I., 13 Aug 1856. The tree's "III" suffix is plausibly a garbled rendering of this generational numbering rather than an authentic regnal-style suffix, per the companion dossier `gen06-hannah-pond-hibbard.md`, which reached the same conclusion independently.
- **"Dorothy Whitney" (wife of Joshua Pond, gen 10) is not supported by the Harris genealogy.** The primary source states explicitly: "The marriage of Joshua Pond is not recorded. His wife, Dorothy, was admitted to the church at Dedham, Feb. 28, 1724-5" — her maiden name is never given. The surname "Whitney" appended in the tree cannot be confirmed from this source and should be treated as **unsupported / possible tree lore** (perhaps an Ancestry hint or a confusion with one of the several other Whitney marriages that do appear elsewhere in the same genealogy, none involving this Dorothy).
- **Duplicate person records.** Every individual supplied in this line's tree data exists twice under separate internal IDs with byte-for-byte identical fields — Hannah Pond (`I182541959296` / `I182541965883`), Samuel Pond (`I182541959337` / `I182541966151`), Jonas Pond (`I182541959422` / `I182541966171`), and Caleb Pond (`I182541959496` / `I182541966295`). These should be merged; they are not independent people or alternate hypotheses, just import/merge duplication.
- **Missing generation-10 person record.** Joshua Pond (b. 1700, d. 1775) is named as a parent of Jonas Pond and as a child of Caleb Pond in the supplied data, but has no standalone person-record of his own in this JSON extract — an apparent gap in this slice of the tree (he may exist as a record elsewhere in the full tree that simply wasn't included in this line extract).
- **The "documented line" does not end at Caleb Pond (gen 11) as the empty `parents: []` field might suggest.** A published, well-cited genealogy carries the documented line one full generation further, to Daniel Pond (gen 12), and the genealogy itself candidly flags that *his* ancestry is where the documentation genuinely runs out.

## New findings

- Samuel Pond (gen 8) was married three times, not once: first to Rachel Lyon (3 children, not in the tree), second to Hannah Stowell (the tree's spouse, 6 children including Hannah), third to widow Fanny Frost (1 child). None of the Rachel Lyon or Fanny Frost children appear in the tree.
- Hannah Pond and Samuel Pond had at least eight documented siblings/children beyond Hannah herself: Roxana, Eliza, Samuel, Lucinda, and others (per Harris pp. 101–103) — not captured in this tree extract.
- Jonas Pond and Sarah White had at least six other children besides Samuel: Ebenezer (b. 1756), Sarah (b. 1759), Moses (b. 1761), Lucy (b. 1762), Prudence, and another Hannah (bapt. 1792) — none in the tree.
- Caleb Pond (gen 11) and Priscilla Colburn had five children on record: Nathaniel, Caleb (a second son, also named Caleb), Joshua (our line), Daniel (d. 1732, apparently unmarried), and Abiel (died young) — only Joshua is relevant to this direct line.
- Daniel Pond's 1697/8 will survives in full and is transcribed in the genealogy; it names his second wife Ann, sons John, Ephraim, Robert, William, Caleb, and Jabez, daughters Abigail Day, Hannah Devotion, and Sarah, and grandchildren by his late daughter Rachel Stone and late son Daniel — a rich, independently verifiable primary document establishing the family's full contemporary structure circa 1698.
- Rev. James Savage's (unproven) hypothesis ties this Dedham Pond family to an early Massachusetts Bay colonist, Robert Pond of Dorchester (d. 1637), and beyond him to a Pond family of Groton, England — but the 1873 genealogist is careful to label this speculative, not documented.
- Dedham's town/county history explains an apparent geography puzzle in the tree: some events recorded as occurring in "Wrentham" or later "Franklin" actually took place on land that was part of Dedham at the time, since Wrentham (1661/1673) and Franklin (later) were both set off from Dedham's original, much larger territory. This likely explains why Caleb Pond's death is given as "Wrentham" in the tree even though the 1873 genealogy itself only says he spent his "brief period of maturity" in Dedham and was granted land at a place called "spring-field" (in the Wrentham area) — plausible but not explicitly stated as his death location in the primary source.

## Open questions

1. Is the tree's exact death date for Caleb Pond ("23 Feb 1706") sourced from the same probate record cited by Harris ("Feb. 23, 1705-6," i.e., Julian/Gregorian double-dating for the same day), or from an independent record? They appear to describe the same event under old/new-style dating conventions.
2. Where did the tree's specific death date for Jonas Pond ("28 Nov 1803") come from? Harris's genealogy says explicitly that "he is said to have died in Dedham, though the date is not recorded" — the tree's precision suggests a source (perhaps a probate inventory or a since-indexed vital record) not used by the 1873 genealogist, and worth tracking down (Dedham town vital records, or Massachusetts probate case files, would be the place to look).
3. What is the actual maiden name (if any beyond "Dorothy") of Joshua Pond's wife? Not resolved by this or any other source consulted.
4. Was Daniel Pond (gen 12) an actual immigrant from England, or was he born in New England to earlier, still-unidentified colonial parents? The 1873 source treats his 1652 appearance in Dedham records as the practical starting point of documentation and does not resolve this question; Rev. James Savage's link to Robert Pond of Dorchester (a colonist by the 1630s) remains an unproven hypothesis only.
5. Should this dossier's findings be reconciled with the existing `docs/research/ancestors/gen06-hannah-pond-hibbard.md` dossier, which researched the daughter (also "Hannah," b. 1831) of the Hannah Pond documented here? The two dossiers are consistent (both cite the identical 15 Dec 1824 marriage date) but were produced independently; a tree edit pass should make sure the mother (gen 7, this dossier) and daughter (gen 6, the other dossier) are not conflated under a single "Hannah Pond Hibbard" node.
