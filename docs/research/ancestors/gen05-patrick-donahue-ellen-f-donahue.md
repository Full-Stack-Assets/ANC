# Patrick Donahue (b. 1855, Ireland) & Ellen F. Donahue (1858–abt 1937) — Generation 5

## Overview

Patrick Donahue, born 1855 in Ireland to Henry Donahue and Mary Gilchrist, emigrated to the United States in the early 1870s and settled in Boston, where on 1 February 1882 he married Boston-born Ellen F. (maiden name not recorded in the tree; b. 29 Apr 1858). The couple raised their family in Boston (Wards 18/19 and later Ward 14 — the Dorchester/Roxbury side of the city), where Ellen lived until her death around 1937. Their names are among the most common in Irish-Catholic Boston, and open-web research (FamilySearch public pages, Find a Grave, WikiTree, Boston Public Library obituary index) produced no independently confirmable new records; the chief value of this deep-dive is a set of internal-consistency findings that flag two probable wrong-person attachments in the tree.

## Verified facts

Facts below are "documented" only in the sense that the tree cites a record collection for them; none could be independently re-verified on the open web during this session (Ancestry/FamilySearch record images are login-gated).

- **Patrick Donahue, b. 1855 in Ireland** — sourced in tree (census collections consistently give Ireland birth; year from census ages). Parents recorded as **Henry Donahue and Mary Gilchrist** (source basis unstated; possibly the 1882 Massachusetts marriage record, which for Boston marriages of this era names both parties' parents).
- **Immigration in the early 1870s** — two sourced arrival events, **1872 and 1875**. These are almost certainly the self-reported "year of immigration" answers from two different census enumerations (1900-era censuses asked this); a 2–3 year spread between censuses is normal and does not indicate two voyages.
- **Residence, Boston 1878** — sourced to *U.S., City Directories, 1822-1995* (Boston directory).
- **Residence, Boston, Suffolk Co. 1880** — sourced to *1880 United States Federal Census*.
- **Marriage: Patrick Donahue and Ellen F., 1 February 1882, Boston** — sourced to *Massachusetts, Marriage Records, 1840-1915* and/or *Massachusetts, Town and Vital Records, 1620-1988*; the Boston Archdiocese Roman Catholic Sacramental Records citation on both spouses suggests a Catholic church marriage record also exists.
- **Ellen F., b. 29 April 1858, Boston, Massachusetts** — sourced (MA vital records; possibly also a Catholic baptism in the Archdiocese sacramental records).
- **Household residences (both spouses, matching):** 1910 Boston Ward 19; 1920 Boston Ward 14 — sourced to the respective federal censuses. Ellen additionally: 1900 Boston Ward 18; 1921 Boston (List of Boston Residents); 1930 Boston (1930 census) — the 1930 entry without Patrick suggests Patrick died between 1920 and 1930.
- **Ellen's death, about 1937** — the "Abt" qualifier and the *List of Boston Residents, 1909-1972* citation suggest the date is bracketed from her disappearance from the annual Boston residents lists rather than from a death certificate.
- **Children (per tree):** Henry L. Donahue, Helen Donahue, John P. Donahue, Francis M. Donahue, Charles J. Donahue. All were born in the 1880s–1890s in Boston and are safely deceased; note the tree links only the first three to Ellen (see Open questions).

## Corrections to the tree

1. **"Hannah Pond Hibbard" as Ellen's (only) parent → almost certainly wrong; remove or re-verify.** Pond and Hibbard are colonial-era Yankee Protestant surnames (the Robert Hibbard of Salem line); every Hannah Pond / Hannah Hibbard findable on FamilySearch, Geni, and WikiTree is a 17th–18th-century New England Congregationalist (e.g., FamilySearch MF59-QXR, 1725–1761; LTP9-FWL, 1660–1698). A Boston Irish-Catholic woman born 1858, married in the Catholic church, whose family appears in the Boston Archdiocese sacramental records, would not plausibly have this mother — and the tree tellingly records no father at all. This has the classic signature of an Ancestry hint mis-merge from an unrelated tree. Ellen's real parents should be recoverable from her 1858 Boston birth record and her 1882 marriage record (both in the collections already cited in the tree).
2. **1900 census residence "Sault Sainte Marie Ward 2, Chippewa, Michigan" for Patrick → probable wrong-person attachment.** The family is documented in Boston in 1878, 1880, 1910, and 1920, and Ellen (with the household) is in Boston Ward 18 in the 1900 census. A lone sourced 1900 record placing Patrick 800 miles away in the Upper Peninsula, plus the **unsourced "arrival: 1900"** event (which reads like the immigration-year column of that same Michigan census record), plus the undated, placeless **second spouse "Mary Donahue"**, form a coherent cluster: a different Patrick Donahue (b. ~1855, Ireland, wife Mary, immigrated 1900?) in Sault Ste. Marie was likely hint-attached to this Boston man. Recommend detaching the Michigan 1900 census, the 1900 arrival event, and the "Mary Donahue" spouse unless the Michigan household can be shown to contain this family's children. (The correct 1900 record for Patrick is presumably the Boston Ward 18 household already attached to Ellen — check whether he appears in it.)
3. **Duplicate birth event for Patrick** ("birth: 1855 @ Ireland" and "birth: None @ Ireland") — merge into one event.
4. **Source-collection mismatch:** *Massachusetts, Death Records, 1841-1915* cannot document Ellen's ~1937 death (collection ends 1915). It is presumably attached for some other event (a child who died young, or Ellen's/Patrick's parents) — worth re-checking what that citation actually supports. Patrick's own death (apparently 1920s, after the 1920 census) is post-1915 and also cannot be in that collection.

## New findings

- **Boston Public Library obituary index (obits.bpl.org, covering Boston Evening Transcript / Globe / Herald 1932–1941): no obituary for Ellen F. Donahue.** I queried the database directly (POST to results.php) for all Donahue entries; the only Ellen is "Ellen T. Donahue, d. 2006, Plymouth" — not her. This is a genuine negative result: it means Ellen most likely received only a paid death notice (which BPL explicitly does not index), not an editorial obituary. The *Newspapers.com Obituary Index* citation in the tree therefore probably points to a short death notice; retrieving it (via a Newspapers.com subscription) is the fastest way to pin down her exact death date and surviving children.
- **No Find a Grave memorial locatable** for either spouse under Donahue/Donohue/Donoghue with matching dates in the Boston Catholic cemeteries (searches across findagrave.com for Ellen d. 1937 Massachusetts and Patrick b. 1855 returned only non-matching individuals). Given the family's profile, the likely burial grounds are Old Calvary (Roslindale), Mount Benedict (West Roxbury), New Calvary (Mattapan), or Holy Cross (Malden); many graves in these cemeteries are simply un-photographed/un-memorialized on Find a Grave, so absence here is weak evidence. Note: New Calvary's pre-1878 interment records burned in the 6 June 1878 records-office fire — irrelevant for this couple's own burials but relevant if seeking Ellen's parents.
- **No WikiTree, Geni, or public FamilySearch tree profile** could be matched to this couple (all candidate "Patrick Donahue" profiles found — e.g., WikiTree Donahue-174 of Lowell, FamilySearch KC7F-S8J of West Virginia, the Somerset MA police chief 1856–1927 — belong to demonstrably different men). This family appears to exist only in Ancestry member trees at present.
- **Chronological inference on Patrick's death:** present with the household in the 1920 census (Boston Ward 14), absent from Ellen's 1930 census entry and with no death year in the tree → Patrick very likely died in Boston between 1920 and 1930. Massachusetts deaths for that window are indexed in the state vital records (registered volumes at the MA Archives / FamilySearch "Massachusetts State Vital Records, 1841-1925" for the early part of the window).

## Open questions

1. **Ellen's maiden name and true parents.** The single most important gap. Her 1882 Boston marriage record (MA Marriage Records 1840-1915, v. 331+ for 1882 Boston) and her 29 Apr 1858 Boston birth record both should state parents' names. Resolving this also formally disposes of the "Hannah Pond Hibbard" error.
2. **Patrick's exact death date and burial.** Search MA death index 1921–1930 for Patrick Donahue, Boston, age ~65–75; then the Boston Archdiocese cemetery office (Catholic Cemetery Association) for the family plot, which would likely also hold Ellen (d. ~1937).
3. **Ellen's exact death date.** Newspapers.com obituary-index hit (cited in tree) or MA death index 1936–1938; the Boston residents lists (1909–1972 collection) can bracket her last year of residence.
4. **Was Patrick in the Boston Ward 18 household in 1900?** If yes, the Michigan attachment is definitively wrong; if he is genuinely absent, the Michigan lead would need real evaluation (though a second wife "Mary" still argues for a different man).
5. **Why are Francis M. and Charles J. linked only to Patrick, not Ellen?** Probably a tree-linkage gap rather than a second marriage; their birth records (Boston, 1880s–90s) would name the mother.
6. **Patrick's Irish origins.** Parents Henry Donahue & Mary Gilchrist — "Gilchrist" is a relatively distinctive north-of-Ireland/Ulster-leaning surname pairing; a civil or parish marriage of Henry Donahue and Mary Gilchrist in Ireland (RootsIreland / irishgenealogy.ie, pre-1855) could locate the townland. No match found in open indexes this session.
7. **Naturalization.** An arrival c. 1872–1875 with decades of Boston residence makes a Suffolk County naturalization likely (indexed in "Massachusetts, State and Federal Naturalization Records, 1798-1950") — not attached in the tree and not findable on the open web.
