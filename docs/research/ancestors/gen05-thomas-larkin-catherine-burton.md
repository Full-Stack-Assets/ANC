# Thomas Larkin & Catherine Burton (Generation 5)

Tree IDs I182789737425 (Thomas) / I182625291737 (Catherine). Irish Catholic immigrants to
Liverpool; parents of (among others) Thomas Larkin (gen 4, m. purportedly "Mary Blackmore" —
see `gen04-thomas-larkin-mary-blackmore.md`, whose real mother has since been traced to
Ellen McAlone via a different husband, William Larkin, so the exact line from this gen-5
couple down to Ellen M. Larkin's branch needs re-checking; see Open Questions).

## Overview

Thomas Larkin (b. ~1823/28, Ireland — precise birthplace unverified) and Catherine Burton
(b. 1831, Ireland) married in Liverpool in late 1851, part of the wave of Irish Catholic
settlement in the city's dockside north end following the Great Famine. They raised at
least nine children in the St Alban's parish district (Athol Street, Vauxhall) and appear,
as a household, in the 1861, 1871 and 1881 England censuses, all already cited in the tree.
This deep dive independently confirmed the marriage and Thomas's 1898 death through the free
Lancashire BMD civil-registration index (which the tree had not cited), found a strong
historical/contextual case for why the family settled in that specific parish, and
identified a coverage gap that explains why Catherine's 1901 death cannot currently be
verified through free indexes.

## Verified facts

- **Marriage: Thomas Larkin m. Catherine Burton, 1851, Liverpool Register Office or Registrar
  Attended, ref. LL:REG_LP/40/194.** Verified directly against the Lancashire BMD civil
  marriage index (region Liverpool, all Liverpool sub-districts, exact match on both
  surnames). This is an independent confirmation of the tree's "23 Nov 1851, Liverpool"
  marriage event, which in the tree itself was sourced only to the compiled "Ancestry Family
  Trees" hint. "Register Office or Registrar Attended" is the index's own wording for that
  entry — before the 1898 Marriage Act, a Catholic religious ceremony still required a civil
  registrar to attend for the marriage to be legally recognized, so this phrasing is
  consistent with (but does not prove) a chapel wedding with registrar present rather than a
  register-office-only ceremony. [Lancashire BMD, Marriages index, Liverpool region, 1851]
- **Death: Thomas Larkin, age 70, 1898, Islington sub-district, Liverpool, ref.
  LL:ISL/131/7.** Verified in the Lancashire BMD death index (Islington sub-district is
  indexed there for 1837–1904, well within range). Age 70 in 1898 implies a birth year of
  about 1827/28 — four to five years later than the tree's "1823," which is itself marked
  unsourced. This is very likely the record behind the tree's "14 Nov 1898, Lancashire"
  death event (Islington is an inner-Liverpool district, not London's Islington), though the
  index gives no exact death date, only the registration year. [Lancashire BMD, Deaths
  index, Liverpool region, 1898]
- **The 1861/1871/1881 census events already in the tree are internally consistent and
  correctly cited** (RG9/RG10/RG11 piece/folio/page references are present for all three),
  and describe the same St Alban's-district Irish Catholic family across three decades.
  Not re-verified against the original images in this pass, but no red flags found. [Tree's
  own 1861/1871/1881 England Census sources]
- **St Alban's Catholic Church, Athol Street, Vauxhall, was built specifically for
  Liverpool's post-Famine Irish community** — construction began October 1848 under Fr.
  Thomas Newsham, and the church opened 19 August 1849 (dedicated by Rt. Rev. George Brown,
  Vicar Apostolic of Liverpool; first parish priest Rev. Thomas Kelly). Its registers (1849–
  1931, 14 volumes) are held at Liverpool Record Office, ref. 282 ALB, and span this
  couple's entire known residence there. This strongly supports the plausibility of a
  religious wedding there in 1851, just two years after the church opened, at the height of
  Famine-era Irish immigration to the district. [Lancashire OnLine Parish Clerk (LAN-OPC),
  St Alban's Athol Street page]

## Corrections to the tree

1. **Birthplace "Ribbesmere, Galway, Ireland" (unsourced) — no such place found; flag as
   unverified/likely garbled, do not treat as a real townland pending a source.** A search
   of Irish townland gazetteers (townlands.ie, logainm.ie) and general web search turned up
   no place called "Ribbesmere" anywhere in Ireland, in Galway or otherwise. Given the tree's
   pattern elsewhere in this family (e.g., "Hamilton, England" turning out to be a Whitehaven
   street, not a place, on a different Larkin's profile — see the gen-4 dossier), this is
   consistent with either an OCR/transcription error in a compiled online tree or an
   auto-suggested place-name accepted without checking. It should not be repeated in
   further write-ups without a supporting record.
2. **Birth year "1823" is likely a few years too early.** Thomas's 1898 Lancashire BMD death
   index entry gives his age as 70, implying b. ~1827/28. Ages at death in this era were
   reported by an informant (often a surviving family member) and are commonly off by a few
   years, so "about 1823–1828" is the more defensible range rather than a single hard year;
   this is a refinement, not a firm correction, since neither figure has a birth registration
   behind it (Thomas was born before Irish civil registration of births began in 1864, and
   before English GRO registration would apply to an Ireland-born person).
3. **Death place "Lancashire, United Kingdom" can be narrowed to Liverpool (Islington
   sub-district)** per the Lancashire BMD index entry above — Islington was one of several
   sub-registration districts within the city of Liverpool, not a separate town.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **GRO/Lancashire BMD marriage index reference for the 1851 marriage: LL:REG_LP/40/194**
  (Liverpool region, "Register Office or Registrar Attended"), which can be used to order
  the actual GRO marriage certificate — this would show both parties' ages, exact addresses,
  occupations, fathers' names and occupations, and witnesses, which would be the single
  highest-value next record for this couple.
- **GRO/Lancashire BMD death index reference for Thomas's 1898 death: LL:ISL/131/7**
  (Islington sub-district, Liverpool, age 70) — orderable death certificate would give exact
  date, cause of death, informant, and address.
- **St Alban's Catholic Church (Athol Street, Vauxhall) background**: opened 19 Aug 1849
  specifically to serve Liverpool's post-Famine Irish Catholic population in the north end;
  first parish priest Rev. Thomas Kelly; registers 1849–1931 survive at Liverpool Record
  Office (ref. 282 ALB). This is the parish the tree's own 1861/1871 census events associate
  with the family, and the likely venue for any baptisms of their nine children as well as
  the 1851 marriage.
- **Coverage gap explains the unverified Catherine Burton death date.** Lancashire BMD's own
  coverage table shows that the "West Derby" sub-district index runs only 1837–1876, is
  again indexed as "West Derby Municipal"/"West Derby Rural" for 1880–1889 only, and then
  has **no coverage at all between 1890 and 1914** (next indexed as "West Derby
  Eastern"/"Western" from 1915). A systematic search of every other Liverpool sub-district
  (Islington, St Martin, St Thomas, Dale Street, Toxteth, etc.) for Larkin deaths 1899–1903
  found no Catherine, but if the family had moved to the West Derby area by 1901 (a common
  outward move for better-off dock-area families late in the century), her death simply
  would not appear in this free index at all — it is a genuine coverage hole, not
  necessarily evidence against the tree's 17 Oct 1901 date.

## Open questions

1. **What was Thomas's exact birthplace and year in Ireland?** No Irish parish baptism has
   been matched. "Ribbesmere" should be discarded as a search target; if a family tradition
   of Co. Galway origin is otherwise well founded (as claimed for a same-named Thomas Larkin
   in the A51/gen-4 research thread, tentatively linked to Laurence Larkin & Winifreda
   Mulken of the Meelick/Muckanagh area), that connection has **not** been independently
   confirmed in this pass — the baptism date cited there (14 Jul 1839) is hard to reconcile
   with a birth of ~1823–28, and may belong to a different Larkin of the same name in the
   same family. This should be reconciled directly against the A51 dossier once it exists.
2. **Order the two Lancashire BMD-indexed certificates** (marriage LL:REG_LP/40/194;
   death LL:ISL/131/7) from the GRO — these would very likely resolve Thomas's parents'
   names, his occupation, and his exact death date/cause.
3. **Catherine Burton's death**: check West Derby-area burial/cemetery registers directly
   (e.g., Anfield Cemetery, which served this part of Liverpool from 1863) or a paid GRO
   index search (which has full national coverage, unlike the free Lancashire BMD project)
   for "Larkin, Catherine" deaths 1899–1903 nationally, not just in the indexed Liverpool
   sub-districts.
4. **The 1891 census** (between the tree's 1881 and Catherine's 1901 death) has not been
   located; it would show whether Thomas was still alive/working, and which children were
   still at home.
5. **Catherine's parents** are listed in the tree as "Thomas Burton" and "unknown" — no
   research was done on this line in this pass; an Irish Burton family origin is plausible
   given Catherine's stated Irish birth, but unconfirmed.
6. St Alban's own registers (1849–1931, Liverpool RO ref. 282 ALB) have not been searched
   for this couple's marriage or their children's baptisms — this is probably the single
   best untapped primary source for this family and would need an on-site or commissioned
   lookup, or a Findmypast/Ancestry Catholic-parish-record subscription.
