# William Blackmore & Eliza Wood (Generation 5)

Tree IDs I182625291060 (William) / I182625291059 (Eliza). Parents, per the tree, of
Mary Blackmore, William Blackmore, Richard John Blackmore, and Elizabeth T. Blackmore.

**Note on assignment mismatch**: the work order for this item named the subjects as
"John Blackmore & Eliza Doney," but the source file `items/A30.json` actually contains

Corrections §1.

## Overview

William Blackmore was born about 1839 in Middlesex and Eliza Wood about 1835,
reportedly at "Willington," Somerset (almost certainly a mis-transcription of
Wellington, Somerset — see below). They married on 30 March 1861 in England and raised
at least four children in central/east London (Limehouse, Clerkenwell, and an
unidentified "St Mark" parish) before William's death on 6 March 1880 and burial at
Brompton Cemetery, West Brompton. Eliza outlived him by eleven years, dying in 1891.
This couple does not appear to be a notable or well-documented family: no independent
census image, GRO certificate, or find-a-grave memorial for either person could be
located through open web search, which is dominated by paywalled Ancestry/FindMyPast
holdings and by many unrelated William/Eliza Blackmores of the same era. The dossier
is therefore necessarily thin on independently-verified new material; most of the
value here is in flagging internal inconsistencies in the tree's own data for the
household to pursue with paid record access.

## Verified facts

*("Verified" here means the fact is attested by the tree's own attached record
citations, listed in its `sources` array; I was not able to independently re-pull the
underlying census/BMD images through open web search, which is consistent with the
adjacent dossier for this same tree noting Find a Grave and FreeBMD/FreeCEN as
blocked-to-automation or form-only. Treat these as tree-documented, not
independently re-verified.)*

- William Blackmore's baptism/birth is placed about 1839 in Middlesex, per "London,
  England, Church of England Births and Baptisms, 1813-1923" attached to his profile.
- William appears as a child in the 1841 England Census in St Anne Limehouse (a real,
  distinct Tower Hamlets parish — Hawksmoor's St Anne's, Limehouse, consecrated 1730),
  and as an adult in the 1861 England Census in St James, Clerkenwell, Middlesex, and
  the 1871 England Census in a parish given only as "St Mark, London."
- William married Eliza Wood on 30 March 1861 in England, per "London, England, Church
  of England Marriages and Banns, 1754-1938" and "England, Select Marriages,
  1538-1973."
- William died 6 March 1880 and was buried at West Brompton (i.e., Brompton Cemetery),
  Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea — a real, still-extant Crown cemetery whose
  1840–1997 burial registers are held at The National Archives, Kew (RG entries) and
  whose 1880 burials would fall inside its main working period. Cited to the "UK and
  Ireland, Find a Grave Index."
- Eliza Wood was born about 1835, per the tree's location "Willington Somerset,
  England" (see Corrections §2 on the likely place-name error).
- Eliza is co-enumerated with William in the 1861 (Clerkenwell) and 1871 ("St Mark,
  London") England censuses.
- Eliza died in 1891.
- The couple's four children as listed in the tree — Mary, William, Richard John, and
  Elizabeth T. Blackmore — are consistent with a marriage in 1861 and a household still
  forming through the 1871 census.

## Corrections to the tree

1. **Work-order subject mismatch**: the task brief named this couple "John Blackmore &
   Eliza Doney," but the actual tree extract (`items/A30.json`, dossier_path
   `docs/research/ancestors/gen05-william-blackmore-eliza-wood.md`) is for **William
   Blackmore (1839–1880) and Eliza Wood (1835–1891)**. No John Blackmore or Eliza
   Doney appears anywhere in the supplied data. Flagging so the discrepancy is not
   silently lost — if a couple named John Blackmore/Eliza Doney also exists in the
   tree, they were not the subject of this research pass.
2. **Eliza's birthplace "Willington Somerset" is almost certainly Wellington,
   Somerset.** There is no place called Willington in Somerset (Willington exists in
   Bedfordshire, Derbyshire, and County Durham); Wellington, Somerset is a real market
   town with well-documented 19th-century parish registers (GENUKI transcripts exist
   for Wellington baptisms/marriages/burials). This reads as an OCR/transcription
   slip (Wel- → Wil-) rather than a real place, and should be corrected to Wellington,
   Somerset pending confirmation from the underlying baptism record.
3. **Eliza's burial event ("20 Jan 1848 @ London, England") is internally
   inconsistent and should be removed or reassigned.** Eliza's tree-given birth year
   is 1835 and death year is 1891; a burial in January 1848 would place it when she
   was about 13 years old and 43 years before her recorded death — impossible for the
   same person. This is very likely a mismatched hit from the "Westminster, London,
   England, Church of England Deaths and Burials, 1812-1910" collection (which spans
   nearly a century and contains many Eliza Woods) attached to the wrong individual,
   analogous to the multi-way name conflations already documented elsewhere in this
   tree (see `larkin-blackmore.md` in this same directory, which found a "Mary
   Blackmore" profile to be a five-way merge of unrelated women). Recommend detaching
   this event pending re-verification.
4. **Eliza's listed parent, "Thomas Wright," does not match her own surname "Wood"
   and should be treated as unverified/likely mis-attached.** If Eliza's maiden
   surname truly was Wood, her father would ordinarily also be surnamed Wood unless
   (a) she was illegitimate and registered under her mother's surname, (b) "Wright" is
   a stepfather, or (c) this is another conflation artifact (a Wright-family record
   attached to the wrong Eliza Wood). None of these can be distinguished without the
   underlying baptism record naming both parents; flag as open question below rather
   than a confirmed correction.
5. **The Devon-parish source citations on both spouses' profiles ("Devon, England,
   Church of England Births and Baptisms, 1813-1920") sit oddly against an otherwise
   entirely Middlesex/London couple** and may be another instance of the source-list
   conflation pattern seen elsewhere in this tree (the Larkin–Blackmore dossier in
   this same folder documents five different women merged under one "Mary Blackmore"
   profile via exactly this kind of mismatched source attachment). Not a confirmed
   error, but worth the same scrutiny if primary images are ever pulled.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Parish identification for the 1841 residence**: "St Ann, St Anne Limehouse,
  Middlesex" is St Anne's Limehouse, one of Nicholas Hawksmoor's six London churches
  (built 1714–1730), in what was then a Thames-side maritime/dockworking parish in
  the East End — consistent with a working-class Blackmore household in 1841.
- **Brompton Cemetery context**: opened 1840, it became one of London's "Magnificent
  Seven" Victorian cemeteries and is now managed by The Royal Parks; its burial
  registers to 1997 are held at The National Archives, Kew, and can be consulted
  there free of charge, or via Ancestry's "Brompton, London, England, Cemetery
  Registers, 1840-2012" collection and Deceased Online. This gives a concrete,
  named route to pull William's actual burial register entry (plot number, exact
  age, officiant) that was not pursued further here due to access limits.
- **No independent corroboration was found** for either spouse via open web search:
  no findagrave memorial, no public FamilySearch "ancestors" tree page, no WikiTree
  profile, and no free-to-view census/GRO transcription turned up a matching William
  Blackmore (b. c.1839 Middlesex, d. 6 Mar 1880) or Eliza Wood (b. c.1835, Somerset,
  d. 1891) despite numerous targeted searches (by name, by cemetery, by parish, by
  spouse pairing, and by children's names). The couple appears not to be otherwise
  documented outside the paid subscription databases (Ancestry, FindMyPast,
  FreeBMD/FreeCEN's form-only search interfaces) that this research pass could not
  query directly.
- **"St Mark, London" (1871 residence) could not be identified to a specific parish.**
  Victorian London had several St Mark's parishes (e.g., St Mark's Kennington, St
  Mark's Marylebone/Hamilton Terrace, St Mark's Whitechapel, St Mark's Notting Hill),
  and the tree gives no district or borough qualifier to disambiguate. This should be
  resolved from the original 1871 census RG10 piece/folio reference if the tree
  retains it, or by checking the 1871 civil registration district in which the
  household's children (if any were born 1861–1871) were registered.

## Open questions

1. Was Eliza Wood actually born in Wellington, Somerset (correcting "Willington"), and
   does a baptism record there in c.1835 name her parents? This would resolve the
   Thomas Wright / surname-Wood discrepancy (§4 above).
2. Is the 20 Jan 1848 London burial event a genuine record for a different Eliza Wood
   that was mis-attached to this profile, and if so, can it be traced back to its
   correct subject and detached?
3. Which specific "St Mark" parish hosted the family in 1871, and does the underlying
   census image name William's occupation and the children's ages/birthplaces?
4. What was William Blackmore's occupation? None is recorded in the tree extract;
   the 1861 and 1871 census images (not accessed here) would show this along with the
   couple's exact street address.
5. Where and when were the four children born/baptized, and did any die in infancy
   (a common enough occurrence that it could explain apparent gaps between 1861 and
   1871)? Not covered by the tree extract supplied for this pass.
6. Does a Brompton Cemetery register entry (via The National Archives, Ancestry's
   "Brompton, London, England, Cemetery Registers, 1840-2012," or Deceased Online)
   exist for William Blackmore's 6 March 1880 burial, and if so, what plot/grave
   number, and is Eliza (d. 1891) buried in the same plot?
7. Given the pattern of profile conflation documented elsewhere in this tree (see
   `larkin-blackmore.md`), do the Devon baptism sources attached to William and Eliza
   actually belong to unrelated Devon Blackmore/Wood individuals rather than to this
   Middlesex/London couple?
