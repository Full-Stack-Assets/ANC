# The Murray Line — Lineage Survey (Generations 7–12)

## Overview
This is a Scottish-to-Canadian surname line, not a colonial New England/New Jersey line: the documented emigrant ancestor is **John Murray Jr** (b. 1761, Canonbie parish, Eskdale, Dumfriesshire, Scotland – d. 1810, Tabusintac, Northumberland Co., New Brunswick), whose father **John Murray "Sr"/UEL** (1735–1805) was a Loyalist refugee who resettled from the American colonies to North Bedeque, Prince Edward Island, after the Revolution. The paper trail is genuinely solid down to about 1704 (William Murray of Canonbie) thanks to well-sourced WikiTree/NBGS/PARO profiles built on land-grant, census, and church records, but generations 11–12 (John Murray of "Ewes," b. 1677, and Alexr. Murray of Alves, b. 1651) are almost certainly the result of a duplicate-record conflation with an unrelated, better-documented Murray gentry family (the Murrays of Unthank/Philiphaugh, Selkirkshire), grafted on because of matching vital-year coincidences. One spouse name in the tree (Honoria "Beatty") also appears to be a mix-up with the correct, well-documented Honoria Hierlihy.

## Verified facts
- **John Murray (gen. 7, b. 1791–d. c. 1821)** was born 21 Aug 1791 in Bedeque, Prince Co., PEI, and died c. 1821 at Tabusintac, Alnwick Parish, Northumberland Co., New Brunswick. Source: WikiTree profile Murray-2863, citing New Brunswick Genealogical Society files (MC1/Murray #2; Thomas A. Murray, "Murrays of Tabusintac"; Hazel Murray, "Descendants of John Murray, United Empire Loyalist") and the 1851 Census of New Brunswick (Northumberland/Alnwick, RG31, item 124631). https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Murray-2863
- He married **Honoria Hierlihy** (not "Honoria Beatty" — see Corrections below), daughter of Philip Hierlihy and Charlotte (Taylor) Hierlihy, about 1813 at Tabusintac. Source: same WikiTree profile, citing NBGS Hierlihy file, "Loyalist Lineages of Canada 1783–1983," and PEI/NB census records.
- Their children per WikiTree, sourced to PEI baptismal/marriage registers and the 1851/1861/1881 censuses: James (b. c.1814), Ann Jane (m. John Blake), Mary (b. 1817, m. James Robertson of Birch Hill, PEI), Charlotte Sophia (b. c.1819, m. John Jenkins in PEI, FamilySearch record ark:/61903/1:1:QVBJ-KF9K), and possibly a son John (b. c.1821). This matches the tree's child list closely (tree also adds "Michael Murray," not found in the WikiTree source).
- After John's death (c. 1821), Honoria (Hierlihy) Murray remarried in 1822 to **Ephraim Gay**; the 1851 and 1861 New Brunswick censuses (RG31) show her as Mrs. Gay / widow Gay. Source: WikiTree Murray-2863, citations 8–9.
- **John Murray Jr (gen. 8, b. 1761–d. 1810)** was baptized 5 Apr 1761 at Over Mumbiehirst (Upper Mumbie), Canonbie Parish, Eskdale, Dumfriesshire, Scotland, and died 27 Aug 1810 at Tabusintac. He received a 290-acre New Brunswick land grant at Tabusintac, dated 6 Dec 1810, Volume E, Grant #568 (38 names on the grant) — Provincial Archives of New Brunswick, Index to NB Land Grants 1784–1997 (RS686), microfilm F16304. This is the immigrant/settlement-generation record for the line's move to Canada.
- **John Murray "Sr"/UEL (gen. 9, b. 1735–d. 1805)** is documented on WikiTree as "John Murray UEL," born 31 Aug 1735 (place given there as Over Mumbiehurst, Canonbie Parish, Eskdale — likely copied from his son's baptismal entry rather than his own), died c. 1805. He is identified as a United Empire Loyalist who resettled at North Bedeque, Dunk River, Prince Edward Island (per Geni.com's "John Murray, Sr" profile, which places his pre-war residence near Longtown, Cumberland, on the Scottish border, and records confiscation of his property in Tryon County, New York, followed by removal to PEI with the Schurman party of Loyalist settlers in 1784). He is commemorated on the Bedeque Loyalist Monument, PEI. His Loyalist muster-roll entry lists a wife and three children (William, Mary, Ellen); additional children (including John Jr, who settled at Tabusintac) are attested by the NBGS Murray family files. Sources: https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Murray-2863 (ancestor chain); https://www.geni.com/people/John-Murray-Sr/6000000046013669340.
- **William Murray (gen. 10, b. 1704–d. 1789)** and wife **Christian (Brown) Murray (d. c.1745)**: WikiTree's ancestor chain for this family gives William Murray, b. 28 May 1704, Canonbie, Dumfriesshire, d. c. 1789, matching the tree's data exactly (place name "Gleingierhead" in the tree is very likely a phonetic/OCR rendering of a farm-name in Canonbie parish, e.g. "Glenzierhead"). His wife is recorded there as "Christian Murray," b. 1 Jan 1708, d. c. 1745, Canonbie — consistent with the tree's "Christian Brown."
- **John Murray (gen. 11, b. c.1675–d. ?) and "Isobel Unknown" (b. c.1679–)**: this is as far back as the sourced WikiTree ancestor chain for this family goes. WikiTree explicitly marks the next generation up as unknown ("[Murray g-g-grandfather?]" / blank), i.e. the documented Eskdale Murray line for this family **ends at generation 11**, with the wife's surname itself unconfirmed (not "Grant").

## Corrections to the tree
- **Spouse of gen. 7 John Murray: "Honoria Beatty" → Honoria Hierlihy.** Every primary-adjacent source found (NBGS Hierlihy file, PEI marriage register FamilySearch image, 1851/1861 NB censuses, PARO baptismal record for grandson James Robertson) names her as Honoria Hierlihy, daughter of Philip Hierlihy and Charlotte Howe Taylor, later Mrs. Ephraim Gay. No source found supports "Beatty." This looks like a conflation with the separate, well-documented Beattie/Beatty family of the same Tabusintac/Miramichi district — an easy mix-up given how many unrelated families intermarried in that small settlement. Evidence: WikiTree Murray-2863, citations 1–3, 8–9.
- **Gen. 11 parentage "Sir James Murray Philiphaugh" → unsupported; almost certainly a duplicate-record artifact.** The tree's John Murray (b. 1 Feb 1677 Ewes, Dumfries – d. 27 Feb 1728) is very likely being conflated with a different, well-documented person of *identical birth and death dates* and a similar name/place tag: "John Murray of Unthank" (b. 1 Feb 1677, Unthank, Ewes, Dumfriesshire – d. 27 Feb 1728, Ancrum, Roxburghshire; WikiTree Murray-5878; Geni "John Murray, II of Unthank"). That documented person's father was John Murray of Bowhill, Selkirkshire (not "Sir James Murray of Philiphaugh"), his great-grandfather was James Murray of Philipaugh (d. bef. 1648) — two generations further back — and his wife was **Anne Bennet** (m. 1712, Ancrum), not Isobel Grant; his children were James, John, and Elizabeth (m. Inman), not William. In other words, the "Philiphaugh" claim in the tree appears to be a mis-transcription of this unrelated gentry line's great-grandfather onto the Eskdale farming family that is our actual ancestor, likely because a family-history compiler matched the two "John Murray, 1677–1728" records by date alone. Treat the "Sir James Murray Philiphaugh" parentage as **legend, no support** — it is not corroborated by any source for our John Murray of Eskdale, whose real WikiTree-documented parentage is simply unknown.
- **Gen. 12 "Alexr. Murray" of Alves, Moray as father of Isobel Grant: unsupported and genealogically implausible as recorded.** If Isobel's surname really was Grant (itself unconfirmed — WikiTree's parallel record calls her "Isobel Unknown"), her father would be expected to carry the surname Grant, not Murray. No source was found connecting an Alexander Murray of Alves (Moray, on the Moray Firth — over 100 miles from Eskdale/Dumfriesshire) to an Isobel Grant who married into the Eskdale Murray family. This entry looks like a stray/duplicate record pulled in from an unrelated Murray line and mis-attached as a parent.

## New findings (not in the tree)
- John Murray Jr's 1810 New Brunswick land grant (290 acres, Tabusintac, Grant #568, one of 38 grantees on that parcel) — Provincial Archives of New Brunswick, RS686, microfilm F16304.
- John Murray "Sr" (gen. 9) is specifically identified as a **United Empire Loyalist** whose PEI settlement is commemorated on the Bedeque Loyalist Monument; per Geni, his pre-Revolution home was near Longtown on the Scottish/English border and he held property in Tryon County, New York, confiscated after the Revolution, before removing to PEI in 1784 with the Schurman party.
- Honoria Hierlihy's mother, **Charlotte (Taylor) Hierlihy** (c. 1755–1841), is a well-known early Miramichi-area historical figure (subject of the local micro-site charlottetaylor.ca and a WikiTree profile, Taylor-13772); this gives the Murray line a documented tie into a locally notable pioneer family, worth flagging for future research even though Charlotte Taylor herself is outside the direct Murray surname line.
- Tree's gen. 7 John Murray lists a son "Michael Murray" not found in the WikiTree children list — possibly a genuine additional child from a different source, or a duplicate/confusion; not verified either way.

## Open questions
- What is the actual parentage of John Murray (gen. 11, b. 1677, Ewes) once the "Philiphaugh" conflation is set aside? WikiTree has no answer; Scottish parish registers for Ewes/Canonbie (via ScotlandsPeople) would be the next step.
- Is "Isobel Grant" the correct wife's maiden name at all, given WikiTree independently records her only as "Isobel Unknown"?
- Where does the tree's "Alexr. Murray of Alves, Moray, b. 1651" data actually originate, and which (if any) real family does it belong to? It does not fit geographically or genealogically as father of Isobel (Grant/Unknown).
- Full original source for John Murray Sr's Scottish birthplace vs. the Geni account's "Longtown, Cumberland" origin — these are adjacent but not identical, and neither account cites an original Scottish parish register entry for his birth.
- The place name "Gleingierhead" (tree) vs. likely "Glenzierhead"/similar Canonbie farm name — worth confirming against Scottish OS maps or parish records.
