# The Williams Line (Line 7) — Lineage Survey, Generations 7–12

## Overview

This tree extract labeled "Williams line" is not one continuous chain but at least **three, possibly four, unconnected Williams families** thrown together under the same surname and generation range (8–12; no generation‑7 or generation‑11 person is actually present in the file). One fragment runs Shropshire, England → Gloucester County, New Jersey (Quaker-era colonial settlers, ending at Rachel Williams m. Cheesman). A second, independently verifiable fragment is a Loyalist family of Westchester County, NY, that resettled in Digby County, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island after the Revolution (Capt. Frederick Williams). A third fragment (Owen Williams of Anglesey, Wales) has no corroborating record online and looks like an unrelated insertion. A fourth entry, Edith N. Williams, is really the *entry point of the Pyne/Pine family* (a Williams daughter marrying out) and appears to carry a **duplicated birth record copied from her husband**. None of the generation-11 "bridge" ancestors named in the data (Thomas Williams Jr, John Williams*, Captain James Pine/Pyne) exist as their own person records in this file — they are named only as parents/children of the generation-10/12 people, so the file itself has structural gaps.

## Verified facts

- Thomas Williams Sr. of Shropshire, England (b. Nov 1585 – bur. 18 Apr 1623) is documented in the *Shropshire, England, Extracted Church of England Parish Records, 1538–1812* collection, as the tree itself already flags ("sourced"). This is the only person in the whole file with an underlying period record cited.
- Rachel Williams (b. 1750, Gloucester Co., NJ) married **Richard D. Cheesman/Cheeseman** on **20 November 1768** in Gloucester County, New Jersey. Richard (1749–1799) was buried in Blackwood Presbyterian Cemetery, Blackwood, NJ, and was the son of Peter C. Cheesman (1704–1788) and Anna (Ellis) Cheesman. — [Find a Grave memorial #103849860](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/103849860/richard-d-cheesman); corroborating snippet via genealogical indexes.
- Their children included **Peter T. Cheesman (1771–1856)** — matching the tree's "Peter Thomas Cheeseman Major" — plus Jacob S., Bathsheba, Thomas Williams Cheesman (1782–1834), Richard William, and Benjamin S. Cheesman. The middle name "Thomas **Williams** Cheesman" is itself circumstantial confirmation the mother's maiden surname was Williams.
- **Stephen Williams married Rachel Vincent**, and Stephen died 1743/4 — documented in the compiled genealogy *"The Vermilyea Family (Vermilya, Vermilye, Vermilyer): Descendants,"* VINCENT section: "Rachel Vermilyea² (Johannes¹)... married in 1694 Charles Vincent, Jr... Children: ... II. Rachel, married Stephen Williams who died 1743/4." This independently corroborates the tree's claim that Frederick Williams's parents were Stephen Williams and Rachel Vincent — though this source does **not** itself name their son as Frederick; that link is not independently sourced here.
- **Captain Frederick Williams** (b. 3 Feb 1741, West Farms, Westchester, NY) is a documented Loyalist: he "joined the British as soon as they arrived in New York in 1776," served as a captain in the **Westchester Refugees** regiment, and also did espionage work for the Crown. Rebels burned his house and seized his ferryboat, livestock, and two enslaved people and their four children. He reached Annapolis Royal, Nova Scotia, in summer 1783, later returning briefly to Westchester to bring out his family. — [UELAC Loyalist Trails 2014-01](https://uelac.ca/loyalist-trails/loyalist-trails-2014-01/) (search-result summary; page itself returned 403 on direct fetch).
- Digby County local history records that Loyalist families named "Turner and Williams... came after the Muster of June, 1784," consistent with a Williams family settling in Digby County in that wave. — *A Geography and History of the County of Digby, Nova Scotia* (archive.org).
- **Eleanor Williams** (1771–1841), daughter of Capt. Frederick Williams, married **John Eachorn/Acorn** (b. 1761, Waldoboro, Lincoln Co., Maine; d. 19 June 1857, Vernon River, Queens Co., PEI) in **September 1788** at Vernon River, Lot 50, Queens County, PEI (bride 17, groom 27); they had 13 children. Both are buried at the Pownal, Lot 49, PEI United Methodist Church Cemetery. Eleanor is explicitly identified as "daughter of Captain Frederick Williams, a retired officer of the British army who settled in Nova Scotia." — [Geni: John Eachorn Achorn Acorn (1761–1857)](https://www.geni.com/people/John-Acorn/390392279380004478).
- **James Pyne (Pine), Sr.** was baptized **4 June 1608** in **Woodbury, near Exeter, Devonshire, England**, and married **Edith Williams** at **St. Mary Magdalen, Taunton**, in **September 1630**. His name disappears from Woodbury town records after 1640 and reappears at the same time in Stamford, Connecticut; he later moved to Hempstead, Long Island, and died 13 June 1685. — [Geni: James Pine (Pyne), Sr. (1608–1685)](https://www.geni.com/people/James-Pine-Sr/4768007432740080179); corroborated by a second independent snippet citing the same Woodbury/Taunton facts.
- The Pine/Pyne family of Hempstead is treated in a full published compiled genealogy: "James Pyne (Pine) of Hempstead, Long Island, and Some of His Descendants," *New York Genealogical & Biographical Record*, vol. 43 (no. 1) — confirms the family's Hempstead residence from at least 1657 and traces five further generations, though the article's introductory metadata alone was accessible, not its full text.

## Corrections to the tree

- **Edith N. Williams's birth data is almost certainly a duplication of her husband's baptismal record.** The tree lists Edith N. Williams born "4 Jun 1608 @ Exeter, Devon, England." Independent sources agree that it was **James Pyne** (her husband) who was baptized 4 June 1608 in Woodbury, near Exeter, Devon — not Edith. There is no record found anywhere of Edith's own birth date/place; the tree appears to have copied the husband's vital record onto the wife's profile. **Correction: Edith N. Williams's birth date/place should be treated as unknown; the "4 Jun 1608 Exeter, Devon" data point belongs to James Pyne, not Edith.**
- The tree tags the *child* of Edith N. Williams as "**Captain James Pine\Pyne (Immigrant)**" — i.e., it treats a son as the immigrant. Published genealogy instead points to **James Pyne Sr. himself (b. 1608)** — Edith's husband, not her child — as the one who crossed from Woodbury, Devon to Stamford, Connecticut circa 1640, later removing to Hempstead, Long Island. If the tree's "Captain James Pine" is meant to be this same immigrant generation, the parent/child relationship (listing him as Edith's *son* rather than her *husband*, or conflating him with a later "Captain" descendant who held public office in Hempstead per the Hudson-Mohawk genealogy) needs reconciliation — this looks like a duplicate-record/generation-shift artifact typical of the unmerged duplicates flagged for this tree.

## New findings

- Confirmed marriage date for Rachel Williams and Richard D. Cheesman: 20 November 1768 (not in the tree, which lists no marriage date).
- Richard Cheesman's parentage (Peter C. Cheesman & Anna/Hannah Ellis) and burial location (Blackwood Presbyterian Cemetery, Blackwood, NJ) — new to the tree.
- Full sibling set for Rachel Williams's children with Richard Cheesman (Jacob S., Peter T., Bathsheba, Thomas Williams, Richard William, Benjamin S.) — the tree names only one child, Peter Thomas Cheeseman Major.
- Corroborating detail on Capt. Frederick Williams's Loyalist service: specific regiment (Westchester Refugees), espionage activity, the burning of his house, and seizure of two enslaved people and their four children by rebel neighbors at "Frog's Neck" — none of this appears in the tree, which has only bare birth/death/burial facts.
- Marriage detail for Eleanor Williams and John Eachorn/Acorn: exact date (Sept. 1788), place (Vernon River, Lot 50, Queens Co., PEI), ages at marriage (17 and 27), and 13 children — the tree lists only the bare spouse fact with no date/place.
- James Pyne's baptismal parish (Woodbury, Devon) and marriage venue (St. Mary Magdalen, Taunton, Somerset — note this is a different county from Devon) — new detail not in the tree, which only carries Edith's mis-assigned birth data.

## Open questions

- **Is the generation-11 "Thomas Williams Jr" link real?** No independent record was found online connecting Thomas Williams Sr. of Shropshire (documented, d. 1623) to a son "Thomas Williams Jr" who in turn fathered Edward Williams Sr. (b. 1672 England, d. 1757 Gloucester Co., NJ). This entire Shropshire→New Jersey emigration story rests on the tree alone; treat as **unsupported/tree-only** pending a documented New Jersey Quaker source (e.g., meeting minutes, will, or deed) tying Edward Williams Sr. back to England.
- **Owen Williams of Pentraeth, Anglesey, Wales (1645–1695)** and his claimed parents (John Williams* / Ann Meredydd) and child (John Williams*) returned no corroborating record in any searched source (WikiTree, Geni, National Library of Wales finding aids, published Welsh pedigrees). This fragment reads as **legend/unsupported tree lore**, and its placement at "generation 12" alongside the unrelated Shropshire and Devon fragments suggests it may be a mis-filed or duplicate branch rather than a true ancestor of the same descendant line.
- **How (or whether) the Edith Williams/Pyne fragment connects to the rest of the "Williams line" is unclear.** Edith Williams has no parents listed in the tree (a dead end at generation 12), and her only tree-recorded descendant, "Captain James Pine\Pyne," does not reappear anywhere else in this file. This fragment may belong to a different research thread (the Pyne/Pine family) that got bundled into "line-williams" solely because Edith's maiden name was Williams.
- **Generation 7 is entirely absent** from the supplied data, and generation 11 exists only as unfilled name-references (Thomas Williams Jr, John Williams*, Captain James Pine/Pyne) with no person records of their own. Either the extract is incomplete relative to the full tree, or those generation-11/7 profiles were never created — worth checking the source tree software directly.
- No source was found for Edward Williams Sr.'s wife, Mary Walcott Ward, or for Thomas Williams Sr.'s wife, Jacosa Gillins — both remain undocumented beyond the tree.
- The immigrant ancestor for the Devon/Long Island fragment is best identified as **James Pyne Sr. (1608–1685)**, who emigrated from Woodbury, Devon to Stamford, Connecticut circa 1640 and later to Hempstead, Long Island — not the "Captain James Pine" the tree tags as immigrant, who is more likely a later (2nd- or 3rd-generation) Hempstead-born descendant who held local office. This should be corrected/reconciled in the tree.
