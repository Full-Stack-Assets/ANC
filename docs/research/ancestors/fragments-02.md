# Fragments Batch 02 — 40 Direct Ancestors, Generations 7–12

## Overview

This is a triage pass over 40 disconnected tree fragments (mostly single individuals or small couple-pairs, generations 7–12, roughly 1580–1812) spanning colonial New England, New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania, plus England, Scotland, Ireland, Germany, and Sweden. Most entries are thin, unsourced-but-internally-coherent stubs; ten were selected for targeted verification based on notability, unusual names, or existing source citations. Several well-documented colonial/Quaker lines checked out cleanly (Dodge/Woodbury of Beverly MA, Drewett/Stockdale/Albertson of Philadelphia, Fairlamb of Chester Co. PA, Eichorn/Achorn of Waldoboro ME), one likely factual error was caught (Robert Crosby of Holme Hall's death year), and one data-quality duplicate surfaced (two identical "Nathaniel Colburn" records). This batch also cross-validates against a person from Fragments Batch 01 (Lady Constance Brigham/Crosby), whose husband appears here as "Lord Robert Crosby."

## Verified facts

- **Mary (Sarah) Dodge** (b. 21 Apr 1675, Beverly, Essex Co., MA; d. 20 Nov 1763, Beverly) was the daughter of Edward Dodge (1647–1727) and Mary Haskell, and married Peter Woodbury (1666–1707) on 18 Nov 1692 in the Chebacco part of Ipswich. She is buried in the "Old Cemetery" off Conant Street, Beverly. All core facts (dates, parents, spouse) match the tree exactly. — [WikiTree Dodge-341](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Dodge-341); [Find a Grave #51668276](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/51668276/mary-woodbury)
- **Edward William Dodge Sr.** (1647–1727) is independently confirmed as Mary Dodge's father via the same WikiTree/Find a Grave records, matching the tree's birth/death years (1647/1727) for his primary event set. — same sources as above
- **Hannah Drewett (Stockdale/Albertson)** (b. 1 Nov 1669, Shadwell, England; d. 15 Sep 1736, Philadelphia) was the daughter of Quaker Morgan Drewett and Cassandra Thompson; emigrated to West Jersey in Aug 1677 aboard the ship *Kent* at about age 8. She married William Stockdale (received clearness 5 Oct 1689, Kennett Monthly Meeting), who died 1693 and was buried in Philadelphia; she then married William Albertson by ca. 1695, living near Camden, NJ, and transferred her Quaker meeting certificate from Abington to Philadelphia in 1729. All of this matches the tree's dates/spouses/parents closely. — [WikiTree Drewett-16](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Drewett-16); [Geni: Hannah Albertson (Druett) {Stockdale}](https://www.geni.com/people/Hannah-Albertson-Druett-Stockdale/6000000019786609818)
- **Morgan Drewett** (1629–1695) is independently confirmed as Hannah's father via the same sources, matching the tree's identification of him as a Stepney/London-born Quaker who died in Chester Co., PA (Chichester township is in the Philadelphia-area Quaker settlement zone consistent with the family's documented migration).
- **Mattheuss Eichorn I** (1664–1732), of Goebrichen (Neulingen), Enzkreis, Baden-Württemberg, Germany, matches a Geni record ("Mattheuss Von Eichorn") with identical birth/death years. — [Geni: Mattheuss Von Eichorn](https://www.geni.com/people/Mattheuss-Von-Eichorn/6000000004279605065)
- **Mary Magdalena Eichorn** (1712–1771) married Johann Georg Ried in Germany and emigrated with the wave of ca. 1,500 German (mostly Rhineland Palatine) settlers recruited by Samuel Waldo, arriving at Broad Bay (now Waldoboro), Lincoln Co., Maine — many aboard the ship *Lydia* in 1742 — matching the tree's dates and destination exactly. Sources note the German surname "Eichorn" ("fruit of the oak") was anglicized in America to "Achorn," which is directly consistent with this fragment's own "John Eacorn (Eichorn)" and "Margaret Catherine Reid" (from Ried) entries. — [Waldoboro German settlers, USGenWeb](http://files.usgwarchives.net/me/lincoln/waldoboro/settlers/german/sj5p140.txt); [History of the Town of Waldoboro, Maine (archive.org)](https://archive.org/stream/historyoftownofw00mill_0/historyoftownofw00mill_0_djvu.txt)
- **Nicholas Fairlamb** (b. 19 Nov 1679, Stockton-on-Tees, Durham; d. 19 Oct 1722, Chester/Middletown, Chester Co., PA) matches a compiled WikiTree/family-history record exactly on both dates. That source further identifies his wife as Katharine Crosby, daughter of Richard Crosby and Ellinor Done — which independently corroborates this fragment's own **Robert Doane** entry (whose listed daughter is "Elinor Done Crosby") and confirms the tree's otherwise-unlabeled spouse "Catharine Crosby" for Nicholas Fairlamb. He also served on the Chester County Assembly in 1712 and 1713. — [WikiTree Fairlamb genealogy](https://www.wikitree.com/genealogy/FAIRLAMB); [us.fairlambs.org family notes](http://www.us.fairlambs.org/generations.htm)

## Corrections to the tree

- **Lord Robert Crosby of Holme Hall** — tree gives death "Abt. 1649." Independent compiled sources (WikiTree, Geni, "Miner Descent") place his death considerably earlier: WikiTree dates him 1596–1636, and Miner Descent gives ca. 1640 or "before 1642." No source found supports 1649. **Correct value is likely ca. 1636–1642, not 1649** (evidence: [WikiTree Crosby-163](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Crosby-163); [Geni: Robert Crosby of Holme Hall](https://www.geni.com/people/Robert-Crosby-of-Holme-Hall/6000000006444252780); [Miner Descent](https://minerdescent.com/2010/06/29/robert-crosby/)). Additionally, no source calls him "Lord" — this appears to be the same unsupported-title embellishment already flagged in Fragments Batch 01 for his wife "Lady Constance Brigham" (the two records are for the same couple).
- **Two identical "Nathaniel Colburn\Colborne" records** (I182541959513 and I182541966374) carry byte-for-byte identical birth/death/spouse/child data — this is a tree data-quality duplicate, not two separate people, and should be merged.
- **Edward William Dodge Sr.** — the tree itself carries two contradictory death-year events for the same person (12 Feb 1727 at Wenham vs. 1710 in Massachusetts, unsourced). External sources (WikiTree/Find a Grave, via his daughter Mary Dodge's record) support **1727**, consistent with his role as father of a woman who married in 1692 and lived to 1763.
- **Mary Ann Eliston** — the tree's own events conflict internally: a baptism dated 16 Sep 1759 (flagged `val=None`) versus a stated birth year of ~1761, which is chronologically impossible (baptism cannot precede birth by two years... more likely the birth year should read ~1759, or the baptism date is mis-transcribed). Not independently resolved online.

## New findings

- **Hannah Drewett** emigrated as a child (age ~8) aboard the ship *Kent*, arriving in West Jersey in August 1677 — a specific immigration detail not present in the tree's own events.
- **Nicholas Fairlamb's** wife Catharine Crosby's parentage (Richard Crosby & Ellinor Done) was not given in the tree but was found online, and ties this fragment's Fairlamb line to its separate Doane/Crosby entries (Robert Doane → daughter Elinor Done Crosby).
- **Eichorn → Achorn**: the German surname was anglicized to "Achorn" among the Waldoboro Palatine community — explains why this same batch carries both "Eichorn" (German-era) and "Eacorn"/"Acorn" (American-era) spellings for what is genealogically one surname line.
- **John Florence 'Fleury' Donovan**: an aggregated genealogy record (WikiTree-sourced compilation) reports his wife as Margaret O'Reilley (b. ca. 1790, Co. Monaghan), an immigration date of June 1842 to New Brunswick, and children including a son Florence (b. ca. 1811, Co. Cork; d. ca. 1908, Irishtown NB) and daughter Norah (b. ca. 1815) — none of which appear in the tree, which lists only son "Patrick Donovan." **Flagged as inferred/unverified**: the search result aggregates multiple genealogy-site trees and the birth year for son "Florence" (1811) would make him older than his stated father's marriage-age norms if John Florence was born 1788, which is plausible but not confirmed against a primary record, and the match to *this specific* Donovan couple (vs. a different Cork Donovan family in the same NB Irish settlement wave) was not independently confirmed.
- **Sgt. Philip Crist's** tree-recorded military event (14–16 Oct 1778, New York) falls within the documented window of Palatine-German-militia engagements around German Flatts/Herkimer during the Revolutionary War (there is a distinct, separately documented "Attack on German Flatts" in 1778, on top of the better-known 1777 Battle of Oriskany) — the date is regionally and historically plausible, though no muster roll or pension record naming "Philip Crist" specifically was located in this pass.

## Open questions

- Was **Nancy Davis** (b. 1780, Ireland; m. Robert McClelland) the same Nancy Davis found in Pennsylvania death-certificate indexes, and does a specific McClelland-family genealogy (e.g., WikiTree McClelland pages found in search) name her directly? Not confirmed in this pass.
- What is the correct death year for **Lord Robert Crosby of Holme Hall** — sources disagree between ca. 1636 (WikiTree) and "before 1642" (Miner Descent); neither matches the tree's 1649, but the two external sources don't agree with each other either.
- Which of the two **Nathaniel Colburn\Colborne** records (I182541959513 vs. I182541966374) should be retained/merged in the working tree?
- Is the **John Florence Donovan** aggregator record (wife Margaret O'Reilley, children Florence & Norah) actually the same person as this tree's John Florence Donovan (children include Patrick Donovan), or a different Cork-immigrant Donovan family in the same New Brunswick Irish settlement wave? Needs a primary record (parish register, NB vital stats, or a specific findagrave memorial) to confirm.
- No further primary-source verification was attempted for the ~28 "plausible" unresearched rows (Scottish, Swedish, German, and colonial-American stubs) — this was a triage pass by design; any of them could reward a deeper look in a future session, particularly the Scottish Dalgleish/Dougleish and Fife/Stirling cluster, which has enough unusual names and consistent geography to likely be traceable via ScotlandsPeople parish registers.
