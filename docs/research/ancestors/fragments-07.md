# Fragments 07 — Batch Triage (40 people, generations 7–12)

## Overview

This is a triage pass over 40 surname-fragment ancestors (initials M–O) spanning
17th-century Sweden (Skaraborg/Bohuslän patronymic lines), colonial New
England (Nantucket Myrick/Newell/Coffin cluster), Ulster-to-Prince Edward
Island Presbyterian migration (Morrow), Cork-Irish-to-Nova Scotia Loyalist
settlement (Monahan/Hierlihy), and Scottish landed gentry (Murray of
Philiphaugh). All are deceased with no living descendants named in this batch,
so full web research was in scope for everyone. Ten people were selected for
targeted checks. The most notable finding is a probable **anachronism/name
conflation** in the Monahan–Hierlihy entry: the tree has Mary A. Monahan dying
in 1774 at "Town Point," Nova Scotia — a place that historically was not
settled until 1784, by the Loyalist corps of Lt. Col. **Timothy** Hierlihy
(not the tree's "Cornelius Hierlihy Lt. Col."). The well-documented Nantucket
Myrick/Coffin/Morrow families corroborate well against secondary sources with
several small date corrections and one solid new finding (a previously
unlisted mother, Jedidah Pinkham).

## Researched in depth

### Mary A. Monahan (I182625292210) and the "Cornelius Hierlihy Lt. Col." problem

The tree records Mary A. Monahan (b. 1 Feb 1724, Cork, Ireland; d. 1774,
"Town Point, Provence, Nova Scotia") as the wife of "Cornelius Hierlihy Lt.
Col.," with a son Philip Hierlihy. Web research surfaced a real, extensively
documented Loyalist officer of that surname: **Lt. Col. Timothy Hierlihy**
(1734–1797), born Ballyvourney/Mallow, Co. Cork, who raised Hierlihy's Corps
(2nd Battalion, Prince of Wales American Volunteers) during the American
Revolution and led his disbanded soldiers to found **Antigonish, Nova Scotia**,
landing at a place named **"Town Point"** on 12 May 1784 (Wikipedia "Timothy
Hierlihy"; FamilySearch L5ZB-ZSR; imagineantigonish.ca). Timothy Hierlihy's
documented wife was **Elizabeth Wetmore** (m. 10 May 1755, Middletown, CT),
not Mary Monahan.

A family-history site (charlottetaylor.ca, "The Hierlihy Years") states that
Timothy Hierlihy's own father is "said to have been **Cornelius Hierlihy**,"
and that it is uncertain whether this Cornelius and a "Guilielmus (William)
Hierlihy" (father of a documented Loyalist sergeant, Philip Hierlihy) were the
same man — i.e., even genealogists working this family are unsure whether
Timothy and (the real) Philip Hierlihy were brothers or cousins. That real
Philip Hierlihy served as a sergeant in the Prince of Wales American Regiment,
was listed among New Brunswick Loyalists, received a land grant at Miramichi,
and married Charlotte Blake there in 1787.

Putting this together: the tree's claim that a Nova-Scotia-based "Lt. Col.
Cornelius Hierlihy" married Mary Monahan and fathered a Philip Hierlihy does
not match any single documented person found in this pass. It reads instead
like a conflation of (a) Cornelius Hierlihy, an earlier-generation figure who
per the family's own historians almost certainly never held rank or served in
Nova Scotia, with (b) Timothy Hierlihy, the actual Lt. Col. who brought his
family/corps to "Town Point" — but ten years *after* Mary A. Monahan's
recorded 1774 death there. **This makes the tree's death place/date for Mary
A. Monahan anachronistic as recorded**: Town Point, Antigonish did not exist
as a settlement in 1774. Verdict: suspect / likely legend-grade until a
primary source (parish register, will, land grant) is found.

### William John Morrow (Morrogh) (I182197770013)

Confirmed via islandregister.com's "Descendants of John Morrow" (a compiled
PEI genealogy). It gives matching birth (c. 1762, County Armagh) and death (27
Jul 1841), and confirms his marriage to **Eunice Coffin** (b. 7 Jul 1766,
Nantucket, MA; d. 20 Dec 1805, East Point, PEI), daughter of Elisha Coffin and
Eunice Myrick — consistent with the tree's "spouse: Eunice Coffin m. ... @
Nantucket." Two discrepancies worth flagging:

- **Death place**: islandregister gives "Surveyors Inlet, PEI" rather than the
  tree's "North Lake, Kings, PEI." Both are in the same general East
  Point/Kings County area of PEI, so this may just be a more/less precise
  place name for the same homestead rather than a real conflict.
- **Mother's name**: islandregister does not name his mother "Mary Twaddle" as
  the tree does — it lists only an unnamed "Female Morrow (b. c.1735, d. 25
  Jan 1815)." Both are secondary/compiled sources, so this is flagged as an
  **open question** rather than a confirmed correction: either "Mary Twaddle"
  is a name found by other researchers that islandregister's compiler simply
  didn't have, or it's an unsupported addition somewhere in the tree's
  chain of custody.

### The Nantucket Myrick/Newell family (Isaac Myrick, Andrew Myrick, Eunice Myrick)

This is the best-corroborated cluster in the batch. FamilySearch ("Andrew
Myrick Sr, 1704–1777") and WikiTree (Myrick-6, Myrick-487) both carry profiles
matching the tree's Isaac Myrick → Andrew Myrick → Eunice Myrick chain,
with parents Isaac Myrick and Mary Newell for Andrew, confirmed.

Corrections/refinements found:
- **Andrew Myrick's birth**: secondary sources give birth as **6 Aug 1704**,
  with baptism on **13 Sep 1705** — the tree's "birth: 13 Sep 1705" appears to
  be his baptism date recorded as a birth date, and his birth year is 1704,
  not 1705.
- **Andrew Myrick's death**: secondary sources say **24 Feb 1777**; the tree
  says 25 Feb 1777 — a one-day discrepancy.
- **New finding**: Andrew Myrick's wife (and Eunice Myrick's mother, not
  listed anywhere in the tree) was **Jedidah Pinkham** (also spelled Jedida),
  married 23 Jan 1728 in Nantucket. Secondary sources list Eunice as one of
  Andrew and Jedidah's roughly 15 children, explicitly noting "Eunice
  (married Elisha Coffin)" — matching the tree's Eunice Myrick → Elisha
  Coffin Sr. marriage exactly.

### Sir James Murray Philiphaugh (I182381538436)

The well-documented historical figure "James Murray, Lord Philiphaugh" (a
Scottish judge, Lord Clerk Register, and political ally of the 2nd Duke of
Queensberry during the 1707 Union) was born **11 July 1655** at Eddleston,
Peeblesshire, and died **1 July 1708** at Inch, Wigtownshire (Wikipedia; the
Murray Clan Society; thepeerage.com) — not 1647–1712 as the tree records. His
son and heir was John Murray (d. 1753), which matches the tree's listed child
"John Murray," strengthening the identification as the same family line even
though the specific dates don't match. It's plausible the tree's dates belong
to a different generation (his father, "Sir John Murray of Philiphaugh," whose
own birth/death years were not found in this pass) rather than to this
specific Lord Philiphaugh — this is flagged as an open question rather than a
firm correction, since a ~10-year date mismatch alone isn't conclusive.

## Corrections to the tree

- Deborah Mott (I182625986572) and *Deborah Mott (I182198586989) → these are
  the **same person** entered twice (identical spouse "Richard Valentine,"
  identical child "Mary Margaret Valentine," identical death 1684 Elizabeth,
  NJ, birth years one apart: 1625 vs. 1624). Recommend merging into a single
  record.
- Mary A. Monahan (I182625292210) death place "Town Point, ... Nova Scotia"
  in 1774 → anachronistic; Town Point, Antigonish was not settled until 12 May
  1784, per Wikipedia "Timothy Hierlihy" and imagineantigonish.ca. Her spouse
  "Cornelius Hierlihy Lt. Col." does not match any documented Lt. Col.
  Hierlihy in Nova Scotia — the only documented Lt. Col. of that surname
  there is Timothy Hierlihy (1734–1797, m. Elizabeth Wetmore), while
  "Cornelius Hierlihy" is elsewhere identified (charlottetaylor.ca) only as
  Timothy's own probable father, an earlier, undocumented-in-NS generation.
- Andrew Myrick (I182381536245) birth "13 Sep 1705" → likely his **baptism**
  date; his birth is dated 6 Aug 1704 in secondary sources (FamilySearch
  LCJG-CB8; WikiTree Myrick-6).
- Andrew Myrick (I182381536245) death "25 Feb 1777" → secondary sources give
  **24 Feb 1777**.

## New findings (not in the tree)

- Andrew Myrick's wife / Eunice Myrick's mother: **Jedidah Pinkham** (m. 23
  Jan 1728, Nantucket, MA; d. 1789) — not present anywhere in the tree data
  for this batch.
- Eunice Coffin (William John Morrow's wife, one generation forward of this
  batch): full dates found — b. 7 Jul 1766, Nantucket, MA; d. 20 Dec 1805,
  East Point, PEI; daughter of Elisha Coffin and Eunice Myrick.
- William John Morrow (Morrogh) and Eunice Coffin had (per islandregister.com)
  at least eight children: Elisha, John III, James, Andrew, Thomas, Benjamin,
  William, and Abigail (d. 1798) — a fuller list than in the supplied tree
  fragment.
- The real historical "Philip Hierlihy" (Prince of Wales American Regiment
  sergeant, New Brunswick Loyalist, land grant at Miramichi, m. Charlotte
  Blake 1787) may or may not be the same "Philip Hierlihy" listed as Mary A.
  Monahan's son in the tree — his documented father is described elsewhere as
  "Guilielmus (William)," not explicitly "Cornelius."

## Open questions

- Is the tree's "Cornelius Hierlihy Lt. Col." actually meant to be Timothy
  Hierlihy (misnamed/mis-ranked), a distinct and otherwise-undocumented
  relative, or a fabrication? Was Mary A. Monahan really married into this
  family, and is Philip Hierlihy really her son?
- What is William John Morrow's mother's real name — "Mary Twaddle" (per the
  tree) or unnamed (per islandregister.com)? Is there a primary Ulster/PEI
  record that resolves this?
- Do the tree's dates for "Sir James Murray Philiphaugh" (1647–1712) belong to
  a different generation of the Murray-of-Philiphaugh family (e.g., his
  father Sir John Murray) rather than to the documented Lord Philiphaugh
  (1655–1708)?
- Which of the two divergent event clusters on "Jon Olsson\Olofsson"
  (I182728423938) is correct — is this actually two different Bohuslän men
  merged into one tree entry?
- No independent verification was attempted in this pass for the bulk of the
  Swedish patronymic cluster (Nilsdotter, Olsdotter, Olsson, Rånge lines
  beyond Magnus/Måns Månsson Rånge), Sarah Morgan, Julia Murphy, Margaret
  O'Reilley, or the various thin one-line entries (William Pattrick Monahan,
  Jane Hellen Mucklow, Thomas Muxloe, Philipp Helfrich Müller, Mercy
  Oakley-Fowler, Pehr Nilson) — these remain plausible-but-unverified.
- Two entries in the tree are marked "[sourced]" in their event lines
  (Anders Månsson, Andreas Olofsson Färdig) despite an empty `sources` array
  — worth checking whether the underlying source citations were dropped
  during data export.
