# The Groff Line — Generations 8–12 (New Jersey, c. 1630–1810)

## Overview
This is a five-generation patrilineal survey of the Groff surname line ending in Hannah Groff (b. 1758, Salem, NJ), who married into the well-documented Pedrick family of Pedricktown, Salem County. Every event in this line is flagged `[unsourced]` in the source tree and no source citations are attached to any of the five people; extensive web searching (WikiTree, Geni, FamilySearch, Find a Grave, Archive.org full text of the 1883 Cushing & Sheppard county history, and general search) turned up **no independent corroboration** for any of the four Groff men (Garrett/Garret Groff, Samuel Joel Groff, Benjamin C. Groff). The internal data also contains an arithmetic impossibility that strongly suggests a duplicate/merged record rather than a genuine extra generation. Bottom line: this line should be treated as **family lore, not documented genealogy**, beyond the bare fact that a Hannah Groff married a Jacob Pedrick Jr. of Salem County.

## Verified facts
- Hannah Groff's married surname line (Pedrick) is independently documented: Roger Pedrick (c. 1615 England – 1692), a Quaker, received a 1682 survey of land in Salem County that became Pedricktown, and the Pedrick descent runs Roger → John → Jacob → **Jacob Jr.** → John Pedrick — a line published in Hubert B. Shoemaker, *A Genealogical and Biographical Record of the Pedrick Family of New Jersey, 1675–1938* (Philadelphia, 1938), and cited in this repo's own `gen05-joseph-elwell-pedrick-mary-e-rankins.md` dossier. — [Shoemaker, *Pedrick Family of New Jersey* (Ancestry collection description)](https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/13733/); [WikiTree PEDRICK surname index](https://www.wikitree.com/genealogy/PEDRICK); repo file `docs/research/ancestors/gen05-joseph-elwell-pedrick-mary-e-rankins.md`
- That published Pedrick descent independently names a "Jacob Jr." who fathered a "John Pedrick" — matching this tree's claim that Jacob Pedrick Jr. and Hannah Groff had a son John Pedrick. This corroborates the *existence* of Jacob Pedrick Jr. and John Pedrick, though the published excerpt located does not itself name Jacob Jr.'s wife, so it does not independently confirm "Hannah Groff" as her name.
- Salem County, NJ was founded in 1675 as John Fenwick's Quaker colony ("Fenwick's Colony"), the historical/geographic setting consistent with all the birth/death places given for this line (Salem, Gloucester, Burlington counties). — [njfounders.org, "1665 – The Founding of Salem"](https://www.njfounders.org/1665-the-founding-of-salem); [Wikipedia, John Fenwick (Quaker)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fenwick_(Quaker))
- The full text of Thomas Cushing & Charles Sheppard, *History of the Counties of Gloucester, Salem, and Cumberland, New Jersey* (1883) — the standard 19th-century county history with biographical/genealogical sketches — contains **no mention whatsoever** of the Groff surname (checked via full-text search of the Archive.org digitization). This is negative evidence: a family prominent enough to be remembered locally would be expected to appear in a 700+ page county history that covers hundreds of area families. — [Full text, archive.org](https://archive.org/stream/historyofcountie01cush/historyofcountie01cush_djvu.txt)

## Corrections to the tree
No corrected values could be sourced — the tree provides no source to correct against — but the following internal defects should be flagged as likely **duplicate-record artifacts**, per your note that this tree has many unmerged duplicates:

- **"Garrett Groff" (gen. 9, b. 1718, d. 1831) is listed as the *child* of "Garret Groff" (gen. 10, b. 1715, d. 1797).** A father born in 1715 cannot have a son born in 1718 (a 3-year gap). This is not a plausible father/son relationship — it is far more likely that "Garrett Groff" and "Garret Groff" are **the same individual entered twice** (once from one source tree with dates 1715–1797, once from another with dates 1718–1831), subsequently merged into the database with an artificial parent→child link stitching the duplicate to itself as its own "father." Evidence → *tree structure is internally inconsistent*.
- **Garrett Groff's own claimed lifespan, 1718–1831 (113 years), is not credible** for any 18th-century American, let alone an unremarked one with no obituary, headstone, or record found. Combined with the point above, this figure most likely belongs to the *other* half of the duplicate pair, not to one real 113-year-old man.
- **Samuel Joel Groff's claimed lifespan, c. 1695–1799 (~104 years), is also extreme** — not impossible in isolation, but combined with total absence of sourcing (no will, no gravestone, no census, no county-history mention) it should be treated as unverified rather than corrected/accepted at face value.
- **The 65-year gap between Benjamin C. Groff's birth (1630) and his son Samuel Joel Groff's birth (~1695)** is unusually wide for a single father/son step (Benjamin would be ~65 at Samuel's birth). This is consistent with either (a) an elderly father/younger second wife, (b) an approximate/rounded birth year for Benjamin that is simply wrong, or (c) a missing intermediate generation that was never captured. No source was found to resolve which.
- No wrong-value → correct-value pair can be responsibly supplied for any birth/death date or parent/child link in this line — there is nothing to correct *to*. The correction here is procedural: **flag Garrett/Garret Groff gen. 9–10 for de-duplication review** rather than presenting them as two verified generations.

## New findings
- The Groff surname, when documented in colonial America, is overwhelmingly associated with the **Lancaster County, Pennsylvania Mennonite "Graeff/Groff" family** (Hans Graeff, immigrant c. 1717) — a completely different, well-published lineage. Samuel Joel Groff's claimed NJ birth year of c. 1695 predates that PA immigration by two decades, so if this Salem/Gloucester/Burlington NJ Groff family is real, it is **not** the same immigrant stock as the famous Lancaster Groffs, and no equivalent published genealogy for a distinct "South Jersey Groff family" could be located. — [Groff surname origin summary, geneanet.org](https://en.geneanet.org/surnames/GROFF); [Groff surname history, forebears.io](https://forebears.io/surnames/groff)
- Genealogical finding aids exist that *could* resolve this line with archival (non-digitized/non-indexed) research: the **Gilbert Cope Collection** and the **Nicholson Collection** (Gloucester County Historical Society, family group records ca. 1690–1900 for Quaker and other South Jersey families) are standard starting points for Burlington/Gloucester/Salem/Cumberland County families and were not searchable online in this session. — mentioned via [Gloucester County NJGenWeb](https://usgenwebsites.org/NJGloucester/)
- Salem County's own clerk holds original land records from 1695 and will/probate records that would predate and could test Samuel Joel Groff (d. "Oct 1, 1799," Gloucester Co.) and Garret Groff (d. "7 June 1797," Salem, precise-sounding date that, if genuine, implies an original source such as a gravestone or will was once consulted, even though none is cited in the tree). — [Salem County Genealogy, FamilySearch wiki](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Salem_County,_New_Jersey_Genealogy)

## Open questions
- Is Benjamin C. Groff (b. 1630, gen. 12) a genuine immigrant/early colonist, or an unsupported "brick wall filler" birth year with no attached record? No baptismal, immigration, or land record was found for him under any spelling (Groff, Graff, Groof, De Groff).
- Are "Garrett Groff" (gen. 9) and "Garret Groff" (gen. 10) truly two people, or — as the 3-year age gap strongly suggests — one person duplicated across two tree branches and then incorrectly linked as father and son? This should be checked against the underlying source database/merge log before this line is used for any further generation-counting.
- Was Samuel Joel Groff really the son of "Benjamin c Groff," or is "c" an abbreviation (e.g., "Benjamin C. Groff") that was misparsed as part of a middle name/generation marker in the original data entry?
- Does Hubert Shoemaker's 1938 Pedrick genealogy, which does independently document a "Jacob Jr." fathering "John Pedrick," actually name Jacob Jr.'s wife? The excerpt available in this session did not include that detail; consulting the full 1938 volume (Ancestry collection 13733, or a physical/library copy) would be the fastest way to either confirm or refute "Hannah Groff" as Jacob Jr.'s wife and thereby anchor at least the youngest end of this line.
- No death/burial record (Find a Grave, cemetery transcription, will index) was located for any of the four Groff men, despite Salem/Gloucester/Cumberland County having reasonably well-indexed Quaker meeting and probate records for this period — this absence is itself worth noting to future researchers rather than assuming records simply weren't checked.
