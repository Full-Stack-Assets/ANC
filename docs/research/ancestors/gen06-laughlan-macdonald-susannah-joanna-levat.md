# Laughlan Macdonald (1816–1902) & Susannah Joanna LeVatte (1823–1899)

> **Note on assignment mismatch:** the task header supplied to this research pass named "Nils Svensson & Bengta Persdotter" as the subject couple, but the actual item file (`A40.json`) and its `dossier_path` are for **Laughlan Macdonald and Susannah Joanna LeVatte**, generation-6 direct ancestors of the tree owner, of Main-à-Dieu, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia. This dossier follows the data file and `dossier_path` per instructions and covers the Macdonald/LeVatte couple. No research on any Svensson/Persdotter (Swedish) couple was performed here.

## Overview

Laughlan (Lauchlin) Macdonald was born 10 October 1816 in North Uist, Inverness-shire, Scotland, and emigrated (presumably as a child, with parents Neil MacDonald and Mary) to the Gaelic-Highland settlement belt of eastern Cape Breton, Nova Scotia, ultimately settling at the fishing village of Main-à-Dieu. He married Susannah Joanna LeVatte (b. 14 June 1823, Main-à-Dieu), daughter of John LeVatte and Catherine Martell of that same community — a family of apparent Jersey/Channel-Islands and Acadian-Martell descent typical of the mixed Scottish-Highland/Jersey-merchant/Acadian population of the Louisbourg–Main-à-Dieu coast. Susannah died 14 May 1899; Laughlan survived her, was enumerated as a widower in the 1901 Census of Canada living with an unmarried daughter, Victoria (b. 1860), and died 7 May 1902, reportedly buried at St. James Anglican Cemetery, Main-à-Dieu. Independent record survival for this couple online is thin — pre-1908 Nova Scotia deaths and this-era marriages are largely outside free indexes, and Find A Grave blocks automated access — but the 1901 census return was located and independently confirms the tree's birth date for Laughlan exactly.

## Verified facts

- **Laughlan Macdonald born 10 October 1816** — CONFIRMED independently via the 1901 Census of Canada, Cape Breton District #28, Main-à-Dieu subdistrict "U", household 57, page (Automated Genealogy transcription id 16712): "McDonald Lauchl[in]," male, Head, Widower, birth "Oct 10 / 1816," age 84. This is an exact match to the tree's (unsourced) birth date, now upgraded to documented.
- **Laughlan Macdonald was a widower as of 31 March 1901** — CONFIRMED, same census record ("W" for widowed) — consistent with wife Susannah's death 14 May 1899 as given in the tree.
- **Laughlan Macdonald was still resident at Main-à-Dieu, Cape Breton, in 1901**, one year before his recorded death there in May 1902 — CONFIRMED by the same census record.
- **Susannah Joanna LeVatte born 14 June 1823 at Main-à-Dieu, and her parents were John LeVatte and Catherine Martell** — the tree's parentage is corroborated (though not independently primary-sourced) by a compiled genealogy aggregation (MyHeritage "John Levatte" family summary) stating John LeVatte married Catharine Martell in 1822 and the couple had seven children, including a daughter "Susanna Joanna" who married a McDonald. Treat as compiled/secondary corroboration, not a primary-record confirmation.
- **St. James Anglican Cemetery, Main-à-Dieu, Nova Scotia is a real, catalogued burial ground** (Find A Grave cemetery ID 2555203, 81 memorials indexed), consistent with the tree's stated burial location for Laughlan. The individual memorial page could not be retrieved (see Open questions/access limits below), so his specific memorial there is unconfirmed, not contradicted.

## Corrections to the tree

None of the tree's core facts (names, birth/death years, parents, spousal relationship, burial location) were contradicted by anything found. One minor spelling note:

- **"Lauchlin"/"Lauchlan" vs. "Laughlan"** — the 1901 census transcribes his given name as "Lauchl" (abbreviated, almost certainly "Lauchlin" or "Lauchlan"), a common period variant spelling of "Laughlan." Not a correction, just a documented spelling variant worth recording alongside the tree's "Laughlan."

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **A previously undocumented daughter, Victoria Macdonald**, born 8 December 1860, appears in Laughlan's 1901 census household as an unmarried 40-year-old daughter living with her widowed father at Main-à-Dieu. She is not listed among the tree's recorded children (the tree shows only son John Macdonald, presumably because only the direct ancestral line was tracked). This is a genuine new lead for the couple's fuller family — Victoria would have been born when Susannah was about 37.
- **Household composition, 1901 census**: household #57, Main-à-Dieu subdistrict "U," Cape Breton District #28 — only two people, Laughlan (head, widower, 84) and Victoria (daughter, single, 40). No other children, boarders, or servants recorded, suggesting the couple's other children (including son John) had already married and left home by 1901.
- **Susannah's likely siblings**: a Geni.com aggregation (snippet-level, not independently verified by direct page fetch — Geni's page did not render for automated retrieval) lists Catherine Martell's children as including "Capt. Martin Levatte," Elizabeth Levatte, Catherine Elizabeth Levatte, Mary Ann Levatte, and Susanna Joanna Levatte — i.e., potential siblings of Susannah. This is unverified compiled/secondary information and should be treated as a lead only.
- **LeVatte family context**: the surname LeVatte (also spelled Levatte) is associated in Cape Breton with the Louisbourg/Main-à-Dieu area and appears repeatedly in the 1901 census for the adjoining Louisburg subdistrict (e.g., a household headed by Martin LeVatte, age 49, and wife Sarah, age 45, with several adult children) — plausibly an extended-family branch, though no direct link to Susannah's own siblings could be established from the records reviewed, and the age (b. ~1852) makes him too young to be a child of John LeVatte (who a compiled secondary source states died in 1838).
- **Regional/historical background**: Main-à-Dieu was originally a French fishing settlement dating to the 17th century, absorbed into British Cape Breton after the conquest of Acadia, and by the 19th century supported a mixed population of Highland Scots (arriving via the well-documented North Uist/Hebridean emigration streams to the Mira/Gabarus/Catalone/Main-à-Dieu district, mainly before 1850), Jersey-descended merchant/fishing families, and Acadian-descended families (e.g., Martell). Laughlan and Susannah's marriage is a textbook example of this Highland-Scot/Jersey-Acadian intermarriage pattern in that specific parish.

North Uist-to-Cape Breton emigration was heaviest in the 1820s–1830s to the Mira/Gabarus/Catalone/Main-à-Dieu district generally, consistent with (but not proof of) this family's origin story.
- **Victoria Macdonald** (b. 1860, found in the 1901 census) — her fate, and whether she is a known/missing sibling in the wider family tree, is worth checking against other records for this line.
