# The Blackett Line (Surname Line 4) — Generations 7–10

## Overview

This is a short surname line (only 4 generations, 7–10, are present in the tree data — not 7–12 as the assignment brief assumed) running from Catherine "Kate" Blackett (b. 1787, Prince Edward Island) back through three English-born Blacketts to a putative Benjamin Blackett of Southwark (b. abt. 1672). Web research locates a well-documented, if secondary, family history — "The Blacketts of North East England" (theblacketts.com) and the Island Register's PEI genealogical database — that independently reconstructs this exact family. That research confirms the immigrant crossing (a Thames-side London shipwright named **William Blackett**, not "John William Blackett," who settled Prince Edward Island in the late 1780s) but contradicts or fails to corroborate several details in the tree, including one blatant duplicate-record artifact (a death date copied from a daughter onto her father) and an unsupported "Ravensworth, Yorkshire" birthplace for the great-great-grandfather that does not fit the family's otherwise consistent London/Thames-side geography.

## Verified facts

- Catherine "Kate" Blackett married a Swallow husband and died 28 Dec 1867 at Grand River, Lot 56, Prince Edward Island — confirmed by the Island Register's compiled Swallow-family genealogy, which gives her death as "28 December 1867" at "Grand River Lot #56 Prince Edward Is." (islandregister.com/swallow.html, compiled/secondary).
- Catherine's parents were a man named **John William Blackett** and a wife whose name is transcribed as "Diana Applecross" in that same source — i.e., the tree's parent-names for Catherine (John William Blackett + Martha Applequest) are directionally correct even though the wife's given/surname spelling varies across compiled sources (islandregister.com/swallow.html).
- The immigrant ancestor of this Blackett family was **William Blackett**, baptised 1750 at St Mary's, Rotherhithe, Surrey, son of John Blackett and his wife Mary, grandson of a Benjamin Blackett who moved from Stepney to Southwark. William was a shipwright, married Anne Martha Applequist in 1777 at Bethnal Green, London, and emigrated to St John's Island (renamed Prince Edward Island in 1799) in the late 1780s, settling first at Launching, Lot 56, then Grand River/Annadale (theblacketts.com/node/34, compiled/secondary family history site).
- Catherine's actual husband was **Robert Swallow** (b. abt. 1790, Yorkshire, England; occupation "seaman/farmer"; d. 21 April 1865, Grand River, PEI), and their documented children include Robert Swallow Jr., **William Swallow**, Mary Anne Swallow, Marie Swallow, and John "Black John" Swallow — matching the two children (Robert Jr. and William) listed in the tree (islandregister.com/swallow.html).
- William Swallow (Catherine and Robert's son) himself later married a different, younger woman also named Catherine "Kate" Blackett (b. 29 Nov 1812, d. after 1901), daughter of Joseph William Blackett and Elizabeth Cheverie — a first-cousin-type marriage within the extended PEI Blackett–Swallow community (islandregister.com/swallow.html). This second Kate Blackett is not the gen-7 person in this tree, but her existence explains why the tree's spousal data is garbled (see Corrections below).
- Great-great-grandfather Benjamin Blackett is independently described by theblacketts.com as "native to Stepney" who "moved south across the River Thames to Southwark" — consistent with the tree's Southwark death location for Benjamin Blackett (theblacketts.com/node/34).
- A separate, unrelated Blackett line on the same research site ("Whickham and Lamesley Blacketts," theblacketts.com/node/32, covering Blacketts of Gateshead/County Durham) explicitly states "the connection between all of these branches has not been established" — i.e., even specialist researchers of this surname have not linked the London shipwright Blacketts to the North-East England gentry/baronetcial Blacketts, reinforcing that any gentry connection for this line is unproven.

## Corrections to the tree

- **"John William Blackett" (gen 8, this tree) → "William Blackett."** The compiled PEI family history (theblacketts.com/node/34) names the Rotherhithe-born (1750) shipwright who emigrated to PEI simply "William Blackett," son of John Blackett and Mary. A search independently turns up mention of a *different*, later-generation "John William Blackett" who died in 1895 aged 101 (i.e., born c. 1794) — one generation younger. The tree's "John William Blackett" appears to be a conflation of the immigrant (William, b. 1750) with this later-generation namesake. Evidence: theblacketts.com/node/34; corroborating WebSearch snippet on the 1895/aged-101 John William Blackett.
- **Death date "28 Dec 1867" for John William/William Blackett (gen 8) is a duplicate-record artifact, not his own.** That exact date — 28 December 1867, Prince Edward Island — is the sourced death date of his *daughter*, Catherine "Kate" Blackett Swallow (islandregister.com/swallow.html). A man baptised in 1750 dying in 1867 would be 117 years old, which is implausible on its face and confirms this is a copy-merge error rather than a real record. His true death date is not established by any source found in this research; it should be marked unknown rather than 1867.
- **Spouse name "Martha Applequest" is only partially right.** theblacketts.com gives "Anne Martha Applequist" (b. 1748, Rotherhithe; m. 1777, Bethnal Green); islandregister.com instead transcribes the same wife as "Diana Applecross." The tree's spelling is closer to the theblacketts.com version but is best treated as inferred/approximate, not firmly documented, given the conflicting transcriptions across the two compiled sources.
- **Catherine Kate Blackett's spouse "Robert William Swallow" is a merged, non-existent composite name.** Catherine's real husband was **Robert Swallow** (b. abt. 1790, Yorkshire; d. 21 Apr 1865, PEI). "William Swallow" was their *son* (and separately the husband of a different, younger Kate Blackett a generation later). The tree has fused father and son into one spousal name. Evidence: islandregister.com/swallow.html.
- **Catherine's birth year (1787) vs. sourced estimate (c. 1786).** Minor, within normal genealogical estimating error; not a substantive correction, but the tree's precision ("1787") is not independently sourced — islandregister.com gives only an approximate "born around 1786."
- **Gen 9 "John Blackett," born 6 Jun 1704 at "Ravensworth, Yorkshire," is unsupported and geographically inconsistent with the rest of the documented family.** Every other person and event in the confirmed compiled genealogy for this family sits on the Thames in London — Stepney, Southwark, Bethnal Green, Rotherhithe, Limehouse. No source found connects this family to Ravensworth (a village near Richmond, North Yorkshire) or to any Yorkshire Blackett gentry line. Two real, unrelated "prestige" Blackett lines exist that an amateur tree could accidentally graft onto a common London family: the Blackett Baronets of Newby Hall, Ripon, West Riding of Yorkshire (Sir Edward Blackett, 2nd Baronet, 1649–1718, whose daughter Diana Blackett happens to have been born in 1704 — the same year given for "John Blackett" here) and the Whickham/Lamesley Blacketts of Gateshead, County Durham. Neither is corroborated as connected to this family by any source located, and theblacketts.com's own researchers state the North-East branches' interconnections are themselves "not established." This detail should be treated as unsupported lore pending a primary-source (parish register) check.
- **Gen 9 spouse "Mary Lowson" and Gen 10 spouse "Mary Balchin" are not corroborated by any source found.** theblacketts.com names John Blackett's wife only as "Mary" (no surname given) and does not name Benjamin Blackett's wife at all. Both surnames in the tree may derive from an uncited Ancestry hint; treat as unsourced/legend until a parish record surfaces.

## New findings (facts not in the tree)

- The Blackett family's PEI settlement history: William Blackett arrived in the late 1780s at Launching, Lot 56, then moved across the Grand River to Annadale, where he bought or leased 100 acres (theblacketts.com/node/34).
- William Blackett's occupation was **shipwright**; in 1849 "William and John Blackett of Grand River" registered a boat named the *Swallow*, built at William Blackett's Grand River shipyard — a charming coincidence given the family's later intermarriage with the Swallow family (WebSearch snippet citing a PEI shipping register).
- Additional children of the immigrant William Blackett and Anne Martha Applequist, not in the tree: **Walter William Blackett** (1786 Limehouse–1875), who moved to Cape Breton, Nova Scotia and founded the settlement of Glace Bay; sons Daniel and Joseph, who reportedly settled in French Polynesia; a son John Blackett (who had a son William); and daughter **Diana Blackett** (1816–1906), who married Michael Taylor of Nova Scotia, with descendants settling in Massachusetts (theblacketts.com/node/34). These siblings of Catherine are worth adding to the tree as a family group even though they fall outside this direct line.
- Catherine's husband Robert Swallow's full documented sibling/child set (per islandregister.com) includes Robert Jr., William, **Mary Anne** (b. Sept 1829), **Marie** (b. c. 1830), and **John "Black John"** (b. 1836) — two more children than the tree currently lists.
- The tree's separately-recorded Swallow surname line (docs/research/ancestors/line-swallow.md, not modified here) should cross-reference this dossier for the Robert Swallow / Kate Blackett marriage detail.

## Open questions

- What is William Blackett's (the immigrant, b. 1750) actual death date and place? No source located gives one; PEI probate, land, or church-burial records for Grand River/Annadale, Lot 56, would be the next step.
- Is "John Blackett" (gen 9, tree) actually father of the immigrant William, and if so, what parish register or bishop's transcript documents his birth — and where? theblacketts.com's own account gives no date for him beyond "father of William, b. 1750," and does not use "Ravensworth, Yorkshire" anywhere.
- What is the source (if any) behind the "Ravensworth, Yorkshire," "Mary Lowson," and "Mary Balchin" details in the base tree? These look like they came from an uncited online Ancestry family tree; identifying that source (or a parish register) would let this line be pushed back with confidence, or definitively debunked as a graft from the Newby Hall or Gateshead Blackett gentry lines.
- Was Benjamin Blackett genuinely "of Stepney," and can a parish burial/probate record for Southwark, Surrey (Jan 1728, per the tree) be located to confirm his death date independent of theblacketts.com's undocumented compilation?
- Confirm which of "Anne Martha Applequist" (theblacketts.com) or "Diana Applecross" (islandregister.com) — or neither — is the correct name of William Blackett's wife, ideally via the 1777 Bethnal Green marriage register.
