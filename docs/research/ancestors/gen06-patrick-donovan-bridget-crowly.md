# Patrick Donovan (b. abt 1807) & Bridget Crowly (b. abt 1821)

Generation 6 direct ancestors; parents of Mary Donovan (gen 5), who married
Jeremiah Regan — see `gen05-jeremiah-regan-mary-donovan.md` for the next
generation down.

## Overview

Patrick Donovan was born about 1807 in Cork, Ireland, and Bridget Crowly was
born about 1821 in Stradbally, County Waterford, Ireland — a roughly
14-year age gap typical of the period. Bridget appears in the 1851 England
Census at Holborn St Andrew, Middlesex (London), before the couple's presumed
emigration; they married and raised at least one known daughter, Mary
Donovan, who was later baptized in the Cork/Rosscarbery area and eventually
settled with her own family in Massachusetts and New Hampshire. Both Patrick
and Bridget's own vital and immigration records sit in login-gated
collections (Ancestry, FamilySearch, the Emigrant Savings Bank ledgers) that
this pass could not access directly; no independent open-web record for
either individual was found, so this is a genuinely thin dossier beyond the
tree's existing citations and surrounding historical context.

No tree fact was found to be affirmatively wrong. One structural point is

## New findings (not in the tree)

- **Likely explanation for the Crowly/FitzGerald surname mismatch:** see
  above — Bridget Crowly was most plausibly born a FitzGerald (or possibly
  a name recorded to her mother Bridget Butler under a different surname
  convention) and carried "Crowly" from a first marriage or relationship
  that also produced son Patrick Crowly, prior to marrying Patrick Donovan
  and having Mary Donovan. This is an inference from the tree's own data,
  not a new external record, but it is not stated explicitly in the tree and
  is worth flagging for future research (e.g., look for a marriage record
  of "Bridget FitzGerald" or "Bridget Butler" to a Mr. Crowly in Waterford
  before her documented 1851 English residence).
- **Geographic/genealogical context for Stradbally, Co. Waterford:** the
  parish's Catholic registers (marriages 1805-1840, baptisms 1823-1833 and
  1857-1881, per NLI microfilm catalog descriptions) are held at the
  National Library of Ireland and would be the primary source for Bridget's
  actual baptismal entry if the tree's citation can be traced to a specific
  film/page.
- **Context for the 1851 English residence:** Holborn St Andrew was a dense,
  working-class central-London parish with a substantial Irish-born
  population by mid-century — a plausible stopover for Irish emigrants
  travelling via London before onward passage to North America, though no
  record of Bridget's household composition, occupation, or travel
  companions was located this pass.
- **Irish emigration context (Cork/Waterford to Massachusetts, 1840s-50s):**
  both counties were heavily affected by the Great Famine (1845-1852) and
  contributed large numbers of emigrants to Massachusetts mill and port
  towns in exactly this window, consistent with — but not proof of — a
  Donovan/Crowly emigration and Massachusetts marriage in this same period.

## Open questions

1. **Exact marriage date and Massachusetts town** for Patrick Donovan and
   Bridget Crowly — the tree cites *Massachusetts, Marriage Records,
   1840-1915* and *Massachusetts, Town and Vital Records, 1620-1988* but
   supplies no date/place; this would need direct (login) access to those
   collections.
2. **Both individuals' death dates and places** — neither is given in the
   tree, and none was found this pass. Given birth years of ~1807 and
   ~1821, plausible death windows are roughly 1850s-1890s (Patrick) and
   1870s-1910s (Bridget); Massachusetts/New England vital records, Find a
   Grave, and Catholic parish burial registers are the logical next
   sources to check once accessible.
3. **The Crowly/FitzGerald surname question above** — was Bridget previously
   married to a Crowly (or born a Crowly with Butler/FitzGerald as
   grandparents rather than parents, an alternative reading of the tree
   data)? Tracing the original Ancestry Family Tree or the underlying
   Ireland Catholic Parish Register / Massachusetts marriage citation would
   resolve this.
4. **The Emigrant Savings Bank Records, 1850-1883 citation on Patrick's
   profile** — this is a real and famous collection of individual depositor
   "Test Books" (recording an immigrant's parish, parents, ship, and
   emigration year) held at the New York Public Library; if Patrick Donovan
   has an actual depositor account, it would likely give his exact Cork
   parish/townland and possibly ship name and arrival year — a high-value
   record to pursue via NYPL or Ancestry/FamilySearch's digitized version.
5. **US census households (1850-1900)** — no census year, state, or
   household was found in the tree or via open search for either Patrick or
   Bridget; if they lived into the 1850-1880 US censuses this would be the
   richest available source for occupation, exact birthplace, and other
   children.
6. **Patrick Crowly, the other child** — no independent record found; a
   search of Massachusetts vital/church records for a Patrick Crowly of the
   right generation (sibling, on the mother's side, to Mary Donovan) might
   help confirm or refute the "earlier marriage" inference above.
