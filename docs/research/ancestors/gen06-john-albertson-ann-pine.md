# John Albertson (1771-1842) & Ann Pine (1775-1863)

Generation 6 direct ancestors (great-great-grandparents of Isaac J. Albertson, who married Mary Ann Shone and was the father of gen-4 ancestor Edwin S. Albertson). Research date: 2026-07-03.

*Note on scope: the task brief that accompanied this assignment named the subjects as "Josiah Franklin Albertson & Hannah Ann Buzine," but the actual tree-data file (`items/A31.json`) supplied for this dossier is for John Albertson and Ann Pine, a Camden/Gloucester County, NJ Quaker couple. This dossier follows the tree-data file, which the task instructions identify as authoritative, rather than the subject line. If Josiah Franklin Albertson & Hannah Ann Buzine were the intended subjects, that pair needs a separate research pass.*

## Overview

John Albertson and Ann Pine were members of an old South Jersey Quaker family, both born in Gloucester County, NJ (John in Camden/Gloucester Twp., Ann probably nearby), married in 1794, raised at least ten children, and are buried side by side at Haddonfield Friends Cemetery in Haddonfield, Camden County, NJ. John descended from William Albertson, an early Quaker settler and trustee of the Newton Meeting (established 1681) in Gloucester County; John's own father, Lieut. Josiah Albertson, served in the Gloucester County militia during the Revolutionary War and died in 1783. This deep dive confirms every tree-listed event via Find a Grave memorials that are cross-linked as a family group, and it identifies nine children beyond the single child ("Isaac J Albertson") carried in the tree, plus two prior generations of Albertsons with documented Revolutionary War service.

- **John's burial place, Haddonfield Friends Cemetery, Haddonfield, Camden Co., NJ** — same memorial; the tree's burial entry named only "Haddonfield, Camden County" with no cemetery, now specified.
- **Ann's birth, 16 Oct 1775, Gloucester County, NJ** — confirmed by Find a Grave memorial 206035193 ("Ann Pine Albertson"), matching the tree's (unsourced) birth entry exactly.
- **Ann's death, 2 Dec 1863, Camden County, NJ** — same memorial, matching the tree's (unsourced) death entry exactly.
- **Ann's burial, Haddonfield Friends Cemetery, Section B, plot 34-35** — same memorial; adds a specific plot location not in the tree.
- **Marriage of John Albertson and Ann Pine, 1794** — confirmed in general (year only) by both spouses' Find a Grave family-member listings ("m. 1794"); the exact day is disputed, see Open questions.
- **John's parents, Josiah Albertson and Eleanor ("Elinor"/"Elenor") Tomlinson** — confirmed by Find a Grave (father: "Lieut Josiah Albertson," memorial 42723316; mother: "Eleanor Tomlinson Albertson," memorial 190494364) and independently by the 1900 published county history transcription (below). Matches the tree's parent names.
- **Ann's parents, John Pine and Rachel** — confirmed by the Find a Grave biographical text on Ann's own memorial ("Ann Pine. Daughter of John & Rachel"), matching the tree's "John Pine" / "Rachel BURROUGH (PINE) (DAVIS)."
- **Child Isaac J Albertson = Isaac Pine Albertson (16 Dec 1797 - 14 Dec 1882)** — Find a Grave memorial 206035516; he died in Philadelphia, PA but was returned for burial at Haddonfield Friends Cemetery, NJ, alongside his parents. (This is the same Isaac J. Albertson who, per the companion gen-4 dossier for Edwin S. Albertson, married Mary Ann Shone and was the father of Edwin S. Albertson — confirming the tree's generational link forward.)
- **Family descent from William Albertson (d. 1709), immigrant Quaker settler** — per *The Daily Union History of Atlantic City and County, New Jersey* (John F. Hall, 1900), "Albertson Family" chapter (p. 369): William Albertson settled in Newton Township, Gloucester County, NJ, by 1682, was a trustee of the Newton Meeting (est. 1681) until 1708, held colonial office, and later removed to Byberry, Bucks County, PA, where he died in 1709. His son Josiah (John's great-grandfather) inherited land on Timber Creek, Gloucester Township, and married Ann Austin; their son Josiah (b. 1738, John's father) married first Eleanor Tomlinson.

## Corrections to the tree

- No hard corrections to the sourced tree facts — every dated/placed event in the tree (John's birth/death/burial, Ann's birth/death, the 1794 marriage) checks out against Find a Grave. The tree is materially **incomplete**, not wrong: it lists only one child (Isaac J Albertson) of at least ten documented children (see New findings).
- The tree gives the marriage date as **13 Oct 1794**; a period-informed Find a Grave biography gives **20 Nov 1794**. Neither source shown here cites a specific marriage record/meeting minute, so this is flagged as an unresolved conflict rather than a confirmed correction — see Open questions.

- **John's mother, Eleanor Tomlinson, died 1777** — the same year that two of John's infant siblings (Josiah, b. 1774, and Mary, b. 1776) also died, per the 1900 published genealogy below — suggestive of a shared cause (epidemic disease was common in the region in the 1770s), though no direct record of cause of death was found.
- **John had an elder sister, Hannah Albertson (b. 1760)**, who married first Samuel Glover and second Paul Toth, per the 1900 published genealogy (not corroborated on Find a Grave). John also had two brothers and a sister who died in early childhood: Isaac (1768-1774), Josiah (1774-1777), and Mary (1776-1777) — the latter two are the same "Mary Albertson 1776-1777" listed as John's sibling on his own Find a Grave page.
- **Full published pedigree back to the immigrant ancestor**, from *The Daily Union History of Atlantic City and County, New Jersey* (1900), "Albertson Family" chapter: William(1) Albertson (m. Hannah Druit) > Josiah (m. Ann Austin) > Josiah (b. 1738, Rev. War lieutenant, m. first Eleanor Tomlinson, second Judith Boggs) > **John (b. 1771, m. Ann Pine)**. The same chapter documents many collateral Albertson lines in Gloucester/Atlantic/Camden Counties (Blue Anchor, Smith's Landing/Absecon, Pennsgrove), several of Quaker background, useful for disambiguating same-named cousins in later research.
- **Find a Grave source citations attached to Ann Pine's memorial** point to several underlying record sets worth pulling directly (not accessed in this pass): *U.S., Quaker Meeting Records, 1681-1935*; *New Jersey, U.S., Deaths and Burials Index, 1798-1971*; *New Jersey, U.S., Marriage Records, 1670-1965*; *U.S., Family History Books*; and a Geneanet community-tree index.

## Open questions

- **Exact marriage date**: tree says 13 Oct 1794; a Find a Grave biographical note says 20 Nov 1794 New Jersey. The underlying *New Jersey, U.S., Marriage Records, 1670-1965* collection (cited on Ann's Find a Grave page) or a Haddonfield/Newton Monthly Meeting marriage record would resolve this.
- **Josiah Albertson's birth year, John's father**: Find a Grave and the first mention in the 1900 published genealogy agree on 1738, but a second internal reference in the same 1900 text (in the passage listing his children) says "Josiah, b. 1730" — almost certainly a transcription slip for 1738, but not independently re-verified against the original 1900 book page images.
- **Spouses' given names** for daughters Ann (b. 1805), Hannah (b. 1807), and the surnames-only-known husbands of Rachel (Atkinson), Eleanor (Rulon), Mary (Thackara), and Keturah (Gregory) are not established from the sources reviewed here.
- **No census records** for John Albertson's household were located in this pass (John would appear as head of household in the 1800/1810/1820/1830/1840 US Census, Gloucester/Camden County, NJ) — a natural next step to identify household composition and confirm the ten children's presence/order.
- **No obituary** for either John (1842) or Ann (1863) was located; Camden County newspapers of that era are thinly digitized.
- **Isaac Pine Albertson's move to Philadelphia** and his marriage/family are not documented in the sources reviewed here (no spouse listed on his Find a Grave memorial), despite his being the tree's sole recorded child and the ancestor of the next generation (per the companion Edwin S. Albertson dossier, his son Isaac J. Albertson married Mary Ann Shone) — worth a dedicated look at Philadelphia city directories and PA death indexes.
- **Sibling Hannah Albertson (b. 1760)**, mentioned only in the 1900 published genealogy, has no located Find a Grave memorial or independent corroboration.
